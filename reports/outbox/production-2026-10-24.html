1. PODCAST EPISODE PACK: "Four Things That Were Missing"

18-22 minutes. No fresh Reaction Fuel for the THIRTY-FOURTH day, and this episode is built so that does not matter.

WHY THIS EXISTS AT ALL, stated plainly because it is the whole pitch: this operation has refused three or four figures per production run for weeks and has never once shown an audience the method. A format built on the refusal turns an internal discipline into content. It also solves the thin-hook problem structurally: it needs no in-window news, only a claim, and there is always a claim.

Cold open, word for word (about 80 seconds)

"Here are four questions. They are not mine. I took them from a paragraph someone wrote about a viral biotech claim, and the paragraph was better reporting than most of what gets written about this industry.

Which laboratory did the work. Where are the results. Is there an assay certificate. Is there an independently reviewed paper.

Four questions. You can ask them of anything. And the useful thing about them is that they do not require you to understand the science. They require the person making the claim to have done ordinary paperwork.

So this is a new thing we are going to do on this show. Someone makes a claim about biology, and we score it out of four. Not true or false. Nobody here can adjudicate true or false from a desk. Just: how much of the ordinary paperwork exists.

I am going to do three of them today. Two companies, and then us.

And I should tell you the result of the first one before we start, because it is funny and it is also the reason the format is worth having. The first claim I tried to score, I could not find well enough to score it. I could not get to the company's own words. A claim you cannot locate scores zero out of four, and that is not a technicality. That is the finding."

SEGMENT 1: Where the four questions came from (about 4 minutes)

THE MATERIAL IS ON FILE AND THREE MONTHS OLD, AND THE EPISODE SAYS SO IN ITS FIRST SENTENCE.

Verbatim quote, public statement, quoted exactly or not at all: "I just cloned myself… as a newborn," and, of the result, that "baby Bryan" currently lives in a petri dish. He also wrote: "With this clone, I can become my own blood boy, test therapies on the clone, grow organs for transplantation, develop new treatments and inject young cells." [^1]

Date: July 2026. THREE MONTHS OLD. Stated on air, not in a footnote.

STANCE, AND IT IS THE HOUSE RULE RESTATED BECAUSE THIS ITEM KEEPS RETURNING: punch at the word, never at the man. The word is "cloned". He has an incurable autoimmune diagnosis reported in 2026 and that is not material for this show in any form.

Talking points with receipts.

  • What the described procedure actually is: induced pluripotent stem cell reprogramming. No embryo, no fetus, no organ and no second person was created by it. No fertilised embryo capable of developing into another human being was made. [^2]
  • Score it. Which laboratory: not identified. Where are the results: not published. Assay certificate: none provided. Independently reviewed paper: none linked. [^2] ZERO OUT OF FOUR, and the claim is still being repeated three months later, which is the point of the scorecard.
  • The honest counterweight, said out loud: zero out of four does not mean nothing happened. A cell line very probably exists. It means we cannot tell, and neither can anyone repeating the headline.

CLIP MOMENT: "Zero out of four does not mean he is lying. It means nobody can check. Those are different accusations and only one of them is mine."

SEGMENT 3: Now us (about 5 minutes)

THE RADAR SET THIS AS A BINDING CONDITION THIS MORNING AND IT IS MET HERE: a format that debuts by scoring a competitor is an attack ad wearing a method's clothes. So the third subject is MySpawn, and the scorecard does not come out clean.

THE CLAIMS WE ACTUALLY MAKE, read out as claims. Samples are held by GenVault, an independent biorepository in New Jersey, at ambient temperature, described as CAP-accredited, ISO 9001 and ISO 20387, and FDA-registered. Storage is $99 a year, billed annually, and the first 1,000 members lock the founding rate.

Score them.

  • Which laboratory: PASS, and this is the strongest thing we have. It is named, it is a separate company from the brand that sold you the kit, and it is in a stated location. Most of this category cannot say that.
  • Where are the results: NOT APPLICABLE, and that is an advantage we should stop pretending is a virtue. We publish no results because we run no tests. Storage only: no analysis, no interpretation, no sequencing. There is no efficacy number to withhold.
  • Is there a certificate: PARTIAL, AND THIS IS THE FAILURE. We state accreditations. We have never published the certificates. An accreditation is a document with a number and an expiry date on it, and asking a custodian to show you one is exactly the ordinary paperwork this episode has spent fifteen minutes demanding of other people. We should publish them. Until we do, a listener applying this checklist to us gets a partial on question three and they are right to.
  • Is there an independently reviewed paper: NO, and no claim of ours requires one, because we make no scientific claim about outcomes. The moment we do, that answer has to change.

AND THE CLAIM WE REFUSE TO MAKE, which is the real answer to the first two segments. We do not promise descendant creation. In vitro gametogenesis has produced no mature human gametes; human primordial germ cell-like cells exist from iPSCs and the final maturation step remains unaccomplished. Artificial womb technology is not commercially available and not approved for human trials. "EctoLife" and the "pregnancy robot" are fakes. We store a biological record and we do not tell you what it will one day permit, because nobody honestly can.

CLIP MOMENT: "We score ourselves partial on question three, and we can fix it this month by publishing a PDF. If we have not done that by the time you hear this, ask us why."
