"There are three different ways to count whether people are having children, and they give three different answers. All three are real.

Ask people what family size is ideal, in the abstract. Gallup has the American preference for three or more at its highest since nineteen seventy-one, and young adults are more likely than older adults to say it.

Ask people what they personally want and expect. Roughly a third of young adults say they neither have children nor want them.

Then stop asking, and count the babies. The US fertility rate hit a record low in twenty twenty-four, about one point six children per woman. That one is reported, from twenty twenty-four data, and I have not read it at source, so take it as reported and not as mine.

Ideal, expected, actual. Three numbers, three answers, no contradiction, because they are three different questions.

Here is the part worth keeping. The gap between the first number and the third one is not confusion and it is not hypocrisy. It is the distance between what people want and what people can currently afford or arrange. That gap is the measurement.

So when somebody hands you one of those three numbers and tells you what it means about the future, ask which of the three it is. They will usually have picked the one that suits the argument.

I do not know which way the next thirty years go. Nobody quoting one number at you does either."

About a third of young adults want none. The 2024 fertility rate was a record low, around 1.6. All three are real. [1]

X POST 2. The gap between the family people say is ideal and the family people actually have is not hypocrisy. It is the distance between a preference and a budget. Most coverage reads it as a values story. It is closer to a price story.

X POST 3. A survey measuring the ideal and a survey measuring the expectation will diverge most when conditions are hardest. So the widening gap is not evidence that people changed their minds. It is evidence of what changed around them.

THREADS POSTS

THREADS 1. Something I keep running into: two sound surveys can point opposite ways and neither is wrong, because they asked different questions. "What is the ideal family size" and "how many do you expect to have" are not the same question, and treating them as the same is how a data story turns into a panic. If you only ever see one number quoted, that is a choice somebody made.

THREADS 2. The useful question when the forecast is genuinely unknown is not "which future is coming." It is "which decisions cost me the same in every future." Those are the ones you can make now without needing to be right. Very few decisions qualify. Keeping a record of something is one of them. MySpawn is $99 a year, billed annually, and it does not require anyone to predict anything.
