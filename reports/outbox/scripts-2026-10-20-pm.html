THREADS POSTS (2)

Threads 1:

There is a measurement problem at the bottom of most cold storage, and it is not exotic.
The thing that goes first is the liquid. The thing most alarms watch is the temperature. The liquid starts going in days and the temperature does not report it for weeks, and then it moves fast, because at the end there is nothing left holding the cold.
It is a fuel gauge that reads full until the tank is empty.
You do not need to know any chemistry to ask the one question that matters: what does the alarm actually measure?

Threads 2:

The most impressive thing I read this week was an organisation describing its own failure.
It had lost a large amount of frozen material. Its published review said the failure was not any single event or source of error but the result of multiple issues.
No one cause. No one person. Nothing to point at.
That sentence is much harder to write than a denial, and a field only learns from the organisations willing to write it. If a custodian has never published anything like it, that is not evidence they have never failed.
