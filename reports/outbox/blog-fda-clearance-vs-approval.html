# FDA Clearance vs Approval vs an IND Go-Ahead: What Each One Actually Means

**"FDA cleared" and "FDA approved" are not synonyms, and neither one is what a company means when it says it got an FDA go-ahead to start a trial. Clearance (the 510(k) pathway) means a moderate-risk device was found substantially equivalent to something already on the market: it is a finding that the device resembles a predicate, not a finding that it works. Approval (PMA) is a separate, higher bar for high-risk devices, requiring independent evidence of safety and effectiveness. De Novo is a third route that grants marketing authorization to a novel low-to-moderate-risk device with no predicate. And an IND go-ahead is permission to begin studying a drug in people. It is a start line, not a result.**

Four words, four meanings, and press coverage routinely swaps them. Here is each one, plus a thirty-second method for reading any regulatory announcement.

**Epistemic note, up front, because this article is about precision.** The descriptions below come from regulatory-industry summaries and secondary analysis read through a search index. **No page was opened at the agency's own domain**, and nothing is quoted from a regulation. **Review times and counts move; re-check any figure before relying on it.** Not medical, legal or regulatory advice.

## First, the distinction that catches most people

Before the four words, there is a prior one. **Registration applies to establishments. Clearance, approval and authorization apply to products.**

A facility that is "FDA-registered" has told the agency it exists and is inspectable. Nobody has evaluated anything it makes or does. We covered that separately, including our own use of the phrase, in [what ISO 20387, CAP and "FDA-registered" each actually certify](/blog/biobank-accreditation-explained). **This article is about products**: everything below concerns a thing being sold or studied rather than a building.

## Cleared: the 510(k) pathway and substantial equivalence

The 510(k) route applies to moderate-risk devices. The sponsor's job is to show the device is **substantially equivalent** to a legally marketed **predicate device**: same intended use, and either the same technological characteristics or different ones that do not raise new questions of safety or effectiveness. ([Freyr Solutions](https://www.freyrsolutions.com/blog/510k-vs-de-novo-key-differences-in-fda-approval-pathways-for-medical-devices), [MedDeviceGuide](https://meddeviceguide.com/blog/pma-vs-510k-comparison).)

If the agency agrees, it issues a **Substantially Equivalent letter**, and that letter is what "FDA clearance" means. Reported typical review time is around **90 calendar days**, though in practice the clock stops and starts.

### Why "it resembles" is not "it works"

Read the test again. The comparison is **against another product**, not against an outcome. A device can be cleared because it is close enough to something that was itself cleared because it was close enough to something earlier. **Nowhere in that chain is anyone required to show the device helps a patient.**

That is not a scandal. It is the deliberate design of a tiered system. **But it does mean "FDA cleared" in a headline is a statement about lineage, not efficacy**, and a company that says "cleared" when a reader hears "approved" has not technically lied.

## Approved: premarket approval

**Premarket approval, or PMA, is the strictest device pathway.** It applies to high-risk Class III devices: the ones that support or sustain life, or that carry a potential unreasonable risk of illness or injury.

A PMA has to contain **valid scientific evidence, usually including clinical data, that independently establishes the device is safe and effective.** The word "independently" is doing the work. **Clearance compares. Approval evaluates.** ([MedDeviceGuide](https://meddeviceguide.com/blog/pma-vs-510k-comparison).)

This is also the word used for drugs, which is one reason the two get conflated: "FDA approved" about a pill and about a device are two different statutory processes sharing a verb.

## Granted: the De Novo route

There is a third market pathway and it is the one most often missing from explainers. **De Novo exists for a genuinely novel device that has low to moderate risk and no predicate to compare against.** It cannot use 510(k), because there is nothing to resemble, and it should not need PMA, because the risk does not warrant it.

A successful De Novo ends in a **grant order**, which authorizes marketing and also **creates a new classification regulation and product code**. That matters more than it sounds: **the granted device can then serve as the predicate for other companies' future 510(k) submissions.** One De Novo opens a door that others walk through. ([Freyr Solutions](https://www.freyrsolutions.com/blog/510k-vs-de-novo-key-differences-in-fda-approval-pathways-for-medical-devices).)

De Novo is slow. One industry analysis reports De Novo wait times rising to around **420 days in 2024**. **Label: third-party industry analysis, not an agency figure, and a two-year-old number as of this writing.**

## Permission to start: the IND and the thirty-day clock

Now the one that is not a market authorization at all.

An **Investigational New Drug application** is how a sponsor gets permission to give an unapproved drug to people in a study. The mechanism is unusual and worth knowing exactly: **an IND goes into effect 30 calendar days after the agency receives it, unless the agency notifies the sponsor of a clinical hold, or notifies them earlier that the trials may proceed.** ([UNC Drug Development](https://drugdevelopment.web.unc.edu/u-s-investigational-new-drug-application-%E2%86%92-ind-application-process/), [FDA guidance document](https://fda.gov/media/72751/download).)

**Read that structure carefully. The default is permission.** The review runs concurrently with the waiting period; if nobody objects within thirty days, the sponsor may begin. **An IND clearing is closer to a non-objection than to a grant.**

### What a clinical hold is

A **clinical hold** is an order delaying a proposed trial or suspending a running one, and it can apply to one study or several under the same IND. Where grounds exist and patients are not at immediate and serious risk, the agency will typically try to resolve the concern with the sponsor before issuing the order. ([ONS](https://www.ons.org/12-2016/why-does-fda-place-clinical-holds-investigational-new-drug-clinical-trials), [HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/sites/default/files/hhs-guidance-documents/FDA/Submitting-and-Reviewing-Complete-Responses-to-Clinical-Holds-%28Revised%29.pdf).)

So when a company announces that its IND "cleared" and the market treats it as validation: **what happened is that a trial may now begin.** Everything that would make the drug worth anything is still ahead of it. For how long that distance can be, see our write-up of [cellular reprogramming](/blog/cellular-reprogramming-explained).

## Designations are not authorizations

One more category, because it generates the most misleading headlines of all.

**Breakthrough Device Designation** gives a sponsor prioritized review, a dedicated review team, senior engagement and more frequent interaction. **It does not lower the standard for clearance, De Novo or approval, and it is not permission to market anything.** ([NAMSA](https://namsa.com/resources/blog/fda-breakthrough-device-designation/), [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/health-industry-washington-watch/102k2a8/fda-updates-breakthrough-device-guidance/).)

Here is the number that settles the argument. **Of 839 devices that had received the designation as of 30 June 2023, 81 had been cleared, approved or granted De Novo classification.** **Label: secondary analysis of agency data, current as of mid-2023 and not re-verified here.** **Designation is a queue position. Roughly nine in ten of those devices had not reached a market authorization at that point.**

## How to read an announcement in thirty seconds

Four questions, in order. None requires any science.

1. **Which word is it?** Cleared, approved, granted, designated, or permitted to begin a trial. Those are five different events.
2. **Product or establishment?** If the sentence is about a facility being registered, it contains no evaluation of anything.
3. **What is the date of the event, not the date of the article?** Coverage recycles. An eighteen-month-old authorization can arrive in a feed looking like news, and two sources giving two different dates for "the same" event are often describing two different events.
4. **What has actually finished?** A designation is a queue position. An IND is a start line. A clearance is a comparison. Only an approval or a De Novo grant rests on an independent evaluation of the product itself.

If a press release will not survive those four questions, the problem is the release.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is FDA cleared the same as FDA approved?
No. Clearance comes from the 510(k) pathway and means a moderate-risk device was found substantially equivalent to a legally marketed predicate device. Approval comes from premarket approval and requires independent valid scientific evidence, usually including clinical data, that a high-risk device is safe and effective. Clearance compares one product to another. Approval evaluates the product itself.

### What does it mean when a company says its IND cleared?
It means the company may begin a clinical trial. An IND goes into effect 30 calendar days after the agency receives it unless the agency imposes a clinical hold or tells the sponsor earlier that trials may proceed. The default outcome is permission, so an IND clearing is closer to a non-objection than to a grant, and it says nothing about whether the drug works.

### What is the De Novo pathway?
De Novo is a marketing route for a novel device with low to moderate risk and no predicate device to compare against. A successful request ends in a grant order that authorizes marketing and creates a new classification regulation and product code, which can then serve as a predicate for later 510(k) submissions by other companies.

### Does Breakthrough Device Designation mean a device is approved?
No. The designation provides prioritized review and closer interaction with the agency. It does not lower the standard for clearance, De Novo classification or approval, and it is not permission to market anything. Of 839 designated devices as of 30 June 2023, 81 had reached a clearance, approval or De Novo grant.

### Does FDA approve companies or facilities?
No. The agency does not approve establishments. Registration means a facility has notified the agency that it exists and is subject to inspection, and it conveys no approval or endorsement of the facility or its products. Approval, clearance and authorization apply to products.
