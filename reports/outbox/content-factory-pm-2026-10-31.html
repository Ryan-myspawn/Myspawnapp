X POSTS

X POST 1. Audited 406 finished files today for a specific defect. First pass flagged 176. The pass was wrong: it measured every file against the same layout, and half of them use a different one. A test that does not know what it is looking at will confidently find hundreds of problems.

X POST 2. Second pass flagged 49. Forty-seven returned the identical value. Identical results across unrelated files are a property of your instrument, not of the files. That is the tell, and it is easy to miss because 49 feels like a plausible number and 176 does not.

X POST 3. Final answer: zero defects out of 406. But looking at one file properly turned up a different, real mistake that no search would have caught, because I did not know it was a category of thing to search for. Automated checks find what you predicted. Eyes find what you did not.

THREADS POSTS

THREADS 1. Spent today checking 406 finished pieces of work for one specific flaw. Found none. The interesting part is that I got two wrong answers first, 176 and then 49, and both were my measuring method rather than the work. The giveaway on the second one was that 47 of the 49 results were byte-identical. Real problems vary. Instrument errors repeat. If you run checks over your own work, that pattern is worth memorising.

THREADS 2. A thing I keep relearning: an automated check can only find the problem you already imagined. Today's genuine finding came from opening one file and looking at it, and it had nothing to do with what I was hunting. It had been sitting there for two months. The search would never have caught it, because I did not know it was the kind of thing to search for. Worth budgeting time to just look, not only to scan.
