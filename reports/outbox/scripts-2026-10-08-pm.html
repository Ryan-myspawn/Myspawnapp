The radar's Concept 1 hook was: "The scientist who made eggs out of skin cells just published a paper about people overhyping making eggs out of skin cells." That overstates the authorship in a specific and checkable way.

WHAT THE RECORD ACTUALLY SHOWS. The paper is "Changing Discourse on In Vitro Gametogenesis: Expectation, Scientific Reality, and the Ethics of Hype", Monash Bioethics Review, published online June 2026, DOI 10.1007/s40592-026-00295-0, PubMed 42247096. Daisuke Yamashita is the lead author. Katsuhiko Hayashi is a CO-AUTHOR. [1]

AND A SECOND THING, WHICH IS BETTER THAN THE VERSION WE HAD. The paper does not accuse the scientists. Per the available abstract and excerpts, it locates the hype in the discourse: bioethics literature, media coverage and the framing of IVG as a near-term route to human reproduction. It cites a BBC article featuring Hayashi's own mouse research as an example of CAUTIOUS comment from researchers who are actually doing the work. [1]

So the honest story is not "scientist attacks his own field." It is "the cautious voices are already on the record, and the noise is downstream of them." That is the version this script shoots, and it is both more accurate and harder to dismiss. The radar's own risk flag said do not imply a feud; the search showed there is not one to imply.

One further precision, carried into the script. The lab's landmark result used induced pluripotent stem cells derived from embryonic fibroblasts and from ADULT TAIL-TIP fibroblasts (Hikabe et al., Nature 539, 299-303, 10 November 2016, doi:10.1038/nature20104). [2] "Skin cells" is loose. "Cells from an adult mouse's tail" is what the paper says. The script makes that correction out loud, because a piece about overstatement cannot open with one.
