THREE X DRAFTS

All three measured programmatically before being typed into this file. 243, 272 and 214 characters against a 280 limit.

X1 (243):

An edit present in some cells of an embryo and absent in others has a name: mosaicism. Once it is in the room, "the edit worked" stops meaning anything on its own. Worked in what fraction of cells? Of embryos? In how many was it in every cell?

X2 (272):

Right to Try removes the FDA review step. Reported figure: the agency authorises more than 99 per cent of expanded access requests, often within hours. The review was not the barrier. And under either route the manufacturer is still under no obligation to supply anything.

X3 (214):

A scope of accreditation is not a gold star. It is a list. It names the specific activities a lab was assessed for, and anything not on the list was not assessed. "Accredited" without a scope is a word, not a fact.

NOTE ON X2, and it is a labelling decision rather than a stylistic one: the 99 per cent figure is introduced on-post with the words "Reported figure", because it comes from policy literature read through a search index and a bare percentage in a 280-character box has no room for a footnote. A number that cannot carry its label should either carry it in the sentence or not be posted.

TWO THREADS POSTS

Threads 1:

Four pieces of coverage discussed mosaicism in human embryo editing. None of them printed the rate.

That is not a conspiracy. It is what happens when a research result gets compressed into a headline: the number that would matter clinically is not the number the study was designed to produce.

Still worth noticing which number survives the compression. It is always the flattering one.

Threads 2:

A right to ask is not a right to receive.

Two of the four routes to a drug that is not approved for your use run through a regulator. Neither of them obliges the company that makes it to hand anything over. It can simply decline, and it does not have to say why.

Permission structures are not supply.
