THE WEEK'S ANGLE: "Who Has To Prove What"

The axis is AUTHORITY, not duration: not how long a custodian keeps a thing, but who is allowed to retrieve it and what they must produce to do so.

DISCLOSED AND ARGUED, because it is the closest thing in the log and a reader of both would notice: 7 October ran "Every Custodian Has A Clock", whose axis is the LENGTH of the retention clock and who sets it. That angle asks how long. This one asks who, and what they have to show. They share the safe deposit box as a proof object and they ask opposite questions of it: 7 October asked what happens if you go silent for three years; this week asks what your executor has to hand over the counter. The two do not share a single sentence and the overlap is stated here rather than left for the founder to spot.

Why it is the strongest COMMERCIAL angle available this week. It sells the $99 a year directly, because the answer to "who can get it and what must they prove" is a product feature we can state in writing. And it is the only angle this week that works against BOTH ends of the competitive set at once without naming anyone: against a cheaper self-stored product, whose access question is answered by whoever finds the object; and against a far more expensive institutional one. It also avoids the binding instruction added at 14:2x today, because it makes no price-superiority claim at all and therefore needs no time horizon attached.

(b) TIKTOK / REELS PAID: two spark-ad-style scripts, 15 seconds

T1 · "The Question"

[SHOT 1, 0.0 to 2.0s: phone-native, handheld, speaking straight to camera, no caption card.]

"There is one question worth asking anybody who is holding something of yours."

[SHOT 2, 2.0 to 7.0s: same framing, no cut, slight lean in.]

"Not how much does it cost. Who can get it, and what do they have to prove?"

[SHOT 3, 7.0 to 12.0s: cut to a hand placing a phone face-down on a table.]

"A bank has an answer to that. A hospital has an answer. Ask a storage company and watch what happens."

[SHOT 4, 12.0 to 15.0s: static, plain.]

"Ours is in writing, before you need it. Ninety-nine dollars a year."

Native feel: no music, no logo until the final second, no on-screen text except the final price line. Hook is spoken, not captioned, which is what makes it read as organic.

T2 · "Two Ways"

[SHOT 1, 0.0 to 2.0s: two objects on a table, a small box and a paper tag, nothing else.]

"There are two ways to keep something for fifty years."

[SHOT 2, 2.0 to 8.0s: hand moves the box an inch.]

"You keep it. Then it survives as long as one person keeps choosing to move house with it."

[SHOT 3, 8.0 to 13.0s: hand moves the tag.]

"Or you have it kept. Then somebody has to still exist, still be paid, and still care."

[SHOT 4, 13.0 to 15.0s: static on both objects.]

"We are the second kind. We would rather tell you that than let you find out."

Native feel: tabletop, no face, no music. This is the paid cut of yesterday's organic script and the relationship is stated so performance can be read against it.

COMPLIANCE PASS

  • Storage only: PASS. Every asset says storage, and three say explicitly that we do not interpret the genome or create anything from it. No descendant creation implied anywhere.
  • Pricing: PASS. "$99 a year" or "$99/year, billed annually" in every concept. The words "one-time", "once", "no subscription" and "no annual fee" appear nowhere.
  • Accreditations: ONE FLAG, raised above. The copy says "independently accredited third-party facility" and names no accreditor, which is correct. RSA headline 4's shorter "Stored At An Accredited Lab" is accurate but compresses the attribution, and it is held unless the landing page attributes GenVault in the first screen.
  • Health claims: PASS. None made.
  • Fear-mongering: PASS, and checked twice on M2. The 40-60 concept uses process friction, not death imagery, and the phrase "if you died tomorrow" was drafted and cut for exactly that reason. No concept implies a reader is failing their family. Nothing targets parents through a child.
  • State-specific figures: DELIBERATELY ABSENT FROM ALL PAID COPY, honouring the 7 October flag that state figures in paid creative go stale. The state rules behind this angle are in this week's blog article and stay there.
  • LEGAL REVIEW, TWO ITEMS CARRIED FORWARD AND NEITHER IS NEW: (1) "first 1,000 members lock the founding rate" depends on a member counter, a flag open since 19 August and never cleared, and it is therefore omitted from every asset in this batch; (2) "who may claim it is answered in your account, in writing" is a factual claim about the product and must be true on the day the ad runs. If the custody terms are not currently visible in a member's account, M2's description and RSA description 2 are false and must not run. This is the strongest claim in the batch and the easiest to falsify.
