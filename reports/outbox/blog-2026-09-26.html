The spine is a number nobody reconciles in public. The same event is reported as $30M, $46.75M, $50M and $150M. The article shows they are mostly one settlement at different points in its life: $46.75M is the consumer class settlement gross, approved 7 July 2026; the ~$30M figure is what remains to distribute; and the $150M is a separate 42-state settlement that is allowed claims against a bankruptcy estate, not cash. The states recover about $18M: roughly twelve cents on the dollar.

The most useful consumer fact is a negative one, and it leads: the claim deadline of 17 February 2026 has passed. Payouts run roughly $50 to $165, with $165 where health information was affected and up to $10,000 documented. A page that let a reader hope they could still claim would be worse than no page.

The generalizable part is why this is lane (d) rather than news: when the payer is a bankruptcy estate, an allowed claim is a position in a queue rather than a payment, the remedy arrives years late, and the money was never the thing anyone wanted.
