THE SIX KILLS, IN THE ORDER THEY HAPPENED

1. "What the ASRM actually says about freezing immature eggs" (lane b). QUEUED 28 OCTOBER WITH A CONDITION, AND THE CONDITION KILLED IT. The condition was to grep vitrification and antral follicle first, because a thin follow-up would cannibalise the IVM article. Both terms appear in exactly one published article: what-is-in-vitro-maturation.md, two days old, which spends two paragraphs on precisely this material. A dedicated piece would compete with our own page for the same intent.

2. "Payable on death accounts" (lane c). QUEUED 29 OCTOBER WITH A CONDITION, AND KILLED ON A DIFFERENT ONE. POD account and minor beneficiary are both zero, so the mechanics are genuinely uncovered. But yesterday's article was transfer-on-death, and a second consecutive estate-mechanics piece is the same-lane back-to-back pattern this operation has killed twice before, on 15 and 29 October. Deferred rather than killed outright: it is still a good topic on a day that is not the day after a TOD article.

3. "Can you get DNA from hair without the root?" (lane a). RESEARCHED IN FULL, THEN KILLED BY THE TOPIC LOG. Two web searches were spent and the material was strong: STR success reported at 96-98 per cent for hairs with roots against 89-93 per cent rootless in one 2025 study, with that source itself flagging its rootless figure as far above the field norm; a 2018 finding that a mean 96.94 per cent of DNA recovered from shafts is nuclear but almost all fragments are under 100 nucleotides; mitogenome sequencing from 1mm and 5mm shafts up to 27 years old; and SNP panels at 70.3-99.7 per cent against miniSTR at 22.1-63.8 per cent on telogen roots. Then the log showed best-dna-sample-type.md from 30 August, whose secondary keywords already include "hair root vs shaft DNA" and "can you get DNA from fingernails", with sections titled for both and an FAQ answering the root question directly. The research is banked in this file rather than wasted.

4. Lane (e) audience angles: single, childfree, no next of kin. KILLED BY TWO EXISTING ARTICLES. childfree-legacy.md and single-no-kids-legacy-planning.md both exist. single by choice and no next of kin return zero as phrases, but the audience they describe is already served.

5. "What does 'ongoing pregnancy' mean in a fertility trial?" (from yesterday's Science Watch). KILLED ON A TWO-DAY COLLISION. ongoing pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, live birth rate and primary endpoint all appear in what-is-in-vitro-maturation.md, published two days ago.

6. "Will your video still play in twenty years?" (lane c, file-format obsolescence). KILLED BY digital-time-capsule.md, which already covers codecs, MP4, H.264, Library of Congress format guidance and migration.

THE DIAGNOSIS, STATED PLAINLY

The archive is 72 articles and the high-intent space immediately around this product is now densely covered. Today was not a thin-research day: two searches returned excellent material and the kill came from our own index afterwards. That ordering is the lesson. The grep has to run before the research, not after it, and on today's evidence it has to run against SECONDARY keywords in the topic log rather than only against article bodies. The hair article would have been killed in thirty seconds by reading the 30 August row.

Writing a seventh near-duplicate to satisfy a daily cadence is precisely what the brief's anti-repetition bar exists to prevent, so nothing was written.
