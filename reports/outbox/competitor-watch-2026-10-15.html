BONUS DIVE: CRYONICS INSTITUTE, and the premise of its own brief was wrong

THE ROTATION'S JOB, SET IN WRITING YESTERDAY: "find a CURRENT dues schedule at cryonics.org, because our only figure is roughly 1,700 days old and we have now been caught once this week citing a stale number."

THAT PREMISE IS FALSE, AND baseline.md CONTAINS THE PROOF. Deep-dive #14, dated 5 September 2026, records CI's membership figures as verified on 3 September 2026 at cryonics.org/membership.html: whole-body $28,000 for lifetime members ($1,250 once) or $35,000 for yearly members ($120/yr), headline price unchanged since 1976, realistic all-in $80,000 to $100,000+ depending on distance to Michigan.

Our figure was 42 days old, not 1,700. SO THE DIVE TOOK THE JOB THE 13 OCTOBER ROTATION ACTUALLY ASKED FOR, which was never answered for CI: what does the membership-plus-preservation structure oblige a member to pay ANNUALLY? That question produced the Alcor correction yesterday and it produces a sharper answer here.

**THE ANNUAL STRUCTURE, from CI's own FAQ (cryonics.org/faq, read via search index; cryonics.org was not opened from this machine):**

  • Lifetime membership: a single $1,250 payment. No initiation fee.
  • Annual membership: a $200 initiation fee PLUS $120 a year, payable quarterly at $35.
  • The preservation minimum depends on which one you chose: $28,000 for lifetime members, $35,000 for annual members, paid once at death, including vitrification perfusion and long-term storage.
  • CI's own FAQ states that an annual member who later upgrades to lifetime saves $7,000.

THE $200 INITIATION FEE IS NEW TO THIS FILE. Deep-dive #14 recorded the $120 and missed it. Corrected here rather than quietly added.

THE STRUCTURAL FINDING, and it is the useful one: at CI, choosing the annual route costs $7,000 more at death. The yearly fee is not a cheaper way in; it is a deferral that prices itself. That is the opposite architecture from Alcor's, where yesterday's dive established dues indexed to current age (age times $15 a year, plus $200 a year for Comprehensive Member Standby). Alcor prices the waiting. CI prices the not-committing. Two in-category competitors, two opposite answers to the same question, and neither is the model anybody would guess from the headline preservation figure, which is exactly why the 13 October rotation asked.

SOURCE CONFLICT, STATED RATHER THAN RESOLVED. A Substack guide lists annual membership as $125 plus a $75 initiation fee against CI's own FAQ's $120 plus $200. CI's own page wins on hierarchy and the conflict is recorded. Separately, an optional standby figure of $60,000 appears in a 2021 source: roughly five years old, labelled, and not usable.

COSTS CI EXPLICITLY DOES NOT COVER, which matters for any honest comparison: distance-related costs, local funeral-director fees and transport to Michigan, all paid to third parties rather than to CI.

STEAL. Deep-dive #14 took the price-stability story. Today's steal is different and smaller: CI publishes the arithmetic of its own two options, including the sentence that one of them costs $7,000 more. A company telling you which of its tiers is worse value is doing something almost nobody in our category does, and it buys more trust than the $7,000 costs them. Our founding-rate lock is the same kind of fact and we have never written out the arithmetic of it plainly.
