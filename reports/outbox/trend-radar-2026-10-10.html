Science Watch

THE ITEM: A FIVE-MONTH-OLD PAPER WITH A THREE-DAY-OLD NEWS CYCLE.

What the paper reports. American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Jian Li et al., DOI 10.1016/j.ajog.2026.05.005, PubMed 42173184. IVF-conceived babies carried a higher overall proportion of LINE-1 DNA than naturally conceived babies, a statistically significant difference. Eleven genomic loci showed differential L1 insertion frequencies and fourteen showed differential deletion frequencies. Levels did not differ significantly between standard IVF and ICSI. Coverage notes the difference was clearer among girls, with boys showing a similar but less certain trend.

The numbers that decide how much this means: a preprint version reports 33 IVF-conceived and 42 naturally conceived offspring. Seventy-five children in total.

EPISTEMIC LABEL: peer-reviewed journal article, read via coverage and abstract; the paper was not opened at its own domain. The authors themselves call the results preliminary, and the long-term disease link in the title is INFERRED FROM GENE PROXIMITY rather than demonstrated. That inference is sitting in the paper's own title, which is where claim inflation usually starts.

SO WHAT, and the preserve-now angle does not require the finding to hold up: this is a story about a measurement taken on a cohort of seventy-five, five months ago, arriving as news this week. We do not have a view on whether IVF alters retrotransposon dynamics and we are not going to acquire one from a press cycle. What we can say is narrower: the record of a person is a different object from a claim about a population of seventy-five.
