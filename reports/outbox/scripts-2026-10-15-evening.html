NO TWO-COLUMN OR SPLIT-SCREEN TREATMENT, DELIBERATELY. The obvious design for a same-words-different-things script is two columns. The 21 September evergreen "Two Columns" owns that format and a 14 October attempt to reuse it was killed for duplication. This script does the disambiguation in sequence instead.

Primary audience: Men 25-55

Trend / hook used: Biomemory's DNA Shield launch, 15 October 2026, same day.

Length: 55 seconds.

Script

[SHOT 1: him, tight, no music, flat. 0:00]

VO: "A company launched a product called DNA Shield today. No, they did not invent a way to back you up."

[ON-SCREEN TEXT: 15 Oct 2026. Company announcement.]

[SHOT 2: 0:07]

VO: "What they built writes your encrypted files into synthetic DNA and keeps it offline. It is a storage medium. The DNA is the hard drive."

[ON-SCREEN TEXT: Their DNA = the medium.]

[SHOT 3: 0:16]

VO: "What we do is the other direction entirely. We keep a physical sample from a person. The DNA is not the container. It is the thing."

[ON-SCREEN TEXT: Our DNA = the contents.]

[SHOT 4: slight pause, tone shifts from correction to interest. 0:24]

VO: "You cannot store a spreadsheet in ours. You cannot recover a person from theirs. Same two words, almost nothing in common."

[SHOT 5: 0:31]

VO: "Except one thing, and it is the part I actually want to talk about."

[SHOT 6: 0:34]

VO: "Read how they designed it. The customer encrypts the data before it ever reaches them. It sits offline. Getting it back is described as deliberate and controlled, not self-service."

[ON-SCREEN TEXT, three lines appearing in turn: Encrypted before it arrives. / Offline. / Recovery is deliberate.]

[SHOT 7: 0:45]

VO: "That is a custody design, not a storage spec. Two companies solving completely unrelated problems landed on the same instinct: the valuable part is who can reach it, and how hard that is on purpose."

[SHOT 8: final card, no face. 0:51]

[ON-SCREEN TEXT, two lines held: Ask any storage company how hard it is to get it back. / A fast answer is the wrong answer.]

[CARD: myspawn.me · $99/yr]

SOUND/MUSIC: room tone only until shot 8, then one low sustained note. No bed. Shot 5's pause is a real pause, not a cut.

  • Reels: add one second to shot 3. The 35-to-60 audience is likelier to have heard "DNA storage" and assumed it meant us.
  • Shorts: start at shot 1 but cut shot 4 to a single sentence ("You cannot recover a person from theirs"), out at 48 seconds.
  • 7-second teaser cut: shot 1 in full, then hard cut to black on "back you up". The correction with no explanation is the hook.

Ship-blockers, four

1. VERIFY BEFORE SHOOTING. The announcement was read through a search index; prnewswire.com was NOT opened from this machine. Open the release, confirm the product name, the launch date and the three design facts in shot 6, and confirm that "deliberate and controlled" is a fair characterisation of their own wording rather than a paraphrase hardening into a claim.

2. NO CAPACITY OR DURATION CLAIM. Our sources do not state how much DNA Shield holds or for how long it stays retrievable. Those numbers are absent from the script on purpose and must not be added from memory.

3. The company is named NEUTRALLY and is never criticised. This is a disambiguation and a compliment, not a comparison. No claim about whether their product works.

4. Shot 3 must not drift into a capability claim. We keep a record. We do not recover, revive, reconstruct or recreate anybody, and the script says "the thing", not "the person".
