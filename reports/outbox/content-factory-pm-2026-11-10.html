2 THREADS POSTS

Threads 1:

Somebody in your family is the archivist. It is usually not the person who volunteered.
It is whoever happens to have the boxes. The photographs, the slides nobody can play, the one hard drive with everything on it from 2004 to 2013. Almost always, it fell to them because they had the garage.
Two things follow from that and both are uncomfortable. The first is that the collection is in whatever condition that person's house is in. The second is that nobody has ever told them what to do with it.
There is a version of this conversation that takes ten minutes. You ask who has what. You write it down. You do not ask anyone to digitise anything, because that request kills the conversation.
The list is the whole value. A thing nobody knows about is already lost, and it does not require a fire.

Threads 2:

A search can fail in a way that looks exactly like success, and it happened to this account tonight.
We check every new piece against the previous week so we do not repeat ourselves. Tonight the check cleared a subject, and the subject had gone out one day earlier.
The reason is small and it generalises. We searched for the technical term from the source. We had written about it in plain English. The word was not there to be found, so the search returned nothing, and nothing read as clear.
The same trap lives in hyphens. Search for attorney-in-fact and you will not find attorney in fact. Search for health care proxy and you will not find healthcare proxy.
If a search comes back empty, the first question is not what it means. It is whether you searched in your own vocabulary or somebody else's.
