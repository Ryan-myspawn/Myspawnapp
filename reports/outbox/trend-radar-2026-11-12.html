CONCEPT 1: "The Letter Is The Deadline" [X or Shorts]

Freshness check: dormant, unclaimed and escheat ALL return ZERO across reports/social/scripts/. The source is this morning's published blog article, which is live and therefore available.

Primary audience: Men 25-55. The 38-year-old father is the one with an account he opened in 2014 and has not logged into since.

Trend-hook used: Washington lowered the dollar threshold that triggers a final notice letter from $75 to $50, generally effective 1 January 2026. Small, dated, and current law rather than history.

2-second hook: "If you get a letter about an account you forgot about, you are already late."

Beats:

1. An account goes quiet. The trigger is contact, not activity: interest posting into itself is not contact.

2. After a dormancy period, commonly three to five years and ranging from one year to fifteen depending on the property type, the balance goes to a state treasurer.

3. The twist, and it is the part nobody prints: escheatment runs on an ANNUAL REPORTING CYCLE. Holders batch it and file on a date their state sets.

4. So the warning letter is timed to the filing deadline, not to your dormancy date. That is why the rule is commonly a notice 60 to 90 days before the deadline.

5. Which means there is a window, and one contact inside it usually stops the transfer.

6. The line: a letter about a forgotten account is not a notification. It is a deadline.

Caption: The trigger is contact, not activity. Check the accounts you stopped thinking about.

Hashtags: #personalfinance #unclaimedmoney

BRAND FIT: strong, and it needs no product mention at all. RISK FLAG: none material. No company is named, no fear is required, and the instruction is free to act on.
