BONUS DIVE: ALCOR, and the narrowed job the rotation set

The rotation wrote this job yesterday: "establish what the membership-plus-preservation structure obliges a member to pay ANNUALLY, because every cryo comparison we have made so far used a headline preservation figure and ignored the dues."

THE PREMISE WAS WRONG AND CHECKING IT ANYWAY WAS WORTH MORE THAN CONFIRMING IT WOULD HAVE BEEN. Dive #13, on 4 September, already logged "$100+/month membership dues" for Alcor and "$1,250 lifetime membership or $120/yr" for Cryonics Institute. The dues were not ignored. They were recorded imprecisely, and today's source disagrees with our own file in two places.

What the annual obligation actually is

  • Dues are AGE-INDEXED, not flat: current age multiplied by $15 per year, for adults 19 and over. A 30-year-old pays $450 a year. A 50-year-old pays $750 a year. A new member at 18 pays $200; a minor pays $60.
  • Comprehensive Member Standby is a SEPARATE mandatory annual charge of $200 for cryopreservation members in the continental United States and Canada, and all payments are non-refundable.
  • So a 50-year-old's recurring obligation is about $950 a year before a single dollar of preservation funding.
  • Dues are locked in at the rate when you first join, and Alcor states it has increased dues in the past for members who are not Lifetime Members.
  • After 40 years of membership, dues drop to $60 a year.
  • An Associate Membership, for people with no cryonics arrangements, is $10 a month, $30 a quarter or $120 a year.
  • Preservation itself is funded separately, usually by life insurance: minimum death benefit currently $80,000 for neurocryopreservation and $200,000 for whole body.

The two corrections to our own baseline

1. Dive #13 recorded "$100+/month membership dues", which is $1,200 or more a year. The age formula does not reach that figure for anyone under about 80. Our file was high, and the shape was wrong: we recorded a flat rate where the structure is age-indexed.

2. Dive #13 recorded whole-body at "~$220,000". Today's source gives the current whole-body minimum death benefit as $200,000 and attributes the $220,000 figure to a DATED Alcor FAQ. We were citing the older number.

Both corrections are recorded rather than silently overwritten, because a baseline that quietly changes its own numbers is not a memory, it is a rumour.

EPISTEMIC LABEL, AND IT IS LOAD-BEARING: the dues schedule found today is published as a 2025 schedule and we have NOT confirmed a 2026 revision. Alcor states costs can change with 90 days' notice. Nothing here should be put in a deck without opening alcor.org/membership/pricing-and-dues directly. The Cryonics Institute figure of $120 a year traces to an Alcor-hosted comparison page roughly 1,700 days old and is UNVERIFIED for 2026; no current CI dues schedule was found.

SO WHAT FOR MYSPAWN

  • Pricing pressure: NONE, and the arithmetic is now precise rather than rhetorical. A 50-year-old pays Alcor roughly $950 a year in dues and standby alone. That is about 9.6 times our $99 a year, before preservation funding. And theirs rises every birthday while ours does not.
  • Copy angle: STRONG, and it is the age indexing rather than the size of the number. Our founding-rate lock is a quantity cap, first 1,000 members. Theirs is a date lock on an age-indexed fee. Both reward joining early; only one of them charges you more for having waited.
  • THE CAVEAT IS MANDATORY WHEREVER THIS COMPARISON APPEARS, exactly as with Acorn: Alcor preserves a body or a brain in cryogenic conditions and maintains an endowment to pay for liquid nitrogen indefinitely. We store a DNA record at ambient temperature. Different product, different promise, different failure modes, and their price is higher because the job is harder. A comparison that omits that is dishonest and we will not run one.
  • Threat level: NONE direct. Different promise, different price universe, different customer.

TACTIC WORTH STEALING, AND IT IS A STRUCTURE WE ALMOST HAVE: dues locked at the rate on the day you join, published as a rule rather than as a promotion. We already say "first 1,000 members lock the founding rate", which is the same mechanic described as a scarcity offer rather than as a policy. Stating it as a policy would be stronger and costs nothing. Founder decision, not an agent one.
