1. SECURIGENE: THE TWO-PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS IS CONFIRMED. THE DO-NOT-QUOTE FLAG CAN COME OFF.

This is the most important development of the run, and it is the resolution of our own open question rather than a competitor move.

Background, stated so the resolution is legible. On 26 September a check returned a SecuriGene figure that disagreed with this file's oldest figure of $498. The entry was flagged DO NOT QUOTE. On 18 October, deep-dive #24 formed a hypothesis: the disagreement was not a price change over time, it was two different products, a $389 capsule for a living person and a $498 capsule for post-mortem use. That dive could not establish it, because every page it reached was cached roughly 200 to 600 days ago, placing the retrieved copies BEFORE the September check. The dive declined the tidy answer and left the flag in place.

Today's sweep returns SecuriGene's own product pages, and the hypothesis holds.

  • DNA Banking Capsule: $389.00. LIST, on the company's own product page. Described as DNA extracted, purified, analyzed and preserved using their patented anhydrobiosis technology for stable room-temperature storage, and marketed as avoiding annual storage fees and dry-ice transportation. (securigene.com/dna-banking/dna-banking-capsule)
  • Post-Mortem DNA Banking Capsule: $498.00. LIST, own product page. And securigene.ca shows the SAME $498.00, which removes the currency hypothesis as well. (securigene.com, securigene.ca)

So the September check and this file's oldest figure were never in conflict. They were reading two different SKUs. The flag comes off, and what replaces it is more useful than a corrected number: a two-tier product structure keyed to WHEN the sample is taken.

NEW AND NOT PREVIOUSLY ON FILE: the forensic line has a full prepaid storage ladder, and it is the first ANNUAL figure we have ever had from SecuriGene.

Forensic Tissue Cryopreservation (whole cells, not a DNA record): $3,200 base, including the first year of storage. Extended storage: 1 year $291 · 10 years $2,000 · 30 years $4,920 · Lifetime $6,850. All LIST, own page. (securigene.com/cryopreservation/forensic-tissue-cryopreservation)

Engraving add-ons: $29 per capsule for name and date, $49 for custom.

And one reseller figure WITH ITS AGE, because the rule requires it: a funeral-services reseller lists the capsule at $498.00 to $1,218.00 depending on add-ons such as ancestry or health tests. That listing is reported as roughly 610 days old and is NOT used as a current price.

SO WHAT FOR MYSPAWN, three things, and the first one is uncomfortable.

(a) The 30-year prepaid at $4,920 works out to $164 a year, and the lifetime at $6,850 sells price certainty rather than savings. That is the third pricing model this file flagged as missing on 30 August, now with numbers attached, and it is the same promise shape as our founding-rate lock. The honest comparison stops there, because whole-cell cryopreservation is a different product from a dry DNA record and we will not pretend otherwise.

(b) Their two consumer capsules are ONE-TIME with no annual fee, and ours is $99 a year, billed annually. Our counter has never been and must never be price: it is that their capsule goes home with the family and ours stays in an accredited facility. That custody contrast is the whole argument and it gets sharper as their line broadens, not weaker.

(c) Threat level: moderate and structural, unchanged. Not a news event. The best-organised incumbent in our adjacency, now with its pricing architecture legible for the first time.

2. LEGACY-TECH: TWO NEWLY DISCOVERED ITEMS, AND BOTH OF THEM LAND ON OUR OWN PRODUCT.

Both are newly discovered, neither is in the baseline, and both are older than the 24-to-48-hour window. Ages stated first.

(a) SEVEN WEEKS OLD, AND IT IS A PREPRINT. A Lehigh University audit of 93 AI afterlife services, published as a preprint on 28 August 2026, reports that 82 of the 93 published thin or no information on who holds consent or authority over a recreation. Lehigh and 93 AI both return ZERO fleet-wide. (Epistemic status: preprint, not peer-reviewed. The figure is 82 of 93, which is roughly 88 percent, and it should be written as the fraction rather than the percentage.)

(b) FIFTEEN DAYS OLD. Sherry Turkle, in a Washington Post opinion piece dated 4 October 2026, describes building an AI avatar of her late mother and concludes that chatbots of the dead are dangerous for people who are grieving. Turkle returns ZERO fleet-wide. (Named real person, public opinion piece, accurately characterised and not quoted verbatim because we have not opened the article.)

SO WHAT FOR MYSPAWN, and this is against our own interest. We ship ECHO, an AI widget framed as talking to your descendant from 2126. The Lehigh finding is about who has authority over a recreation, and the Turkle critique is about what a simulation of the dead does to a grieving person.

There is a real distinction and we should state it rather than hide behind it: ECHO points FORWARD at a descendant who does not exist, not BACKWARD at a person who died. Nobody is grieving the subject of an ECHO conversation. That distinction is genuine and it is also not a complete answer, because the 82-of-93 consent finding is about documentation, and the honest question for us is whether our own pages say who controls an ECHO persona and on what terms. A founder decision, and a fair one to put plainly: if we cannot answer the Lehigh question about our own product, we are in the 82 rather than the 11.

(c) HEREAFTER AI: TODAY'S RESULT IS NOT TREATED AS A RESOLUTION, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE. Today's sweep returned the shutdown account again, with two added specifics: a farewell notice replacing the homepage in July 2026, and the website becoming inaccessible on 18 August 2026, with no explanation, deadline or refund policy, and families asked to request recordings by email.

This file has held HereAfter's status as CONTESTED since the 2 October dive, which returned live 2026 pricing pages and contradicted the shutdown finding, and the baseline carries an explicit standing instruction: DO NOT CALL THIS A FAILURE IN ANY ASSET.

Today's account comes from afterlife.ai, a company in the same category writing about a departed rival. That is the SAME conflicted source the 2 October dive already weighed. A conflicted source restating itself with more detail is not a second source. The contest stands, the instruction stands, and nothing in the baseline is changed on this point. Resolving it needs an independent outlet or the company's own statement, and neither has appeared in eight weeks.

3. DEEP-DIVE #25: TOMORROW BIO. THE QUEUED CONDITION CANNOT BE MET, AND THE REASON IS THE FINDING.

The condition set on 18 October was: find Tomorrow Bio's current US consumer price, state whether it is LIST or PROMOTIONAL, and record the cache age of the page it comes from.

THE ANSWER IS THAT THERE IS NO USD PRICE LIST. Tomorrow Bio publishes in euros only, and every dollar figure in circulation is a third-party conversion. They launched in the US in July 2024 with New York, California and Florida coverage, and more than two years later a US buyer still cannot read a price in their own currency on the company's own pages. That is a real competitive observation and it is the dive's result, not its failure.

A CORRECTION TO OUR OWN BASELINE, AND THE MECHANISM OF THE ERROR IS INSTRUCTIVE. This file has recorded since deep-dive #8 on 22 August that Tomorrow Bio membership is "$550/yr ($660 monthly; hardship rate €250/yr)." Their own membership page today reads €50/month and €500/year. A euro figure appears to have been converted to dollars and then written down as if it were the published price, with the hardship rate left in euros in the same sentence. The old line is printed here rather than deleted, per house practice. A converted figure is not a price, and a file that mixes the two cannot be used for a price comparison.

NEW TIER WE DID NOT HAVE, AND THEIR OWN SITE GIVES IT TWO DIFFERENT PRICES. A LIFETIME membership exists. The membership page lists it at €15,000 one-time. The membership-fees knowledge page and the cryonics-costs page both give €9,999 one-time. That is a 50 percent discrepancy across three pages of the same company's own website, and it is printed as a conflict rather than averaged, resolved or quietly reported at one value. lifetime membership, 9,999 and two months free were each near-zero in this file before today.

LIST OR PROMOTIONAL, answered properly. €50/month and €500/year are LIST. The annual plan is marketed as giving two months free, and the arithmetic confirms that is what it is rather than a discount on top: €50 × 12 is €600, and the annual price is €500. So the headline promotion is the annual price, not a reduction from it.

The "50 percent off membership for life" promo this file logged on 22 August does NOT appear in today's sweep. We cannot say whether it ended or simply is not in these results, and we are not going to guess.

Other stated inducements: the lifetime payment is credited toward the cryopreservation, which the company frames as making membership free; a 5 percent member discount on pet preservation; and a €30,000 member discount on whole-body against non-member pricing.

UNCHANGED AND THEREFORE A CONFIRMATION RATHER THAN A DELTA: procedure fees at legal death are €200,000 whole-body and €75,000 brain-only for members, €230,000 and €115,000 for non-members. Identical to our 22 August record.

THIRD-PARTY DOLLAR CONVERSIONS, both labelled. A comparison site estimates whole-body member pricing at about $220,000. A Substack analysis gives about $235,000, using a rate of roughly $1.18 per euro as of September 2025, which is thirteen months old. Neither is a Tomorrow Bio price and neither goes in any asset as one.

CACHE AGE AND ONE DISQUALIFICATION, which is the part the 18 October rule was written for. The sweep reported that some of the pages are marked as roughly a year old. And one of the returned results sits on a webflow.io staging host rather than tomorrow.bio: a price read off a staging or preview domain is not a published price and is excluded from everything above. The company's own calculator also states that its figures assume future inflation, which means a calculator output is a projection and not a price either.

SO WHAT FOR MYSPAWN. Counter unchanged and now better evidenced: they are the opposite end of the same instinct. Their entry is €500 a year plus a €200,000 commitment at death for a speculative revival bet; ours is $99 a year, billed annually, with the first 1,000 members locking the founding rate, for a certain record. "Not ready to bet six figures on being frozen? Start with the $99-a-year version of not disappearing." Never mock cryonicists. Adjacent believers are potential customers and we are the accessible first rung.

Threat: low commercially, unchanged. Narratively still high, and the US-expansion watch continues, but the absence of a USD price two years in suggests the US push is slower than the funding announcement implied.

STEAL, and it is a sharper version of the 22 August note: their finance calculator normalises a six-figure decision. A "what does your legacy stack actually cost per year" interactive would put $99 next to €500, $291 and $164 in one view, and every one of those numbers is now on file with a source.
