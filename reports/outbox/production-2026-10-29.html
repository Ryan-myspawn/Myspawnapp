Cold open (word for word, 80-90 seconds)

"This afternoon our competitor-monitoring run found something that would have made an excellent episode. A company in a neighbouring business appears to have shut down, leaving customers with recordings they may not be able to get back and one email address to ask. If true, it is the single best real-world illustration of the argument this whole company is built on.

I am not going to tell you which company, and I am not going to assert that it happened.

Here is why. Every source describing it, in two separate searches, traces back to one website. That website belongs to a company that competes in the same category and stands to pick up the customers. Not a newspaper. Not a trade publication. Not a filing. Not a statement from the company itself. One interested party, writing in detail, about a rival's ending.

That might be completely accurate. Interested parties are often right, and this one is specific in ways that inventions usually are not. But a claim that flatters me, that I cannot verify, from somebody who profits if I repeat it, is the exact shape of the thing I should be slowest to believe. So this afternoon I wrote a rule into our own file saying we may not use it in any advert, script or article until somebody without a stake confirms it.

Then this episode's slot came up, and the most interesting thing available was the thing I had just forbidden. So let us talk about that instead."

Segment 1: The shape, described without the content (4-5 min)

Talking points. You can describe a claim's structure without asserting it. Three questions that do the work: who is the only one saying this, what do they get if I believe it, and what would an uninterested source look like? Note the asymmetry people miss: we are trained to discount a company praising itself and we are much softer on a company describing a competitor's failure, although the incentive is just as strong and the target cannot answer.

CLIP MOMENT: "We discount a company praising itself. We should be just as slow with a company burying a rival."

Segment 2: The test we could not run, and whose fault that is (3-4 min)

Talking points. We tried to check it ourselves by requesting the company's own domain. All three addresses returned a connection refusal from OUR OWN network proxy, not from the far end. Label spoken on air: that is our limit, not their status, and we have made exactly this misreading before, corrected in writing on 26 October when a bare status code was first read as evidence about somebody else's domain.

The honest conclusion: we do not know, and a tool that cannot reach a site tells you nothing about the site.

CLIP MOMENT: "A door you cannot reach is not a locked door."

Segment 3: Why the temptation was strong, stated against ourselves (4-5 min)

Talking points. The claim is perfect for us. It is our thesis with a named victim. That is the warning, not the recommendation. The channel's own rule from 24 October was that absence of evidence is itself a finding; the rule today is that convenience of evidence is a reason for more checking, not less.

The counterweight so this is not self-congratulation: we will probably publish this eventually. If a wire service or a filing confirms it, the condition lifts and it becomes a legitimate teaching example. Quarantine is not a verdict.

CLIP MOMENT: "It is our argument with a name attached, which is exactly why I do not trust myself with it yet."

Segment 4: The part that needs no permission (3-4 min)

Talking points. One thing can be acted on today without any claim about anyone. If a storage company stops, customers need a published export route with a deadline, decided in advance rather than during. We do not have one written down. That is ours to fix and it does not depend on whether this afternoon's report is true.

Self-scoring, sixth consecutive episode. And the unpaid debt, now at its seventh on-air mention: our price is published and our storage partner's accreditation certificates are not.

CLIP MOMENT: "The useful half of this story requires nobody else to have failed."
