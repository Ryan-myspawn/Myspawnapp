THREADS POSTS

THREADS 1.

A number I want to show you, carefully, because it is easy to get wrong.

Survival for infants born at 22 weeks of gestation is reported very differently in different countries. One paper citing country-level figures puts it at roughly 0.7 per cent in France, 2 per cent in the United Kingdom, 5 per cent in the United States, 10 per cent in Sweden and 33 per cent in Japan.

That is a forty-fold spread in wealthy countries with good hospitals, in the same decade.

Some of it is measurement. But a survey of treatment practice found that differences between hospitals in how often active treatment was offered explained a large share of the variation in survival.

Which means part of "how many survive at 22 weeks" is a measurement of what clinicians decided to attempt, not only of what the infant could do.

I am not qualified to say which practice is right and I am not going to pretend otherwise. The transferable point is narrower and it holds everywhere: when you are given a percentage about an outcome, ask what share of it is measuring the thing and what share is measuring the decision to attempt the thing.

THREADS 2.

We published something this week that criticises us.

The phrase "chain of custody" appears in six of our own articles. In every one of them it is used as a selling point, or as a thing cheaper options lack. Not once, until this week, did we say what it actually is.

So: it is a chronological record of who had control of a sample, when, and what they did. The draft of the biobanking standard says such a record "can help assess" the integrity of the material. Can help assess. Not ensures, not proves.

Which means a perfect chain of custody can document, in immaculate detail, the complete ruin of a sample. It would tell you exactly who had it when the freezer failed. That is genuinely valuable and it is not the same as the sample surviving.

We are a storage company, ninety-nine dollars a year, billed annually. We used a phrase for months without defining it, and defining it makes our own claim smaller and more checkable. That seems like the right trade.
