A result surfaced claiming that "as of this week in Montana, any biotech company with an experimental drug has a clear path to selling it to consumers." Undated in the index.

WHAT THE LAW ACTUALLY DOES, with the limits attached. It creates the first US state licensing framework for facilities to recommend and sell therapies not approved by the FDA. Eligibility is limited to drugs that have completed at least one Phase I trial. Licences are annual. Patients must give written consent listing approved alternatives and the worst potential outcome, and centres must allocate 2 per cent of annual profits to low-income patient access. Critics note the obvious: a Phase I trial tests safety, not whether the drug works.

TWO THREADS POSTS

Threads 1:

Something changed in American medicine this year and almost nobody outside the field noticed.

Montana now has a licensing framework for clinics to recommend and sell therapies the FDA has not approved. It was signed in May 2025 and the rules took effect at the end of July this year. Coverage from then said the first clinic would likely be running around the end of the year, which is now about seven weeks away.

The eligibility bar is the part worth holding onto. A drug qualifies if it has completed at least one Phase I trial.

Phase I asks whether a dose is safe enough to continue studying. It does not ask whether the drug works. That is what the later phases are for, and those are the ones that most candidate drugs fail.

So the honest description of what is being made available is: things that cleared the safety gate and have not yet been shown to do anything.

There are real safeguards written in. Written consent listing approved alternatives and the worst plausible outcome. Annual licences. Two per cent of profits directed to low-income access. Those are not nothing.

And I should say the obvious thing about my own position. We sell storage of a DNA record. Storing one gives you no eligibility, no priority and no access to any of this, in Montana or anywhere else. If anyone in our category implies otherwise, including us, that is the sentence to check.

Threads 2:

If you are estranged from family, there is one document that matters more than your will, and it is not the one people tell you to write.

Most US states let you name, in writing, the person who will have authority over your body and funeral arrangements. It goes by names like Designation of Agent for Final Disposition. In some states the authority is folded into a health care proxy or power of attorney instead.

Without it, the decision goes to a ranked list of relatives set by state law. The order varies. The person at the top may well be the person you have not spoken to in a decade.

The formalities are specific and they are not decorative. New York wants a written instrument and says a handwritten note is not enough. Washington wants it signed and dated in front of a witness. Rhode Island wants a notarized original.

One honest limit, because the people selling forms rarely mention it. Your agent is not obliged anywhere to carry out wishes that are impractical, illegal or financially burdensome. The designation assigns authority. It does not create an order anyone must obey.

It is still the only lever you have, and it takes an afternoon.
