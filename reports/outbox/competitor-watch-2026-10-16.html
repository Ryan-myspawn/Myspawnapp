BONUS DIVE: CRYO-CELL, and the control did not hold

THE ROTATION SET THIS JOB IN WRITING YESTERDAY: "re-verify the $710 entry and $199 a year, because a control that is never re-tested is not a control."

ONE HALF HELD PERFECTLY AND ONE HALF DID NOT.

THE RECURRING FEE IS EXACTLY WHERE WE LEFT IT. Cryo-Cell's own enrollment page gives storage at $199 per year, with the standard caveat that it is subject to increase up to the then-current rate for new clients. Its pricing page carries the same $199 for both the standard and the premium cord blood service, and cord tissue storage is a separate $199 a year. (Company's own pages, read via search index; cryo-cell.com was not opened from this machine.) That matches our 30 August verification and the 28 September re-check exactly. Seven weeks, no drift on the annual line.

THE ENTRY FIGURE IS NO LONGER WHAT A BUYER SEES. Our baseline says "from $710 entry". Today's pages give:

  • Standard cord blood, retail first year: $1,685, processing, testing and first-year storage included.
  • Standard cord blood on a current October offer: from $885.
  • Premium: $2,035 retail, $1,235 on the same offer.
  • 18-year prepaid, cord blood only: $5,068.
  • A third-party comparison separately lists a $895 collection and processing fee described as based on 2025 prices.

THE DRIFT IS IN THE PROMOTIONAL LAYER, NOT THE LIST PRICE, AND THAT DISTINCTION IS THE FINDING. The recurring fee is stable to the dollar across seven weeks. The entry number swings by eight hundred dollars depending on whether you catch an offer, and our file recorded one number without recording which kind it was.

SO THE DIVE PRODUCED A METHODOLOGY FAULT IN OUR OWN RECORDS RATHER THAN A COMPETITIVE EVENT, which is the second time in two days. Yesterday a rotation note mis-stated the age of our own Cryonics Institute figure by a factor of forty. Today a baseline entry carries a competitor's entry price with no indication of whether it was a list price or a live promotion.

BINDING FROM TODAY: every competitor price recorded in baseline.md must state whether it is a LIST price or a PROMOTIONAL price, and carry the date it was observed. A promotional price captured without that label becomes a false constant the moment the promotion ends, and it then sits in our files looking exactly like a verified fact.

THE 30 AUGUST $710 IS NOT BEING CALLED WRONG, because we cannot establish what it was. It may have been an offer price, a component fee, or a figure that has since moved. What is established is that it is not the current entry price and that our record does not say which kind of number it ever was. Corrected in the baseline as superseded rather than deleted.
