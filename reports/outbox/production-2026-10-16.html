2. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER

"Nobody In This Category Sells You A Price. They Sell You A Structure."

Freshness check: this angle has not been used in the last 7 days. endowment, dues, annual structure and certainty return ZERO across production-log.md.

DISCLOSED AND DIFFERENTIATED, because the nearest neighbour is close and this is exactly where a duplicate gets made. An episode called "How Many Prices Does It Have?" already exists and its axis is how many prices ONE service displays. This explainer's axis is how many price STRUCTURES exist ACROSS a category, and what each structure actually charges you for. One is a count inside a single pricing page. This is a taxonomy across four companies. Also disclosed: the 15 October Content Factory Short "They Tell You Which One Is Worse" carries ONE of these companies' arithmetic in 42 seconds and makes no comparison at all. This carries three and names the pattern.

Primary audience: Men 25-55, estate-planner end

Trend / hook used: Our own competitor dives of 14, 15 and 16 October, which answered the same question for three different companies in three days and got three incompatible answers.

Length: 9 to 10 minutes.

Script

[COLD OPEN. Locked-off shot, no B-roll yet.]

VO: "Over three days this week we asked three companies in the same adjacent category one boring question. Not what does it cost. What does the recurring part actually pay for.

We got three different answers, and none of them is the answer you would guess from the headline price. I want to show you the three, because once you can see the shape, you can read any of these companies in about ninety seconds."

[B-ROLL: a plain printed page on a desk, slow push. No logos, no brand marks, no company names on screen at any point.]

SEGMENT 1 (2 min): The structure that charges you for waiting.

VO: "Company one indexes its annual dues to your current age. Older when you join, more per year, every year. On top of that there is a separate annual fee for a standby service.

Read that structure honestly and it is not a membership fee. It is a price on the interval between now and the thing you are paying for. The longer you might wait, the more the waiting costs."

[ON SCREEN: Structure 1. You pay for the waiting.]

[B-ROLL: a wall calendar, pages still.] [LABEL: company-sourced figures, read via a search index, not opened at source.]

SEGMENT 2 (2 min): The structure that charges you for not committing.

VO: "Company two offers two ways in. One payment, or a yearly one. The yearly one looks cheaper and it is the one most people would pick.

Their own published material says that if you switch from the yearly route to the one-off later, you save seven thousand dollars. Which means the cheaper-looking option costs seven thousand more at the end.

That is not a fee for a service. It is a price on indecision, and the remarkable part is that they wrote it down themselves, on their own site, where anybody can read it."

[CLIP MOMENT: "The cheaper-looking option costs seven thousand more at the end, and they published that themselves."]

[ON SCREEN: Structure 2. You pay for not deciding.]

SEGMENT 3 (2 min): The structure that charges you for certainty.

VO: "Company three, different product entirely, sells an eighteen-year prepaid plan. We did the arithmetic. The prepaid plan costs more than paying annually for eighteen years would.

Not a discount. A premium, for knowing the number will not move. And that is a real product. People buy fixed-rate mortgages for the same reason. But it should be bought with open eyes, because almost everybody assumes prepaying is a saving."

[ON SCREEN: Structure 3. You pay for certainty, not for savings.]

[B-ROLL: a long receipt curling off a desk. No text legible.]

SEGMENT 4 (2 min): Now read ours.

VO: "Here is where I have to turn this on us, because the whole piece is worthless otherwise.

MySpawn is ninety-nine dollars a year, billed annually, and the first thousand members lock the founding rate. So which structure is that?

It is the third one. We are selling certainty, not a saving. The founding-rate lock is a promise that the number does not move for you, and it is worth exactly as much as our ability to keep that promise and nothing more.

And I will give you the limit that cuts against us: over a long enough life, an annual fee costs more than a single payment does. Anyone comparing us to a one-time product should do that arithmetic with a time horizon in it, and if nobody gives you the horizon, the comparison is marketing."

[ON SCREEN: $99/year, billed annually. Structure 3.]

SEGMENT 5, close (1 min): The ninety-second test.

VO: "So the test, for any of them, including us.

Find the recurring line. Ask what it pays for. If the answer is 'the waiting', expect it to rise. If the answer is 'not deciding', decide. If the answer is 'certainty', ask who guarantees it and for how long.

And if a company cannot tell you which of the three it is, that is your answer about the company rather than about the price."

[B-ROLL: the printed page again, now face down.]

Peer-review and sourcing labels, read ON AIR in segment 1 and repeated in the end card

"Every figure in this video comes from the companies' own published pages, read through a search index rather than opened at their own domains. No company is named on screen, and nothing here is a claim about whether any of their products works."

Ship-blockers, four

1. NO COMPANY IS NAMED, on screen or in the voiceover, in any segment. The piece is a taxonomy and naming turns admiration into comparison without consent.

2. VERIFY ALL THREE STRUCTURES BEFORE RECORDING. None of the three pages was opened at its own domain. Our own competitor file found yesterday that one of these companies' entry prices had drifted by eight hundred dollars while we were treating it as stable, so this is not a formality.

3. Segment 4's limit line is not optional. Remove it and the piece becomes an advert that pretends to be an analysis.

3. EVERGREEN BANK

"The Vending Machine"

Freshness check: vending returns ZERO across production-log.md and every scripts file. The last eight evergreens were a split screen, a reflection in dark glass, two pairs of hands, a printer, a fuse box, a strike-through list, a two-column fill (killed) and a metronome. This is none of them, and it is deliberately NOT another precision instrument, because the metronome was banked yesterday and two timekeeping-adjacent machines in two days is a family repeat.

Primary audience: Men 25-55

Trend / hook used: None, and no claim. An object piece with no person and no voiceover.

Hook (first 2 seconds): A coin goes in. The sound of the mechanism accepting it.

Beats:

1. Open on the coin slot. The transaction works perfectly. The machine is not broken.

2. The coil turns. One full rotation, exactly as designed.

3. The item moves forward, tilts, and hangs. It does not drop.

4. Hold. Four full seconds on an item suspended behind glass, paid for and not delivered.

5. One line of text, held, then gone: "Everything worked. Nothing arrived."

6. Black. No price. No CTA. No URL.

Subject: the difference between a system functioning and a system delivering. Every failure this channel has documented this month is this shot: the switch fired and the credential was stale, the accreditation held and the sample was never transferable, the mirror ran and the folder stayed empty.

Ship-blockers, three: (1) a real vending machine and a real hang, staged with a genuinely stuck item rather than cut to imply one, because a faked mechanical failure is a lie about a machine. (2) No hand, no face, no reflection of a person in the glass at any point. (3) No brand marks or product labels legible behind the glass, which on a real vending machine takes a specific choice of machine rather than a specific crop.
