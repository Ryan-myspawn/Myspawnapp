The one thing worth your attention tonight

UK Biobank's Research Analysis Platform has been closed to researchers since April 2026, and we had UK Biobank on file three times without knowing it.

Dated honestly, because the honest version is better than the dramatic one: the incident is April, not tonight. Deidentified participant-level data that had already been exported from their platform was found offered for sale on Chinese e-commerce. They paused researcher access. What is in-window is the phased reopening that began this month.

The useful part is what they decided the fix was. Not a stronger front door. An airlock and an output checking system. At reopening researchers can go in and compute and download nothing at all. A manual output checker arrives later in 2026, an automated one is targeted for early 2027. Every affected project got a six-month extension. A GBP 200,000 independent review was commissioned and its recommendations accepted.

That is the missing half of every digital-legacy question in our archive. We have spent a month teaching people to ask who can get in: legacy contact, access key, death certificate, inactive-account timeout. Almost nobody asks what can leave, who approves it, and whether there is a record. Tonight's script is built on that distinction and nothing else.

Three things the script deliberately refuses to do: name the institution on camera; state the widely quoted 500,000-participant figure, which is security-press reporting rather than UK Biobank's own statement; and assert how the data got out, which we do not know.
