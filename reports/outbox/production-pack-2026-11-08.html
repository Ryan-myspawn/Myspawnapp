SOURCE BASIS AND ITS LIMIT, STATED ON AIR IN THE FIRST MINUTE. Everything in this explainer comes from a sweep of publicly retrievable writing about two cryonics organisations, read through a search index, with no source opened at its own domain. The script's argument does not depend on any of the figures being right. It depends on them disagreeing, which is observable from the outside.

Script

[COLD OPEN. B-roll: a plain table, nothing on it.]

"I am going to give you four prices for the same service, from four sources, all of them public. Then I am going to tell you why the disagreement matters more than any of the numbers."

[B-roll: four cards laid down one at a time, each with a figure.]

"A company that preserves bodies after death. One source puts its whole-body price at two hundred and twenty-two thousand, six hundred and three dollars, plus fifty-five dollars a month. A second puts it at two hundred thousand euros, with a brain-only option at seventy-five thousand. A third gives the same two hundred thousand but puts brain-only at sixty thousand. A fourth quotes monthly dues that a fifth says were doubled in twenty twenty-four."

[Hold on the four cards.]

"I cannot tell you which is correct. Neither can you, and that is the finding."

[B-roll: hands squaring the cards into a stack.]

"The second organisation is easier and still not easy. Two hundred and twenty thousand whole body, eighty thousand for the brain. Annual dues that one source puts near seven hundred dollars for an adult, and sixty dollars for a child. Membership counts that three separate sources give as one thousand four hundred and forty-two, about one thousand five hundred, and about one thousand six hundred."

[B-roll: a pen, unused, beside the stack.]

"None of this is an accusation. Currencies move. Prices change. Aggregators go stale and nobody updates them. A company can publish one clear page and still be described four different ways by four different writers. I am not saying anybody lied. I am saying that from the outside, the public record does not resolve to a number."

[LABEL CARD, on screen and spoken.] "Everything I just read is index-sourced. I did not open these companies' own pages. If I had, I might have found one clean figure and this would be a different video."

[B-roll: the four cards fanned out again.]

"So here is the practical part, and it transfers to anything you are considering buying that you will hold for a long time.

One. Find the number on the seller's own page, not on a comparison site. A comparison site is a secondary source about a price, and prices are exactly the kind of fact that goes stale invisibly.

Two. Ask what recurs. Three of the four sources quote a headline figure. The recurring item, the monthly or annual membership, is where the long-term cost actually lives, and it is the number most likely to be missing.

Three. Ask when the recurring item last changed. One source records dues doubling in a single step. A price that has moved once will move again, and a seller who will tell you the history is telling you something a current price cannot.

Four, and this is the one people skip. Ask what the price is for. Whole body and brain-only are not two tiers of one product. They are different things with a single word between them."

[B-roll: a single card, laid down alone.]

"Now the part where I have to be fair to the companies I just spent six minutes on.

Every criticism I have made is a criticism of the public record, not of their conduct. The disagreement sits in the writing about them. And I should hold us to the same test.

We sell storage of a DNA record. The price is ninety-nine dollars a year, billed annually, and the first thousand members lock that founding rate. One number, one page, one recurring item, and the recurring item is the whole price.

That is not a boast, it is a hostage. If somebody ran this same sweep on us and found four different figures on four aggregators, every argument in this video would belong to them instead. We have given you the test. Use it on us."

[FINAL CARD.] "Find the seller's own page. Ask what recurs. Ask when it last moved. Ask what it covers."

B-ROLL SUMMARY: a bare table, four cards laid and re-laid, a stack, an unused pen, a single card. No people, no stock footage of laboratories, no money.

EPISTEMIC LABELS SPOKEN, not printed: index-sourced, no source opened at its own domain, no accusation of misconduct, and the explicit statement that opening the companies' own pages might produce a clean figure and a different video.

PRESERVE-NOW THESIS CLOSE: deliberately absent. There is no preserve-now argument in this piece and inserting one would undo it. The close is a test the viewer is invited to run on us.
