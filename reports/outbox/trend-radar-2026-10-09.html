Science Watch

THE ITEM OF THE DAY IS A CONTRADICTION, AND THE HONEST MOVE IS TO REPORT THE WHOLE SHAPE OF IT RATHER THAN THE HALF THAT SUITS A HEADLINE.

(1) THE NEW FINDING. Frontiers in Endocrinology, reported 7 October 2026: semen and urine from 179 men at an assisted reproductive technology clinic in Jinan, China. Higher neonicotinoid levels in semen associated with lower sperm concentration; top exposure quartile versus bottom, a 27 percent difference. The analysis looked at mixtures of insecticides rather than one chemical at a time, which is the methodologically interesting part. (Peer-reviewed. Read via The New Lede coverage, 7 October 2026; the paper itself was not opened. Clinic population, not a general-population sample.)

(2) AND IN THE SAME SWEEP, THREE STUDIES POINTING THE OTHER WAY.

  • A 2026 Beijing study of sperm donors: concentration up 12.3 percent and total sperm count up 18.7 percent from 2011 to 2018.
  • An Irish single-centre review: concentration up by nearly 23 percent over its study period, whose authors call the increase unexpected and say it contrasts with widely cited reports of global decline.
  • A 2025 meta-analysis of fertile American men: no statistically significant change in sperm concentration.

(All peer-reviewed, all read via abstracts and coverage, none opened at its own domain.)

SO WHAT, and this is the preserve-now angle that does not require us to pick a side: the decline narrative is the thing everybody has heard, and the recent single-population evidence is genuinely mixed. We are a storage company. We do not fix fertility, we do not have a position on whether counts are falling, and we are not going to sell against a number that four studies cannot agree on. What we can say is narrower and true: a biological record taken today is a record of today, whatever the trend turns out to be.
