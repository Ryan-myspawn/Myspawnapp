THE NEAR PRIOR, named because it is close: 30 September ran "How Many Prices Does It Have?", whose axis is the COUNT of prices a service displays, with the RSA headline "One Price. No Tiers." That is a question about tiers. This week's axis is whether a seller's own surfaces AGREE WITH EACH OTHER, which is a question about consistency and not about count.** A service can have exactly one price and still publish it three different ways.

Primary audience: Men 25-55. M1 leads at 25-40 future-optionality. The 40-60 and childfree lanes split from the same axis.

Trend-hook used: this morning's Competitor Watch, nine hours old. A company in our category publishes a machine-readable page, a provider page and an FAQ whose starting prices do not agree with one another. NO COMPANY IS NAMED IN ANY ASSET IN THIS BATCH, and the finding is used as a PROCEDURE the reader can run on anybody, including on us.

The angle

"Ask It, Then Check It"

AXIS: SURFACE CONSISTENCY, INCLUDING THE SURFACE WRITTEN FOR MACHINES.

THE COMMERCIAL ARGUMENT, in one paragraph. People increasingly ask an assistant what something costs before they ever reach a website, and assistants read the page a company publishes for machines. If that page disagrees with the human page, a buyer is quoted a number the seller may not honour, and neither party did anything dishonest to get there. Our entire commercial offer is one figure, annually, with nothing to prepay and nothing to compare. That is not a boast about being cheap. It is a claim that the offer is small enough to be stated identically everywhere, and it is checkable in under a minute by anyone who doubts it.

WHY IT SELLS THE $99 A YEAR RATHER THAN MERELY ENTERTAINING. Every asset in this batch invites the reader to verify a number instead of asking them to trust one. The conversion event is a reader who opens our pricing page to catch us out and finds nothing to catch. That is the cheapest kind of trust to earn and the only kind that survives a second visit.

  • Longer variant: Ask an AI what DNA storage costs. Then open the company's own page. Same number? Ours is one figure: $99 a year, billed annually, nothing to prepay, no tiers to compare. Run the check on us. It takes a minute, and a minute is the whole point.

So I have not read our pricing page, our FAQ, or any machine-readable file we may or may not publish. BINDING RELEASE CONDITION: before any asset in this batch runs, confirm by eye that the pricing page, the FAQ and any llms.txt or equivalent all state $99 per year, billed annually, with no second figure anywhere. 2. "The same figure on every page we publish" is a structural commitment and not only a price claim. It is in the same class as 30 September's "One price, no tiers, nothing to prepay", which was flagged then for the same reason. It is true only for as long as the pages agree, and it becomes false the moment a promotion, a tier or a bundle is introduced on one surface. Whoever adds one must retire this copy on the same day.

LAUNCH-FIRST RECOMMENDATION

M1 at $10 a day, and this week the recommendation is not the cautious one, which is a departure. The last two batches both led with M2 on the grounds that it needed no news peg and no third-party figure. M1 this week needs neither either.
