Cold open, word for word (about 85 seconds)

You have seen the picture. A baby in a clear pod, tubes going in, a row of them down a bright corridor. It has been shared tens of millions of times.
That picture is a CGI concept. It is called EctoLife, it was made by a filmmaker in 2022, and no such facility exists. The other one you may have seen, the Chinese "pregnancy robot", was an AI-generated hoax and has been debunked.
I am starting there because I want the next number to land properly.
Here is what the actual frontier looked like earlier this year, reported in May. Some researchers working on artificial uteruses are not trying to gestate anything yet. They want to use the technology to study implantation, the step where an embryo attaches, which is where an enormous number of pregnancies quietly end and which almost nobody can observe.
And one device, for injecting embryos into the uterine lining: fewer than ten women underwent the procedure. None of them became pregnant.
That is not a scandal. That is an early trial, doing what early trials do.
But hold those two images side by side, because the distance between them is where every bad decision in this category gets made. On one side, a render with a corridor full of pods. On the other, fewer than ten people and a result of zero.

[CLIP MOMENT] "Fewer than ten women. None of them became pregnant. That is not a scandal, that is an early trial, and the fact that it sounds like a scandal is a problem with how we read science."

2. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER (target 8 minutes)

Title: "The People Closest To Lab-Grown Eggs And Sperm Keep Saying The Same Thing"

FROM THE BACK CATALOGUE, PER THIS PACK'S OWN RULE: the source is from February 2026, roughly eight months old.

[0:00 to 0:25] COLD OPEN. [B-ROLL: a lab corridor at night, no people, no logos.]

Every few months a headline says lab-grown human eggs or sperm are close. And every few months, somebody who actually works on it publishes a sentence saying they are not.
Here is the one from this year, with its address, so you can go and read it instead of me.

[0:25 to 1:40] THE SOURCE, LABELLED PRECISELY. [ON SCREEN TEXT held: Nature Biotechnology, 26 February 2026, Vol 44 Issue 4, DOI 10.1038/s41587-026-03034-2]

**A Comment in Nature Biotechnology, published the twenty-sixth of February 2026, volume 44, issue 4, titled "Considerations for the future of in vitro gametogenesis in fertility care." The authors are Hannah Landecker and Amander Clark, both at UCLA.**
A Comment is not a research paper. It is a piece of expert argument, and I am telling you that rather than letting the word "Nature" do work it has not earned here.
What it says, in the authors' framing: in vitro gametogenesis is NOT yet feasible for human reproduction, and substantial hurdles must be overcome before it becomes a clinical reality.

[1:40 to 3:30] WHAT THE TECHNOLOGY CAN ACTUALLY DO TODAY. [B-ROLL: slow macro of a pipette tip; no stock "science" montage.]

This is the part that gets lost, and it is one word.
What stem-cell-based IVG can currently produce is IMMATURE cells: oogonia and pro-spermatogonia. Precursors. Not an egg you could fertilise. Not a sperm that could fertilise one.
The authors also flag low gamete and embryo quality as a limitation, which matters because "we made a cell" and "we made a cell that works" are different sentences, and only one of them has been demonstrated.

[3:30 to 5:00] WHY THE LIMIT KEEPS BEING STATED BY INSIDERS. [ON SCREEN TEXT: "The limit is being stated by the people with the most to gain from it not being true."]

Ask who benefits from the hype version and the answer is obvious. Ask who benefits from the limit, and it is less obvious, which is why it is worth noticing that the limit keeps coming from inside.
A limit stated by someone with no stake in your purchase is worth more than the same limit stated by a company selling you something. We sell storage, so when we say IVG has produced no mature human gametes, you should weight that less than when they say it.
The same Comment argues for public engagement in shaping where this research goes, which is a thing researchers ask for when they expect the public to be surprised later.

[5:00 to 6:30] THE ONE RESULT PEOPLE MISREAD. [B-ROLL: a freezer door closing.]

Around the first of July this year, a company reported generating early human egg cells from stem cells. Primary oocytes. Immature. They cannot be fertilised.
That is a real result and it is consistent with everything above. It is not a lab-grown baby and it is not a lab-grown egg in the sense you are picturing.
Reported timelines from scientists quoted in September coverage run from several years to a decade for usable human IVG eggs. Estimates are estimates, and a decade-long estimate has been wrong in both directions in this field before.

[6:30 to 8:00] PRESERVE-NOW CLOSE, WITHOUT OVERPROMISING. [B-ROLL: wide static shot, empty room, no music swell.]

So what does a man in his thirties or forties do with this?
Nothing, in the direction you might expect. Nobody should bank anything today because of IVG. The technology does not exist, and we would be lying if we implied our product was a ticket to it.
MySpawn stores a DNA record. Ninety-nine dollars a year, billed annually, at GenVault in New Jersey. Their accreditations, not ours. We do not create descendants, and nobody can.
The only honest reason to keep a biological record is the boring one, and it has not changed in a century: you cannot go back and collect it later.
