## THREE X / TWITTER DRAFTS

**X1, announced versus shown.**
> A company announced a nine-year study's results this week. The results are being presented on Thursday.
>
> Not a scandal. Conferences work that way.
>
> But the headline exists three days before the finding does, and the sentence that forms in those three days is the one that sticks.
>
> Three questions, and they cost nothing: has it been presented yet. Can anyone outside check the data. Is this a paper or a talk.
>
> None of those is an accusation. They are the difference between announced and shown.

**X2, the two prices.** **NO COMPANY NAMED AND NO FIGURE QUOTED, deliberately.**
> Something I keep finding in this category, and it is not a scandal either.
>
> There is the price a company shows you and there is the price it also has. Sometimes they differ by more than double.
>
> Neither is a lie. One is a promotion and one is a standard rate, and which one lands in front of you is a choice somebody made.
>
> We have one number. Ninety-nine dollars a year, billed annually. There is no second one, and I only noticed that was unusual by going and looking at other people's ladders.

**X3, four words.**
> Cleared. Approved. Investigational. Exempt.
>
> Cleared means a device resembles something already on the market. Approved means somebody independently assessed whether it works. Investigational means permission to begin. Exempt means a rule does not apply here.
>
> A company will use one of those this week and a headline will use a different one.
>
> Save this. It is the cheapest media literacy in health tech.

## TWO THREADS POSTS

**T1, the practical one.**
> Twenty minutes, and most people will not do it.
>
> Find the research-participation setting on any DNA account you opened years ago and look at what it says right now, not what you remember choosing.
>
> Then ask, separately and in writing, what happens to the physical sample if you delete the account. Data deletion and sample destruction are two different operations with two different policies.
>
> Companies email you about new features. None of them emails to ask whether you would like your original tube discarded.
>
> How many of your old accounts could you even name?

**T2, the one that changes how you read the word.**
> "De-identified" is a procedural label, not a physical property.
>
> A genome is identifying by construction. It is the most durable identifier a person has, and it is shared in predictable proportions with relatives who never consented to anything.
>
> That is not an accusation against any company. It is a statement about the molecule.
>
> Worth remembering every time you read that something was anonymized.
