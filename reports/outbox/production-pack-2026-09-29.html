**Primary audience:** Men 25-55.
**Trend-hook used:** two sources we hold give two different dates for one company's FDA clearance, and the likeliest explanation is that they are describing two different events.
**THIS EXPLAINER WAS BANKED TWICE AND IS NOW UNBLOCKED.** Killed on 28 September for register collision with that day's episode, and again this morning against the governance episode. **That episode is dead, so the collision is gone.** **Twice-blocked by its own topicality is a scheduling problem, not a quality problem**, and the Daily Review addresses it.

**THESIS:** four different FDA words describe four different things, the gap between them is where most health-tech headlines go wrong, and **you can use the difference without knowing any biology.**

**B-ROLL AND STAGING.** Locked-off desk, four index cards laid down one at a time, nothing else in frame. **No FDA logos, no agency buildings, no stock lab footage**: this is a vocabulary piece and borrowed imagery would imply authority we do not have.

**SCRIPT BEATS.**

**Open (0:00 to 0:45).** "Four words. Cleared. Approved. Investigational. Exempt. A company will use one of them in a press release this week, and a headline will use a different one, and most of you will not notice." **CARD 1 goes down.**

**Beat 1, clearance (0:45 to 2:15).** **510(k) clearance is for moderate-risk devices** and the standard is **substantial equivalence to a predicate device already on the market.** **It is not a finding that the thing works.** It is a finding that it is close enough to something already out there. Reported review times run roughly **30 to 90 days.**
**ON SCREEN, and say it out loud: "CLEARED IS NOT APPROVED."**

**Beat 2, approval (2:15 to 3:45).** **Premarket approval, PMA, is for high-risk or genuinely novel devices**, and the standard is independent: **reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness, supported by valid scientific evidence, usually including clinical investigation data.** Longer review, higher evidentiary bar, no predicate to lean on.
**The one-line version: clearance compares. Approval evaluates.**

**Beat 3, permission to try (3:45 to 5:15).** **An IND is a request for authorization to give an investigational drug or biological product to humans.** **An IDE (21 CFR Part 812) lets an investigational DEVICE be used in a clinical study** to gather the data a future 510(k) or PMA would need.
**Neither is a verdict on anything. Both are permission to start.** **A company announcing an IND or an IDE has announced a beginning, and beginnings get reported as arrivals constantly.**

**Beat 4, the worked example, and it is ours (5:15 to 7:00).** We carry a fact with two dates on it. **One source describes a company's FDA clearance for a cellular-rejuvenation program as announced in 2025 with first-in-human trials planned for early 2026. Another describes the company receiving an FDA go-ahead in February 2026.**
**We have logged it CONTESTED and we do not quote either date.** But now you can see the likelier explanation: **those may not be two reports of one event. They may be two events**, a year and a different pathway apart. **The vocabulary is the resolution.**
**NOT IN DISPUTE and stated on camera: our own published work says the first patient in that program was dosed on 9 June 2026. The dosing date is not what is contested.**
**EPISTEMIC LABEL, ON SCREEN: neither date is asserted. The company is not named. This is a worked example of a confusion, not a claim about anybody.**

**Beat 5, the four-question habit (7:00 to 8:15).** Which pathway. Cleared or approved. Is this permission to start or a result. And **what is the date of the event, not the date of the article.**

**Beat 6, preserve-now close (8:15 to 9:00).** **No overpromise available here and none attempted.** MySpawn is not a regulated medical product, makes no medical claim, and **storage confers no health benefit.** The connection is one sentence: **the vocabulary that separates a permission from a result is the same skill that separates a stored record from a promise about the future.** We keep a readable copy of a genome at an accredited New Jersey biorepository. **$99 a year, billed annually. First thousand members lock the founding rate.**

**HONEST LABELING BLOCK, read on camera at the end.** "Everything in this video about FDA pathways comes from regulatory-industry summaries and university research-office guides, **not from the agency's own pages, which I could not open from my network today.** The structure is standard and uncontroversial. **The specific review timelines vary by product and year, so treat the numbers as typical rather than exact**, and check with a regulatory professional before relying on any of it."
