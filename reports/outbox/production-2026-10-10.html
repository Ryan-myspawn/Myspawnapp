THE ADJACENCY, DISCLOSED UP FRONT

On 9 September a script listed "who physically holds it, you or an independent accredited facility?" as one of four audit questions. That is one line in a checklist. This is twenty minutes on what the answer usually is, and why the honest version implicates us too.

COLD OPEN (word for word, ~85 seconds)

"Here is something I learned about my own industry this week, and I am going to tell it against myself.

I went looking at how a large cord blood bank actually operates in the United States. Good company, long history, proper accreditations. And what I found is that their American arm does not run the laboratory. They contract the processing out, to two separate named facilities in two different states.

My first instinct was that this is a story. My second instinct, which took about ninety seconds longer, was that it is exactly what we do.

MySpawn does not own a building full of freezers. A company called GenVault holds your sample, in New Jersey. We are a brand and they are a facility, and if that arrangement is a scandal when somebody else does it, it is a scandal when we do it.

It is not a scandal. It is the industry. What varies is whether anybody tells you."

SEGMENT 1 (~5 min): The three layers almost nobody separates

  • The brand you bought from. The broker or operator in the middle. The building where the material physically sits. Three layers, often three different companies, sometimes three different states.
  • The example, described without naming the company, because the point is the structure and not the firm: a US arm headquartered in one state, contracting processing to a facility in Indiana and another in New Jersey, both FDA-registered and accredited.
  • Say plainly: contract processing is normal, and specialist facilities are often better at this than a brand would be. The argument is not vertical integration. It never was.
  • CLIP MOMENT: "You did not buy a freezer. You bought a relationship with a company that has a relationship with a freezer."

SEGMENT 2 (~5 min): Why the layers matter when something goes wrong

  • Three concrete failure points, none hypothetical in kind: the brand is acquired or folds; the contract between brand and facility ends; the facility itself changes hands. Your material does not move in any of those events. The paperwork around it does, and the paperwork is what you can act on.
  • The question that actually tests a company, and it is four words: who physically holds it. The follow-up is harder: what happens to my sample if your contract with them ends.
  • Against interest, out loud: we have published that question three times this month and our own custody page still does not exist. Name it on air as a debt rather than letting a listener find it.
  • CLIP MOMENT: "If the company that sold it to you disappears on a Tuesday, the sample does not move. The question is whether anybody still knows whose it is."

SEGMENT 3 (~4 min): Accreditation is not one thing

  • AABB, CAP, ISO 9001, ISO 20387, FDA registration. These are different bodies testing different things and they are not interchangeable.
  • FDA-registered means a facility told the FDA it exists. We made a whole ad about that distinction on 14 September and the point stands.
  • GenVault's accreditations are GenVault's: CAP, ISO 9001, ISO 20387, FDA-registered, New Jersey, ambient temperature. A competitor's AABB accreditation is theirs. We never borrow either direction.
  • CLIP MOMENT: "Four acronyms, four different questions. Anybody who lists them in one breath is hoping you will not ask which is which."

SEGMENT 4 (~4 min): What we are actually claiming

  • The claim is disclosure, not structure. We name our storer on the product page and in every ad. That is a low bar and most of this sector does not clear it, and the reason it is a low bar is worth saying.
  • And the sector-wide finding from our own competitor file: four companies in a row whose price we could not establish from public sources. Price behind a consultation and custody behind a brand name are the same habit.
  • The ask is not "switch to us". It is "go and ask whoever has your material right now".

2. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER (7-9 minutes): "The Parts of Your Genome That Move"

Thesis: a large share of the human genome is made of sequences whose defining behaviour is that they copy themselves to new locations, and most people have never been told this exists.

SCOPE LIMIT, BINDING AND STATED ON SCREEN: this explainer covers the biology only. It does not mention this week's study, it makes no claim about IVF, and it reports no findings about any group of people. That material belongs to the 17:11 run and the separation is deliberate. retrotranspos, jumping gene, LINE-1 and transposon return ZERO across the entire fleet, so this is new ground by any measure.

Script with B-roll

  1. The opening fact, flat. Your genome is not a static text. Parts of it are mobile. (B-roll: a long printed strip of text, scrolling past, overhead.)
  2. What a transposable element is. A sequence that can relocate or copy itself within the genome. Two broad families: those that cut and move, and those that copy via an RNA intermediate and paste the copy elsewhere. The second kind are retrotransposons.
  3. LINE-1, named and placed. Long Interspersed Nuclear Element 1. The most studied of the human mobile elements, and one of the few still capable of moving in humans. (B-roll: the same printed strip, with one block highlighted appearing twice.)
  4. Why evolution tolerated this. Mostly because most copies land harmlessly, and over very long timescales mobile elements have been a source of genomic novelty. Say clearly that "tolerated" is not "beneficial" and that this is a description of history, not a value judgement.
  5. When it matters clinically. An insertion landing inside a functional gene can disrupt it. This is a known mechanism in a small number of documented disease cases. EPISTEMIC LABEL ON SCREEN: established mechanism, rare in practice, and we are making no claim about frequency.
  6. Why anyone is measuring this now. Because sequencing got cheap enough to count insertions and deletions at specific loci, which was not practical before. The measurement is new; the biology is ancient.
  7. The honest close, and no product. A genome is not a document that sits still. It is a document with a maintenance history. The useful instinct is not alarm, it is noticing that "your DNA" is a slightly simpler phrase than the thing it describes.

HONEST-LABEL BLOCK (on screen, 8 seconds): "General biology, not news. No claim is made here about any study, any group of people, or any method of conception."
