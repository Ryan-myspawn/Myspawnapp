THE WEEK'S ANGLE: EVERY CUSTODIAN HAS A CLOCK. ALMOST NOBODY HAS READ THEIRS.

The commercial insight, in one sentence: people assume the things holding their records keep them by default, and nearly every one of those things is running a countdown they have never looked at.

The four proof points, all verified this week, all checkable by the reader.

  1. HIPAA does not set a retention period for medical records at all. It requires six years for HIPAA's own administrative paperwork. Your actual medical record is governed by state law and payer rules: Florida requires at least two years after a practice closes; Massachusetts requires seven years from the final visit.
  2. A safe deposit box does not need you to die. It needs you to go quiet. Escheatment dormancy clocks run three years in Florida, Arizona and California, five in New Jersey, Virginia and Washington. Contents are remitted to the state, appraised and sold at public auction. If it sells before you claim, the cash proceeds stay claimable indefinitely. The object does not.
  3. Colorado legislated the opposite of what the privacy conversation assumes. SB 22-224 makes donor records subject to permanent retention, because for donor-conceived people deletion was the injury rather than the protection.
  4. So the real question was never "how fast will they delete it." It is how long, who can open it, and who decides. Three questions, in writing.
