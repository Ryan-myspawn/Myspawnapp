QUIET-DAY DEEP DIVE: ACORN BIOLABS (the rotation's named target, and it REFUTED yesterday's prediction)

The rotation gave this dive a specific job rather than a description: TEST yesterday's claim that price disclosure tracks the SALES MOTION, with subscriptions publishing prices and consultations not. Acorn was chosen because its price had been refused on sourcing twice before.

THE PREDICTION FAILED, AND THE FAILURE IS MORE USEFUL THAN A CONFIRMATION WOULD HAVE BEEN. Acorn has BOTH: a mandatory specialist consultation to qualify you, AND a storage store that publishes exact prices. The rule as stated yesterday predicted that a consultation-gated company would not publish prices. It does.

WHAT IT IS. A cell-banking service that cryopreserves living stem cells collected from hair follicles, raised $8m for the model (Longevity.Technology, undated in our reading). This is NOT our product and the distinction has to travel with every number below: Acorn preserves LIVING CELLS in cryostorage. MySpawn preserves a DNA RECORD at ambient temperature. Different物 physics, different promise, different failure modes.

THE PRICING, AND IT IS THE FIRST COMPETITOR IN SIX DIVES WE CAN STATE TO THE DOLLAR.

  • Collection and processing: "From $945" on the US landing page, with the first step being a specialist consultation who confirms you are "a good candidate". Affirm payment plans offered. A 2024 Acorn page gives "around $945 plus tax at most clinics" and says clinics set their own fees; one Vancouver clinic lists a $900 collection procedure.
  • US storage plans, published to the dollar at store.acorn.me: Yearly $190/year. Agility $850 for 5 years. Vitality $1,650 for 10 years. A family option at $350/year.
  • An older Canadian FAQ gives a monthly storage fee of $16 CAD. No current US monthly rate found.
  • A storage plan must be bought in the same country as the collection.

(All via search index; no page opened at its own domain.)

THE REFINEMENT THAT SURVIVES THE FAILED PREDICTION, and it is sharper than the rule it replaces: the RECURRING fee is published to the dollar and the ONE-TIME, CLINIC-DELIVERED fee is a range at best. And the reason is structural rather than cultural: the clinic sets its own collection fee, so the company CANNOT publish a single number for it even if it wanted to. Restated as a testable rule for the next dive: a company publishes the prices it controls and ranges the prices a third party sets. That predicts CellSave's unfindable US pricing correctly, because the procedure happens at a birth in somebody else's hospital.

THE FIRST HONEST HEAD-TO-HEAD PRICE COMPARISON THIS FILE HAS EVER BEEN ABLE TO MAKE, with its caveat welded on. Acorn storage: $190 a year, or $165 a year if you prepay ten years at $1,650. MySpawn: $99 a year, billed annually. Roughly half. THE CAVEAT IS NOT OPTIONAL AND MUST APPEAR WHEREVER THE NUMBER DOES: they are storing living cells in cryogenic conditions and we are storing a DNA record at ambient temperature. Cryo is more expensive because it is harder, not because they are overcharging. A comparison that omits that is dishonest and we will not run one.

THE TACTIC WORTH STEALING, AND IT IS A STRUCTURE NOT A SLOGAN: Acorn separates the one-time entry fee from the recurring storage fee and prices the storage in three durations, one year, five years and ten. A ten-year prepay at $1,650 reframes an open-ended subscription as a finite, bounded decision. We sell one duration: a year, forever. We have never offered a reader a way to stop thinking about it. Worth a founder decision, not an agent one.

SO WHAT FOR MYSPAWN. Pricing pressure: NONE, and the opposite is closer to true, because we are half their annual rate for a product with no compressor to fail. Copy angle: STRONG, and it is the duration structure rather than the price. Differentiation proof: moderate. Threat level: LOW, different product and different customer moment.
