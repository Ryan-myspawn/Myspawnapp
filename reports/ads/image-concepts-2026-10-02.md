## THE COLLAPSE TEST, APPLIED RATHER THAN QUOTED

Today's Content Factory attached a testable kill condition to this ad:

> "If the artwork ends up showing a single document, the concept has collapsed into 137 and it does not ship."

**The artwork shows six identical controls, not one document.** The brief's own reasoning was that Ad_CustodyNotAccess_137 (18 September) asserts one model while this ad is about the VARIANCE between models, which is why the visual had to be two things that look the same rather than one locked door. A row of identical handwheels, each bolted to a different pipe, is that idea in one object: **the handle is the same at every company and what it opens is not.**

**The brief's literal visual direction was "two identical printed forms side by side on a dark surface", and that direction was NOT followed.** It was tested against this log's own 25 September structural finding (consumer and office objects are photographed by the people who sell them) and against the 23 September note that a generic pen-on-blank-page is forbidden by the photo mandate. **Printed forms are the paper family, which Ad_SixHundredAndOneDays_129 already holds and which reliably returns mockups.** The brief's *idea* was kept exactly and its *object* was replaced. That substitution is recorded here rather than left for a reader to notice.

**BINDING RAILS HONORED.** No company or platform is named in either ratio. **The third model, the ownership claim, does not appear in any form**, in line with the radar hold; the sub describes only that the meaning varies, and does not describe either verified model in detail.

**Yesterday this log killed a photograph on the rule that "either alone might have been disclosable; both together is a kill."** That precedent was re-read before shipping rather than worked around. **It does not apply here, and the reason is a real distinction rather than a convenience: on 1 October both adjacencies pointed at the SAME prior ad being nearly reproduced** (concrete-as-subject and the composition of Ad_141 together amounted to remaking 141). **Here the two adjacencies point at two DIFFERENT prior ads on two DIFFERENT axes**, and neither prior ad is approached: 172 contains no repeated units and 148 contains no machinery. A composite that resembles no single existing asset is not the thing that rule was written to stop.

## THE FINDING OF THE DAY: THE COLLISION GREP WAS SCOPED TO THE PHRASE RATHER THAN THE OBJECT

Before sourcing, this run grepped `image-concepts-log.md` for "phone number", "payphone", "delegate", "two doors" and "same two words". **All returned zero and the payphone concept was treated as clear.**

It was not clear. **Grepping "telephone" returns Ad_Echo2126_93 (6 September), "We built a phone to 2126", whose hero object is an antique rotary telephone.** The telephone family was already held, by an ad whose metaphor is also a phone, and the check missed it **because it searched for the words in the proposed headline rather than for the name of the thing in the proposed photograph.**

The signage kill made the point moot and that is luck, not process. **RULE ADOPTED: the collision grep runs on the OBJECT FAMILY, not the headline phrase, and it runs on synonyms of the object before a search is issued.** Today's valve concept was re-checked that way against box, crate, jar, bottle, rail, weight, counter, bulb and nine other terms before the search was run, which is how the luggage collision at Ad_149 and the key collisions at Ad_105 and Ad_138 were found and avoided in time.

**A second pattern worth naming: three photographs have now been killed at full resolution in four days for text a thumbnail hid** (29 September wet street, 30 September the same, today's payphone and pegboard). **The full-resolution inspection is not a formality at the end of sourcing. It is the step that decides, and it should run before composition work rather than after.**
