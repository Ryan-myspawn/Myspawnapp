# Ad Copy Lab: 23 September 2026
## Angle: THE DATE IS THE PRODUCT

**Freshness check:** **This angle was explicitly banked on 16 September as "NEXT WEEK'S LEAD unless something breaks."** Nothing broke hard enough to displace it. The week's other strong material (the export question, the four-company audit) is **editorially excellent and commercially wrong right now**: the export angle's whole strength is inviting scrutiny back at us, and **we cannot answer it in public yet** because the sample-continuity page is not live. **Running a paid campaign on a question we cannot answer would be the worst possible use of this week's best idea.** No prior batch has used time-locking as the lead; the closest is "The Date Takes Longer" (an ad direction, not a copy angle) and the back-to-school milestone angle, which is seasonal and still banked.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55.
**Trend / hook used:** Competitor Watch found a video-first family-legacy product at **exactly our $99/year price**, and this week found a **second possible $99 convergence** (contested, see below). **When a competitor matches your price, the only thing left is the mechanic they do not have.** Ours is the lock.

---

## THE POSITIONING, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

**Anyone can store a video. The product is that you cannot open it early.**

You pick the day. After that it is not yours to move, and that includes you at 2am feeling sentimental. **A folder on a hard drive is a promise you can break. A sealed date is not a promise, it is a mechanism.** That is the difference between keeping something and addressing it.

**Why now, commercially:** price parity has arrived in this category. **Competing on $99 is no longer a differentiator; competing on what $99 buys is.** Time-locking is the thing the video-first alternatives do not do.

**Contested-fact guardrail, binding on this batch:** today's Competitor Watch found **two of three tiers of a major incumbent's pricing now in dispute**, with the primary source blocked from our container and both secondary sources conflicted. **No ad in this batch references any competitor's price, tier, or name.** Every claim here is about us. That is safer and it is also better copy.

## (a) META: three concepts, one per audience

### CONCEPT M1 · Audience: 25-40, future-optionality
- **Primary text (lead, 64 chars):** *You can open a folder on your drive at 2am. That is the problem.*
- **Longer variant:** *You can open a folder on your drive at 2am. That is the problem. A Memory Vault message is sealed to a date you choose, and after that it is not yours to move either. Record it now, at the age you actually are. She sees it on the day. $99/year, billed annually. First 1,000 members lock the founding rate.*
- **Headline (23):** The Date Is The Product
- **Description (20):** Sealed until the day
- **CTA button:** Learn more
- **Visual direction:** a phone face-down on a dark surface, one hard line of light across it. No hands, no faces. **Not a calendar**, which is the obvious and worse choice.

### CONCEPT M2 · Audience: 40-60, family history and mortality salience
- **Primary text (lead, 73 chars):** *Record it at 44. She opens it at 18. Not a day earlier, including by you.*
- **Longer variant:** *Record it at 44. She opens it at 18. Not a day earlier, including by you. That last part is the whole design. A message you can retrieve whenever you like is a file. A message sealed to a date is addressed to somebody. MySpawn seals video messages to milestone dates and stores a DNA record alongside them in an accredited third-party biorepository. $99/year.*
- **Headline (23):** You Do Not Hold The Key
- **Description (16):** Opens on the day
- **CTA button:** Learn more
- **Visual direction:** an empty chair at a kitchen table, morning light, shot wide and cool. **No child in frame, no cake, no candles.** The restraint is the tone.

### CONCEPT M3 · Audience: childfree legacy
- **Primary text (lead, 60 chars):** *No kids does not mean no date. Pick who, pick when, seal it.*
- **Longer variant:** *No kids does not mean no date. A niece. A friend's kid. A person who does not exist yet. Pick who, pick when, seal it. The Vault does not ask you to justify the recipient and it does not assume one. $99/year, billed annually.*
- **Headline (20):** Pick Who. Pick When.
- **Description (25):** $99/year, billed annually
- **CTA button:** Learn more
- **Visual direction:** a single sealed envelope-shaped object on stone, deliberately ambiguous, shot dark. **No family imagery of any kind.**
- **BINDING TONE NOTE:** **nothing in this concept may imply that not having children is a problem to solve.** The line is "does not mean no date," not "still counts." **If the copy ever reads as consolation, kill it.**

## (e) COMPLIANCE PASS

| Check | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Storage only** | **PASS** | No concept implies creation, cloning, editing or any reproductive service. **No promise of a descendant anywhere.** M3 names recipients who may not exist yet and is careful to say "a person who does not exist yet" rather than implying we would make one. |
| **Pricing** | **PASS** | Every price reference is **"$99/year"** or **"billed annually."** **No "once," no "one-time," no phrasing implying a single payment.** RSA4 says "Same Every Year", which is a claim about our own price behavior and is true. |
| **Accreditation** | **PASS, and deliberately thin** | Copy says **"an accredited third-party biorepository"** and stops. **No ISO, CAP or FDA reference appears in any asset**, because the FDA de-claim does not fit in ad copy and an undeclaimed "FDA-registered" is the exact error the partner outreach templates were corrected for this week. **If a longer-form asset needs the full list, it must carry the de-claim in the same sentence.** |
| **Health claims** | **PASS** | None. No mention of aging, biological age, fertility or any health outcome. |
| **Fear-mongering** | **PASS** | No concept threatens loss, death urgency or family failure. M2 is the closest and is written cool and specific rather than ominous. |
| **Childfree tone** | **PASS, with a binding kill rule in-asset** | M3 carries an explicit instruction: **nothing may imply that not having children is a problem to solve, and if the copy reads as consolation, kill it.** |
| **Competitor references** | **PASS, zero** | **No competitor is named, priced or alluded to in any asset**, which is a deliberate response to today's finding that two of three tiers of an incumbent's pricing are now contested and unverifiable from here. |
| **Right of publicity** | **PASS** | No real person named or depicted. **Spark 2 must be delivered by the founder or recast in third person; an actor must not claim a daughter.** |
| **Needs legal review** | **ONE ITEM** | **RSA4, "$99/Year. Same Every Year."** is a forward-looking price commitment. It is true today and consistent with "first 1,000 members lock the founding rate", but **a price claim phrased as a promise about future years should get thirty seconds of legal eyes before it runs.** Everything else is standard. |

**Summary: 10 assets, 0 claims cut, 1 flagged for legal review, 0 competitor references by design.**
