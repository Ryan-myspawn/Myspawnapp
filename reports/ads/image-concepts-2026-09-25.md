**Primary audience:** Men 25-55.
**Trend / hook used:** Rotating preferred direction, practical why-now. **The copy angle "you do not need a cryotank to start" has been sitting unused in our competitor file since 19 August**; today's Acorn dive put live-cell cryostorage back in front of us and made it current again.

- **Headline:** COLD IS NOT THE ONLY WAY TO KEEP SOMETHING.
- **Support:** "Keratin is a water-free matrix. Nails have been STR-typed after more than ten years at ordinary room temperature."

- **Placement:** paid, square and vertical. **No person, no face, no text, no brand in frame.**

## THE STRUCTURAL FINDING, and it is the most useful output of this run

**Two days, two Content-Factory-briefed consumer objects, two total wipeouts: hairbrush 8 for 8 yesterday, cast iron 6 for 6 today.**

**The cause is not bad luck and it is not the kill rules.** **Consumer objects are photographed overwhelmingly by the people who sell them.** A hairbrush, a frying pan, a comb, a stove handle: the supply on Unsplash is brand product shoots, 3D product renders and food styling, because that is who has a commercial reason to photograph them well.

**Look at what actually clears.** Every photo shipped in the last week is a **non-consumer subject**: a fallen leaf, stacked antique luggage, a museum sundial, worn stone steps, draped knitwear, and today's cracked ice. **All photographed by enthusiasts and travel photographers with no product to sell.**

**ACTIONABLE BRIEF RULE, proposed for the Content Factory rather than adopted unilaterally: when a script names a household object as its device, the AD does not have to use the same object.** The script can hold the hairbrush. The ad should carry the idea on a subject nobody sells. **That single change would have saved both of this week's wipeouts.**
