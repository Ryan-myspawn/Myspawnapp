## WHY SIX AND NOT TEN

Yesterday's shortfall was **rights and brand failures** at inspection. Today's is **photo availability for the specific objects the strongest concepts needed**. Two good concepts died for want of an image:

1. **"The Only Retention Numbers Anyone Is Required To Keep"** (the CLIA receipt, drafted as a static direction in this morning's Content Factory file). It needs either a receipt or an archive of records. The receipt searches returned mockups and toilet paper. The one credible archive photo, a wall of small wooden drawers at Hardwick Hall, is the **same visual family as yesterday's library** (Ad_PropertyOnOctoberFirst_130) one day apart, which is exactly the visual-hook repetition the binding rules forbid. **Banked to the reserve for next week**, when 130 clears the seven-day window. The concept keeps; only the image was missing.
2. **A vial-led execution of "Delete Is Not Destroy."** The literal object, a sample vial on a shelf, had no usable photograph. **The concept survived by changing the image, not the idea**: a data-centre floor makes the same point better, because the thing that persists after you press delete is a building full of hardware you will never see.

**A third concept, killed on right-of-publicity:** a photograph of a technician working on server equipment, which would have earned the CLIA concept well, shows **an identifiable person in profile**. The rules permit shipping a face photo flagged organic-only; I did not, because this concept's whole point is a regulatory claim and putting an unreleased stranger's face beside one is the wrong risk to take for a marginal creative.

**Padding to ten was available and I did not take it.** The options were a generic stock-feel pairing, a repeat of yesterday's visual family, or a face without a release. The mandate says each photo must earn its concept. Six that earn it beats ten that do not.

## NEW CONCEPT 1: Ad_DeleteIsNotDestroy_132

**Freshness check:** New. No prior ad has used deletion mechanics, and no prior ad has used a data-centre visual. Distinct from yesterday's Ad_PropertyOnOctoberFirst_130, which was about a statutory right rather than about what a button does.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55.**
**Trend-hook used:** Connecticut's genetic-privacy provisions take effect **October 1, 2026**, and today's blog article on retention policies documents the delete-versus-destroy gap that the law addresses.

- **Headline:** DELETING YOUR RESULTS DOES NOT DESTROY THE SAMPLE.
- **Support:** Those are two different requests. At some companies the second one has to be made separately.
- **Vertical:** DELETE IS NOT DESTROY. / Two different requests. Make sure you made both.
- **Placement:** feed and Stories. **This is the day's lead and the ad to run first.** It pairs with the M1 Meta copy written in this morning's Ad Copy Lab, which means the copy and the creative were developed for each other rather than glued together.
- **Photo:** modern data centre, rows of white server cabinets under overhead lighting. **Tony Marinescu (@tonymarinescu)**, https://unsplash.com/photos/fImCPTZ026U **(consumed from the photo reserve; row removed.)**
- **Claims note:** the copy says "at some companies", names nobody, and describes documented published policy. That phrasing is deliberate and should not be tightened into a specific accusation.

## NEW CONCEPT 2: Ad_AClosingLabMustPlan_133

**Freshness check:** New claim and new visual. The log has nothing on regulatory retention obligations, and no night-architecture photo has been used.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55**, estate-planner lead.
**Trend-hook used:** the CLIA cease-operation provision documented in today's blog article, alongside Connecticut's October 1 date.

- **Headline:** A CLOSING LAB MUST MAKE ARRANGEMENTS.
- **Support:** Federal rules require it for clinical labs. A consumer contract is a different kind of promise.
- **Vertical:** A CLOSING LAB MUST MAKE PLANS. / Federal rules require it. A contract is a different promise.
- **Placement:** feed. The most intellectually interesting of the three and the slowest to read; not a Stories candidate.
- **Photo:** a tall city building at night, lit windows, deep shadow. **Yuliia Dementsova (@julia_demo)**, https://unsplash.com/photos/6bTEvoIXsVQ **(consumed from the photo reserve; row removed.)**
- **Claims note:** the regulatory claim is real (42 CFR 493.1105 requires a laboratory ceasing operation to make provisions for retention of records, slides, blocks and tissue) and it is stated as applying to **clinical labs**, not to consumer DNA companies. **Do not let anyone edit that qualifier out**; without it the ad implies a protection consumers do not have.
