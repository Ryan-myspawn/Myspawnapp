## VOLUME: 6 FINISHED, NOT 10. The reason, stated plainly.
**Three new squares plus their three verticals.** This is well under target and I am not dressing it up.

**Two causes, and they are different from each other and from the last four runs.**

**Cause 1: two of five sourced photos were killed for legible business names.** The shutter and storefront searches, which were the day's strongest concept-to-image fit, returned photographs with **"LA RESERVE"** and **"Santa Barbara"** clearly readable on the buildings. **A paid ad about companies closing, carrying a real named business shown shuttered, is a worse problem than a brand logo on an object.** Both killed before composition. *(Search note: "closed metal shutter shopfront street" returned a total of **three** results on Unsplash. The category is thin, not unlucky.)*

**Cause 2, and this one is a PIPELINE FAILURE worth more than the missing creatives.** The plan was to make up the shortfall with verticals of four proven backlog ads that have none: **Ad_TwoDishes_114, Ad_ThirtyYears_118, Ad_PreservedSecret_123, Ad_TheBoringOne_125.** **They cannot be re-rendered.** Their HTML masters reference **local filenames** (`src_marsh.jpg`, `src_lab.jpg`, `src_vault.jpg`, `src_clock.jpg`) that no longer exist in the scratchpad, and **their raw images.unsplash.com URLs were never recorded anywhere**, only the human-facing photo page URLs. I attempted recovery by searching Unsplash for the exact photo descriptions: **the vault query returned zero results and the marshland query returned a different photograph.** Recovery by search is unreliable and would risk putting the wrong image on a known concept, so it was abandoned rather than guessed.

**What that means, and it is the important sentence in this document: the mirror-safe rule assumes the HTML masters ARE the masters, and for every ad made before the remote-URL rewrite that assumption is false.** Those ads exist only as the JPEGs already committed. They cannot be re-cropped, re-tinted, re-headlined or adapted to a new ratio, ever.

**FIX, applied today and recommended as standing:**
1. **Every master from today forward carries an HTML comment recording the Unsplash photo ID, the photographer, and the full raw URL.** Today's three do. *(Example: `<!-- source photo: unsplash RH6EpUQfd7A by huang gugu (@7gugu) :: https://images.unsplash.com/... -->`)*
2. **Backfill where the concept docs make it possible.** Several older docs record the photo page URL, which is enough for a human to open the page and copy the raw URL in seconds, but not enough for this session to do automatically. **A single pass by the founder or one authorised sourcing run would repair the archive permanently.**
3. **This strengthens rather than weakens the banked-photo-reserve proposal**, now on its fifth request: a reserve entry already stores the raw URL, the native width and the photographer. **Had these four ads come from the reserve, they would be re-renderable today.**

## NEW CONCEPTS

### Ad_HowManyPeopleWereInIt_142
**Freshness check:** No concept, headline or visual hook in the log touches study quality, participant counts or supplement evidence. No stadium, arena or crowd-space photo has ever been used.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55
**Trend / hook used:** A wave of "seafood reverses signs of aging" coverage on 2 September 2026, sitting on a mouse study published in 2022.

- **Headline:** HOW MANY PEOPLE WERE IN THE STUDY?
- **Support:** The one everybody shared in September had none. It was mice, and it was published in 2022. Ask the question before you buy.
- **Placement:** cold interest audiences in health, supplements and longevity. **Zero product in the body; the lockup is the only mention.**
- **Ratios:** 1080x1080 + 1080x1920.
- **Photo:** a large empty stadium, rows of seats receding under a lit roof. **huang gugu (@7gugu)**, https://unsplash.com/photos/a-large-empty-stadium-filled-with-lots-of-seats-RH6EpUQfd7A
- **Why the photo earns it:** the headline asks how many people, and the picture is thousands of seats with nobody in them. **The image answers the question before the body copy does.**

### Ad_MoneyOnceObligationForever_143
**Freshness check:** New concept, new headline, new visual hook. The category-failure argument has never appeared in an ad; no bench, pew or seating-row photo is in the log.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55
**Trend / hook used:** Four of the best-known digital-legacy companies have shut down, pivoted or paused in roughly two years.

- **Headline:** THE MONEY COMES IN ONCE. THE OBLIGATION LASTS FOREVER.
- **Support:** Four companies built to keep people's memories have closed, pivoted or paused in about two years. We are in the same gap.
- **Placement:** the most honest creative in the batch and the hardest to run. Warm audiences and retargeting, where the viewer already knows who we are. **Not a cold-traffic ad: it opens with a structural problem and admits we share it.**
- **Ratios:** 1080x1080 + 1080x1920.
- **Photo:** rows of empty wooden benches on a wooden floor, low warm light. **Ivan Stepanov (@evil_bumblebee)**, https://unsplash.com/photos/a-row-of-wooden-benches-sitting-on-top-of-a-wooden-floor-JR6Pjdq3Qx0
- **Why the photo earns it:** benches are built to sit unattended for decades and to still be there. **That is the promise the headline says is hard to keep.**
- **NO COMPANY IS NAMED**, in the creative or the alt text.

### Ad_ItMissedItsPrimaryEndpoint_144
**Freshness check:** New concept, new headline. No track, lane-marking or sports-surface photo in the log. Distinct from 142 despite the shared research peg: **142 asks about participants, 144 is about the endpoint**, and they are designed as a two-beat sequence.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55
**Trend / hook used:** The largest human plasmalogen trial, 328 patients over 24 weeks, was null on its primary outcome.

- **Headline:** IT MISSED ITS PRIMARY ENDPOINT.
- **Support:** 328 patients. 24 weeks. No significant difference from placebo. The result you saw quoted came from a subgroup.
- **Placement:** second beat after 142, same audiences. **Zero product in the body.**
- **Ratios:** 1080x1080 + 1080x1920.
- **Photo:** a running track surface, white lane lines crossed by a coloured band. **Nikita Godunov (@nikgodunov)**, https://unsplash.com/photos/red-and-brown-stripe-textile-99AF3uOp3GA
- **Why the photo earns it:** lanes and a line across them is what an endpoint looks like. **Tint raised to .62**, well above our usual, because the source is bright orange and red and fights the navy treatment; verified at full size that it now reads as a dark graphic rather than a sports photo.
- **CLAIMS NOTE:** every figure in the body is from **Fujino et al., *eBioMedicine* 2017** (328 participants, 24 weeks, null on the primary outcome and on all secondary outcomes in intention-to-treat; the quoted benefit is a subgroup finding). **Peer-reviewed. Verify against the paper before this runs.**
