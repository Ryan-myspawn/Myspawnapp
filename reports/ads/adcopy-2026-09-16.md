# MySpawn Ad Copy Lab, Wednesday, September 16, 2026
## Week's angle: DELETE IS NOT DESTROY

**Freshness check:** New angle, verified against `adcopy-log.md`. The log's three custody-adjacent weeks are distinct from this one: Sept 2 was the **access gap** (elite price ladder), Sept 9 was **Audit Us First** (our accountability, the four-questions heuristic). This week is about **something the consumer does not know about the sample they already gave away**, which is a different insight, a different hook family and a different job. No prior angle has used retention or deletion mechanics.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55** (audience splits below still cover 40-60 and childfree, with the lead concept on 25-40).
**Trend / hook used:** **Connecticut's genetic-privacy provisions take effect October 1, 2026**, fifteen days out, creating a consumer property right in a biological sample and an explicit right to require its destruction. Supported by today's blog article on retention policies, which gives every asset a real destination.

**Why this is the week's strongest COMMERCIAL angle rather than just the most interesting one:** it reframes the category. A sample sitting at a testing company is a **leftover from a product you bought for something else**. Ours is the product. That contrast sells the $99/year storage without disparaging anyone, and it gives the free Letter tool an honest lead-gen role for people who are not ready to buy anything.

**HARD COPY RULE FOR THIS WHOLE BATCH: no competitor is named in any asset.** The insight is true of the category and naming a company turns a factual contrast into a legal problem. Every reference is "your DNA testing company" or "some companies".

## (e) COMPLIANCE PASS

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| **Storage only, no descendant creation** | Clean. No asset implies creation, revival, or anything reproductive. M3 explicitly says "no kids required, none implied, and nothing about fertility" |
| **Accreditations attributed to GenVault** | RSA description 3 says "an accredited biorepository in New Jersey" **without claiming the accreditation as ours**. Any asset that names ISO, CAP or FDA registration must add the full qualifier: those are GenVault's credentials, and FDA-registered means the facility is registered with the agency, not approved or endorsed by it. **No asset in this batch names them, which is the safest version** |
| **No health claims** | Clean. Nothing in the batch mentions health, longevity, disease or fertility |
| **No fear-mongering of childfree or parents** | Clean. M3 treats childlessness as a design choice, not a loss. No asset implies a reader is failing anyone |
| **Pricing** | $99/year, billed annually, in M1, M3 and RSA H4/D3. No "once", no "one-time", no "no subscription" |
| **Competitor naming** | **Zero competitors named anywhere**, deliberately, and this is the batch's main legal protection |

**FLAGS FOR LEGAL REVIEW, three:**
1. **M1 and Spark 1 make a factual claim about industry practice** ("at some companies the physical sample stays until you separately ask"). It is accurate and documented in published policies, and it is unnamed, but it is a claim about third parties in paid creative. **One legal read before spend.**
2. **M2 states the effect of a specific statute and a thirteen-state count.** The Connecticut effective date is solid; the thirteen-state figure comes from a **2026 law-firm survey, not an official registry**. Either soften to "many states" or get the count confirmed before this runs.
3. **M2 expires.** After October 1 the copy reads as stale and should be pulled or rewritten to past tense. **Set the end date at launch, not later.**
