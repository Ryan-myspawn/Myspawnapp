## NEW CONCEPTS

### Ad_YouDoNotHaveToBeGone_147
**Freshness check:** New concept, new headline, new visual family. No interior with seating exists in the log. The closest prior is Ad_MoneyOnceObligationForever_143 (19 September), **rows of empty wooden benches**, which is a public hall read: institutional, many seats, no window. This is a **domestic room with two chairs**, and the idea is the opposite: 143 was about an obligation outliving a payment, this is about using the thing while alive. Logged so a third seating photo does not follow without a reason.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55, warm.
**Trend / hook used:** Competitor dive #26. A company that began as voice and life-story preservation for after death reported that **most of its users were not preparing for death; they wanted to use the technology while still alive.**

- **Headline (square):** YOU DO NOT HAVE TO BE GONE / FOR SOMEONE TO WATCH IT.
- **Headline (vertical):** YOU DO NOT HAVE TO / BE GONE FOR SOMEONE / TO WATCH IT.
- **Support:** A company built for after you die found most users were not preparing for death. Record it for a birthday. Sit beside them.
- **Placement:** warm audiences and retargeting. This is the **living-use** angle, which the dive flagged as a founder-level question about our own positioning rather than only a content angle.
- **Ratios:** 1080x1080 + 1080x1920.
- **Photo:** two upholstered chairs beneath a frosted window in a dark room. **Dmitry Lee (@dr_edmitry)**, ID `tppELstLEP8`.
- **Why the photo earns it:** **two seats, not one.** The support line ends "sit beside them", and the frame is the only thing in the run that shows the second person's place without showing a person. The frosted window supplies light without a view, which keeps the room anonymous.
- **CLAIMS NOTE:** the finding is a **founder's public account of his own company's users**, not a study, and the ad is worded to match: "a company ... found", no company named, no figure, no percentage. **Epistemic label for any reuse: company self-report, single source, not peer-reviewed.**
- **RAIL CHECK:** we store records. The ad promises a recording watched by someone, which is the product. **No health claim, no biological-age claim, no gamete claim.**

### Ad_WhichModelWhoBuiltIt_148
**Freshness check:** New concept, new headline, new visual hook. No architectural grid, facade or repeated-aperture photograph exists anywhere in the log. Biological-age measurement has appeared in copy before (Ad_SameAgeLowerNumber_146, 20 September) but on a **different claim**: 146 was about testosterone decline being two findings collapsed into one, this is about **the number of clocks**.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55, cold interest in longevity and quantified self.
**Trend / hook used:** A peer-reviewed *Cell* study released a platform that predicts biological age across **233 clocks spanning six modalities**, using 429,165 coefficients. Hold on this concept was released today.

- **Headline:** WHICH MODEL. / WHO BUILT IT. / COMPARED TO WHOM?
- **Support:** One study now computes biological age 233 different ways. We keep a record, not a score. Ask those questions of anyone else.
- **Placement:** cold interest audiences. **ZERO product in the body beyond the disclaimer clause**; the lockup is the only commercial mention. **Not a conversion ad.** Its job is to make the reader skeptical of a number that is sold to them, and the fourth sentence explicitly invites them to point the same three questions at us.
- **Ratios:** 1080x1080 + 1080x1920.
- **Photo:** a concrete facade of identical circular windows in a regular grid. **Zhiyuan Sun (@ssszy)**, ID `QHg0uG0jiEM`.
- **Why the photo earns it:** **the frame is the argument.** Dozens of apertures, identical in construction, each showing something slightly different behind the glass. That is what 233 clocks measuring one person look like. It is the rare case where the image carries the statistic without a chart.
- **CLAIMS NOTE:** the *Cell* study is **peer-reviewed** and the 233 figure comes from it. **The separately circulating "beats every frontier system tested" claim is a developer self-report on their own benchmark and does NOT appear in this creative.** The ad names no company, no product and no author.
- **RAIL CHECK, and this one is the point of the ad:** **MySpawn does not measure, affect or reverse biological age.** The support line says so in five words: *we keep a record, not a score.*

## SEARCH NOTE 2026-09-21

**Fourth consecutive run confirming the two- or three-word rule, with one new failure mode.** Working queries: **"two armchairs window" (150 results)** and **"many clocks wall" (41)**. The second returned enough images but **the usable fraction was near zero**, because a wall of clocks is a wall of clock faces and a clock face is where a manufacturer puts its name.

**New constraint to add to the file: a query that returns a repeated manufactured object will return a repeated brand mark.** Clocks, appliances, instruments, keyboards, watch faces, packaging. The abundance is real and the clearance rate is still close to zero. **This is a distinct failure from the thin-category problem noted on 19 September: there the photographs did not exist, here they exist in quantity and nearly all of them are unusable for the same reason.**

**And the finding that actually mattered:** `LTFAllQyAb0` entered the candidate set from a neutral interior query with no clinical terms in it. **Unsplash descriptions are the only thing that caught it** and the description had to be read, not skimmed. **Standing rule, effective now: read the full description of every candidate before composition, not only the visual.** A photograph can be rights-clean, dark, brand-free, person-free and still be disqualifying on subject alone, and the subject is not always visible in a thumbnail.
