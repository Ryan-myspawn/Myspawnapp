## THE VOLUME LINE: FOURTH CONSECUTIVE SHORTFALL, AND TODAY THE BINDING CONSTRAINT CHANGED

Four finished against a target of ten. **What is different about today is which rule did the cutting.**

On the last three shortfalls the constraint was **photo supply**: not enough images cleared rights, darkness, brand marks and people. **Today four photographs cleared all of that and were killed anyway, three of them on visual-hook repetition against our own recent work.** The lane is no longer running out of usable photographs. **It is running out of visual territory.**

**And the vertical fallback does not exist.** Every ad from 137 onward already has its vertical, and the four backlog ads without one were established on 19 September to be un-re-renderable. **There is no way to reach ten by adapting proven work, which is what the spec's own fallback assumes.**

**Standing recommendation, eighth asking, wording unchanged so it is not softened by restatement:**

> **(1) authorise ONE sourcing-only run banking 30-40 vetted photos, roughly a week of headroom at the observed clearance rate; (2) lower the daily target to 4-6 new concepts plus a refresh lane; (3) keep the kill rules exactly as they are, because every shortfall this month came from enforcing them and every kill was correct.**

**Today adds a fourth item to that request:** the reserve is no longer only a speed fix. **A bank of 30-40 photographs spread across different visual families is the only thing that would let the repetition window be honored without capping output at four.**

## NEW CONCEPTS

### Ad_OneOutOfFour_150
**Freshness check:** New concept, new headline, new visual family. **No ad in the log uses a leather object, a document case, or a closed container of any kind.** The nearest subject adjacency is Ad_WhoIsAllowedToBuyThem_145 (20 Sep, brass lever) which is hardware, not a carried object. The **argument** is new to the ad lane entirely: no ad has ever been about our own verification process.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55, warm and cold.
**Trend / hook used:** Our four-company audit, completed this morning, of a market claim we were one asset away from publishing.

- **Headline (square):** WE CHECKED THE CLAIM / WE WERE ABOUT TO PUBLISH.
- **Headline (vertical):** WE CHECKED / THE CLAIM WE WERE / ABOUT TO PUBLISH. *(head set to 70px for this creative only; the shared bottom offsets are untouched)*
- **Support:** One of four held. All three misses ran the same direction: the one that flattered whoever wrote it. Ask us how we count.
- **Placement:** cold and warm. **Zero product in the body**; the lockup is the only commercial mention. **This is a credibility ad, not a conversion ad**, and it should be judged on saves and replies rather than clicks.
- **Ratios:** 1080x1080 + 1080x1920.
- **Photo:** a worn leather document case on a dark surface, single raking light. **Junsu Jeong (@ekslr)**, ID `968LK3Sqn90`.
- **Why the photo earns it:** **it is closed.** The whole concept is about the interval between receiving a claim and opening it, and a closed case is the only object that shows that interval. Tint raised to **.62** to cool a very warm brown source.
- **CLAIMS NOTE:** the ad states **"one of four held"** and nothing else. **No company is named**, none of the four is identifiable from the copy, and the source of the original claim is not described. Every underlying fact is in `reports/competitors/baseline.md` dive #27 with its evidence grade attached (court record, funding news, and a company's own investor document).
- **RAIL CHECK:** no health claim, no biological-age claim, no gamete claim, no promise about descendants.

### Ad_NotWrongJustNotNew_151
**Freshness check:** New concept, new headline. **No ad in the log uses stairs, steps, or any receding horizontal structure.** *(Adjacency named rather than glossed: Ad_WhichModelWhoBuiltIt_148 shipped yesterday on a facade of repeating circular apertures, and both photographs contain architectural repetition. They are separable: 148's subject IS the repetition, this one's subject is WEAR, the materials differ (poured concrete versus cut stone), and the orientation is opposite. **Logged with a forward rule: no third repeating-architecture photo before 29 September.**)*
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55, cold.
**Trend / hook used:** Today's blog research, which traced a statistic quoted across estate-planning content with no date attached.

- **Headline:** NOT WRONG. / JUST NOT NEW.
- **Support (square):** The statistic every estate planner quotes is from 2005, before half your family archive moved into a cloud account.
- **Support (vertical):** The statistic every estate planner quotes is from 2005. Ask when, not just whether.
- **Placement:** cold interest audiences in estate and legacy planning. **Zero product in the body.** It teaches a question and leaves.
- **Ratios:** 1080x1080 + 1080x1920.
- **Photo:** worn public stone steps, black and white, shot flat and receding. **Jonathan Lim (@jonathankslim)**, ID `9QOhtfvq7v0`.
- **Why the photo earns it:** stone steps are **the clearest image of something old that is still correct and still in daily use.** That is the exact claim of the headline, which is not a debunk.
- **CLAIMS NOTE, and it is the reason this ad is careful:** the underlying figure is from the **Allianz American Legacies Study, Age Wave with Allianz Life and Harris Interactive, n = 2,627, published 2005. Industry-commissioned, not peer-reviewed, twenty-one years old.** **The ad states only the year and names no study, no sponsor and no number**, so it asserts nothing a reader cannot check in one search. **It also does not say the finding is false, because as far as anyone knows it is not.**

**Fifth consecutive run confirming the short-query rule.** Working queries today: **"concrete seam shadow" (1,067)**, **"dark leather folder desk" (86)**, **"worn stone steps" (186)**, **"wooden table hard shadow" (279)**. All two or three words. All returned usable volume.

**THE FINDING, AND IT IS A CHANGE OF DIAGNOSIS.** For three weeks the shortfall has been reported as a **supply** problem: not enough photographs clear the rights, darkness, brand and people tests. **Today the supply tests were not the binding constraint.** Four photographs cleared every one of them and **three were killed for looking too much like something we shipped this week.**

**So the constraint has moved from supply to inventory.** Concept-first sourcing asks for one photograph at a time, and the seven-day window is a query against a set of one. **A banked reserve is a set of thirty, spread across families, which is the only structure in which the repetition rule and a daily target of ten can both be true.** That is a different and stronger argument than the speed and durability arguments already on file, and it is the eighth time the reserve has been requested.

**One process note, recorded because it nearly went the other way.** The strongest photograph of the day was killed for an adjacency one day old, and I wrote a disclosure paragraph to justify shipping it before deleting that paragraph. **The archive has a precedent for shipping-with-disclosure (20 September, brass lever against door), but that gap was two days and the materials differed. One day and the same material is not the same case.** The rule only works if it costs something.
