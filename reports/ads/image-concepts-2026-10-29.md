# WHY NOTHING SHIPPED

## Four declines, each with a distinct cause

**1. `counter`: 95 results, and the index returned the DOMESTIC sense.** Kitchen counters, coffee-shop counters, bar tops, bottles, bread. Several carried people or consumer packaging. **The concept needs the institutional sense, the place where you ask for something back, and that sense is not what the word retrieves.**

**2. `intercom`: 23 results and not one intercom.** Old radios, a monorail station, remote controls, boat throttle levers, a satellite dish, and a control panel of clocks which is barred by the dial-and-gauge blocker anyway. **A second word-sense miss in the same run.**

**3. `garments`: 96 results, all RETAIL.** Market racks, shop rails, fabric studies, people wearing clothes. **The object I wanted was a rail of items held on someone's behalf and retrievable with a ticket. What exists is merchandise.** A rack of sweatpants for sale does not earn a line about custody, and pairing them would be the generic stock-feel failure the photo mandate exists to prevent.

**4. The reserve's `GJao3ZTX9gU` (hands signing a contract, no face) was considered and REJECTED ON VISUAL-HOOK REPEAT.** It fits the concept better than anything the searches produced. **But Ad_MissingLineItem_92, on 6 September, used hands signing documents.** Fifty-three days is far outside any window, **and the brief's rule on new concepts is not a window: "NEVER repeat a concept, headline, or visual hook."** A second hands-signing photograph is the same hook with a different photographer. **Declined on the rule as written rather than on the calendar.**

## The real finding: the custody-object space in this log is saturated

Before any search ran, the obvious objects were grepped and the counts are the story. **`lock` 47. `window` 26. `vault` 19. `archive` 17. `drawer` 13. `gate` 10. `queue` 6. `locker` 5.** **Every natural photographic object for "somebody else is holding your thing" has been used, most of them repeatedly, across roughly 218 creatives.**

**That is not a bad day. It is a structural limit arriving on schedule**, and it is the same limit the Deep Production lane reported on 25 October in its own words: **the format space is crowding faster than the topic space.** This lane has now hit the object-space version of it.

**WHAT THIS CHANGES, and it is a founder question rather than a fix I can apply alone.** **The concept space is fine; today's copy is good and measured. The IMAGE space for custody arguments is spent.** Two honest routes: **(a) widen the visual vocabulary deliberately, by commissioning concepts whose objects are NOT custody objects, which means briefing the Content Factory to supply arguments that photograph differently; or (b) accept a lower new-concept rate on custody themes and spend more days on verticals of proven performers.** **FIRST ASKING.**
