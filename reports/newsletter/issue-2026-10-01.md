## (c) THE LEAD

**As of this morning, Connecticut's genetic-privacy provisions are in force.**

They are unusually strong. A consumer has a **property right** in their biological sample and in the genetic test results derived from it. Not a privacy interest. Property.

You can require that **the sample itself be destroyed**, which has always been a separate request from deleting your results. You can revoke consent. A company has to obtain express consent for collection, use, disclosure and retention, **and separately for secondary uses, third-party transfers and keeping your sample after the test is done**.

Here is the part almost nobody is mentioning today.

**There is no private right of action.** Enforcement runs solely through the Attorney General, under the state's unfair trade practices act, with reported penalties up to **$5,000 per willful violation** plus restitution and injunctive relief.

So the right is yours. The remedy is not. **If a company ignores your destruction request, you do not sue. You complain to one office.**

One detail makes that office more serious than it sounds: Connecticut's statutory **right to cure expired on 31 December 2024**. Most state privacy laws give a company a mandatory window to fix a violation after notice. This one no longer does.

**What to actually do with that:** ask in writing, ask for both things separately, and keep the reply. Your leverage is not a lawsuit. It is a documented refusal that an Attorney General can read.

*Epistemic status: law-firm and trade analysis of enacted text. We could not open the statute itself from our systems and we quote no clause.*

## (d) Three things worth knowing

**Science.** A number worth carrying into every health-tech headline you read: of **839 devices granted the FDA's Breakthrough Device Designation as of 30 June 2023, 81 had been cleared, approved or granted De Novo classification.** Roughly nine in ten had not reached the market. The designation buys priority and access to the agency. **It does not lower the standard for anything.** *Secondary analysis of agency data, current as of mid-2023, not re-verified by us.*
