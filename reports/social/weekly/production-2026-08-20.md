### Segment 1 — What the pilot actually is (facts first)
- **Quote (verbatim):** "The benefit will pay service members up to $500 per year for sperm retrieval and cryopreservation and $10,000 per year for egg preservation services." — Army Times, Aug 12, 2026. Source: https://www.armytimes.com/pay-benefits/2026/08/12/dod-to-launch-pilot-for-some-service-members-to-freeze-sperm-eggs/
- **Stance:** report it straight. Three-year pilot, begins Sept 14; eligibility = hazardous-duty orders (or likely within 120 days) or 180+ day separation from partner; covers military and civilian clinics; does NOT cover IVF or embryo creation (The Hill: https://thehill.com/policy/healthcare/6030762-military-eggs-sperm-freezing-reimbursement-program/; Federal News Network: https://federalnewsnetwork.com/benefits/2026/08/troops-can-get-up-to-10000-to-freeze-eggs-under-new-pilot/).
- **Talking points:** Congress required this in the FY2025 NDAA; the Pentagon will study participation, cost, and retention effects. This is preservation-as-benefit, tested at institutional scale.
- **CLIP MOMENT:** the cold-open "ten thousand versus five hundred" beat restated over the Federal Register screenshot.

### Segment 2 — Why the gap is (mostly) biology, not bias
- **Stance:** defuse, don't inflame. Egg retrieval is a surgical procedure: weeks of hormone injections, monitoring, anesthesia, an operating room; $10,000 to $15,000 per cycle is the widely cited US market range. Sperm cryopreservation is a clinic visit and a storage fee; a few hundred dollars is a real market price (Medical Daily framing of the cap: https://www.medicaldaily.com/pentagon-fertility-preservation-pilot-egg-sperm-freezing-reimbursement-477478).
- **Talking points:** the caps roughly track procedure cost, not human worth; the sharper critique some advocates make is the opposite one, that $500 may still undershoot first-year banking costs at some clinics. Acknowledge, cite, move on.
- **Never:** mock either side of the gender fight; no service-member jokes.
- **CLIP MOMENT:** "The Pentagon didn't price men and women. It priced two procedures."

### Segment 3 — The unspoken admission
- **Stance:** this is the segment we exist for. Institutions act when logic becomes undeniable: before exposure to risk, preserve what cannot be recreated. The military's risk is deployment. Civilians' risks are quieter: illness, accidents, chemotherapy, time itself.
- **Talking points:** connect to ASRM's 2026 fertility-preservation committee opinion (preservation programs are now standard-of-care infrastructure, not exotic); note the government is ALSO cryobanking endangered-species DNA at scale with Colossal's BioVault (2,300+ species, USFWS partnership, Gizmodo: https://gizmodo.com/america-is-creating-a-dna-noahs-ark-for-more-than-2300-endangered-species-2000777344). Pattern: institutions everywhere are backing up biology.
- **Honest line:** gamete freezing is fertility preservation; DNA banking is record preservation. Different jobs. We do the second. Say it plainly.
- **CLIP MOMENT:** "The government backs up soldiers before deployment and species before extinction. Nobody's backing up you."

### Segment 4 — What this costs you (and the custody question)
- **Stance:** practical close. If the logic moved you: fertility preservation is a medical decision, talk to a clinic, some employers now cover it. DNA preservation is the accessible layer: $99 once, sample stored at an accredited, FDA-registered biorepository in New Jersey, chain of custody you can name. Contrast with app-company DNA (23andMe: 15M customers' data through a bankruptcy; keep it to one line, it's had full episodes).
