### SEGMENT 1: The four rungs, and why each one exists (5 to 6 min)

**Source:** our own competitor file, `reports/competitors/watch-2026-09-30.md`, dated today.
**EPISTEMIC LABEL: search-index material. The provider's own pages were NOT opened, because general-web fetching has been refused from our network for four consecutive days. NO COMPANY IS NAMED ON AIR AND NO FIGURE FROM THAT FILE IS SPOKEN.**

**Stance:** describe the machine, do not accuse the operator.

**Talking points.**
- **Rung one, the annual fee.** Lowest barrier, highest lifetime cost, and the one that keeps a relationship alive.
- **Rung two, up front plus annual.** A larger opening payment that buys a lower recurring one. **This is the rung most often promoted, because it looks like commitment and reads as a discount.**
- **Rung three, prepaid term.** Pay for a fixed number of years and stop thinking about it. **Converts renewal risk into counterparty risk, which is a real trade and not a trick.**
- **Rung four, lifetime.** The largest single number and the one most likely to carry a headline percentage next to it.
- **WHY IT IS NOT A SCAM, said clearly and early:** each rung solves a different problem for a different household. **A company with four rungs is doing more work than one with one.**
- **The cost of four rungs is comparability.** You cannot line two providers up against each other when each has four prices and you met a different one on each site.

**CLIP MOMENT (mark):** "You are not being told a false number. You are being shown one of several true ones, and nobody tells you which shelf you are standing in front of."

### SEGMENT 2: Why the promoted rung is almost never the plain one (4 to 5 min)

**Stance:** a structural observation, not a motive claim.

**Talking points.**

- **The honest test for the listener:** find the page that is NOT linked from the homepage. **Terms pages, prepaid pages and fee schedules are where the plain number lives.**
- **And the counterweight, because it is fair:** a company that leads with its highest number would be a company nobody calls. **Every business shows its best foot. The skill is knowing there are other feet.**

**CLIP MOMENT (mark):** "Find the page nobody links to from the homepage. That is where the plain number lives."

## 2. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER (6 to 10 min)

### "Do Your Experiences Get Passed Down? The Honest Version"

**Freshness check:** grepped `production-log.md` and the entire blog archive for **transgenerational (0 hits both)**, **"epigenetic inherit" (0)**, **imprint (0)**, **"Dutch Hunger" (0)**. The fleet has explainers on **partial reprogramming (12 Sep)**, **cellular reprogramming**, **methylation-based age clocks (4 Sep)** and **genetic nurture (13 Sep)**. **None is about whether acquired marks cross generations.** Confirmed new.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55.
**Trend-hook used:** none, and it is declared rather than invented. **This is a standing claim that circulates constantly in the legacy and wellness space, and it is the single easiest thing for a company like ours to be accused of implying.**

**WHY WE ARE MAKING IT: because the overclaim is adjacent to our own product and we would rather be the ones who say it is not true.**

**B-ROLL AND STAGING.** Locked-off desk. **No family photographs, no children, no hands on bellies, no generational montage.** Those images would do the argument's opposite job. **Plain type cards only.**

**SCRIPT BEATS.**

**Open (0:00 to 0:50).** "You have heard some version of this: what happened to your grandfather is written into you. Famine, trauma, hardship, passed down in the biology rather than the family stories. **It is one of the most repeated ideas in this space and the honest answer is that in humans it is not established.** Here is what is actually known."

**Beat 1, what epigenetics IS (0:50 to 2:20).** Chemical marks that sit ON DNA and change which genes are read, without changing the sequence. **DNA methylation is the best known.** **This part is not in dispute. Epigenetic marks are real, they respond to environment, and they matter enormously within one lifetime.**
**ON SCREEN: "REAL WITHIN A LIFETIME" and "CONTESTED BETWEEN GENERATIONS" as two separate cards.**

**Beat 2, the erasure problem (2:20 to 4:00).** **This is the mechanism and it is the whole explainer.** Mammals run **two rounds of epigenetic reprogramming**: one in the early embryo and one in the germ line. **Both rounds erase marks.** Reviews describe that pair of erasures as leaving **little chance for inheritance of epigenetic marks, whether programmed, accidental or environmentally induced.**
**Say the implication plainly: for an acquired mark to reach a grandchild, it has to survive being deliberately wiped twice.**

**Beat 3, where it HAS been shown, and where it has not (4:00 to 5:30).** **Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance has been observed in plants, nematodes and fruit flies.** **In mammals, and humans in particular, it is described in the literature as a matter of controversial debate.**
**The reason is not squeamishness. It is confounding.** Human studies cannot easily separate epigenetic inheritance from **genetic, ecological and cultural inheritance.** **Your grandfather's hardship also changed the house you grew up in, the money in it, and the way your parent was raised.** **Those are real mechanisms of transmission. They are not epigenetic ones.**

**Beat 4, the honest exception (5:30 to 6:30).** **Not everything points one way.** Reviews note that while DNA methylation alterations are reset during reprogramming, **non-coding RNAs in human sperm have been proposed as candidate carriers.** **Label that clearly on screen: PROPOSED MECHANISM, NOT ESTABLISHED INHERITANCE.**

**Beat 5, sources and their ages, stated on camera (6:30 to 7:30).** **"The critical review I am leaning on most is in *Nature Communications* and it is from 2018. The other material is 2013 to 2021. None of it is from this year."** **Say the years out loud.** **Our own rule, and this week we flagged a paper hitting its first anniversary for exactly this reason: an old result can arrive looking new, and the fix is to say the date.**

**Beat 6, preserve-now close without the overclaim (7:30 to 8:30).** **"So what does travel? The sequence."** Your genome is copied and passed on; that part is not contested. **"What does not reliably travel is what happened to you."**
**And therefore the honest product line:** **we store a readable copy of a genome.** **We do not store experience, we make no claim that anything you have lived through is written into anybody, and storage confers no health benefit.** **$99 a year, billed annually.**
**"If you want your experience to reach somebody, the mechanism is not biological. It is a recording with a date on it."** Memory Vault, video message.

**HONEST LABELING BLOCK, read on camera.** "Everything here comes from peer-reviewed reviews I could not open at their own journals today; my network has blocked general web fetching for four days. **I am reporting the direction of the literature and not quoting any of it.** The specific claim that acquired experience reaches grandchildren in humans is **not established**, and anyone telling you otherwise is ahead of the evidence."

**SOURCES.** *Nature Communications* 2018, "A critical view on transgenerational epigenetic inheritance in humans"; *Biochemical Genetics* 2021 review on epigenetic inheritance of experiences in humans; *Biology of Reproduction* 2021 on germline development; earlier PMC reviews on germ-cell reprogramming. **ALL index-level, none opened, none quoted.**
