### SEGMENT 1 — What actually happened, without softening it (4-5 min)
**Receipts:**
- **RTR242 is not a reprogramming candidate.** It is a small-molecule therapy targeting **autophagy via lysosomal function**, aimed at Alzheimer's and brain aging. Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled **Phase 1 in healthy volunteers in Australia**, with exploratory autophagy and lysosomal biomarkers. Retro became clinical-stage in **2025**. *(Company and trade sourcing — Longevity.Technology, Fight Aging!, Retro's own pipeline page. Not peer-reviewed.)*
- **What survived the correction:** no reprogramming candidate has published human data, and NewLimit's first human study is expected **2027**. Those were right.
- **What died:** the generalisation.

**Stance:** state it flatly, once, and move to the mechanism. Do not perform contrition — the audience did not lose anything, because nothing had been published or run when it was caught.
**CLIP MOMENT:** "I contradicted myself inside one article and didn't notice, because the sentence sounded good."

### SEGMENT 2 — Why good sentences evade checking (5-6 min)
**Talking points:**
- A fact-check tests **propositions**. A well-formed sentence smuggles a **generalisation** on the back of a true particular. "NewLimit's first study is 2027" is checkable and true. "None of the dates is now" is a quantifier over a set I never enumerated.
- **Parallelism is an anaesthetic.** "Every X has a Y, and none of the Y is Z" is a rhythm, and rhythm reads as authority. You check the parts you can see; the shape hides the claim.
- The two gates that passed it were both **local** checks — is this fresh, is this sourced. Neither asked the global question: *is the set complete?*
- This is the same failure that makes bad science writing persuasive, and it is why "no study has ever shown" and "there is no evidence that" are the two most dangerous phrases in the genre. They are quantifiers over unenumerated sets, and almost nobody can check them.

**Stance:** generalise outward fast. The episode is worth the audience's time only if it hands them a tool, not a confession.
**CLIP MOMENT:** "A fact-check tests the parts you can see. The shape of the sentence hides the claim."

### SEGMENT 3 — The three-bucket tool (4-5 min)
Every science claim goes into one of three buckets before you feel anything about it:
1. **Published result** — and in what, and in which species.
2. **Running, no result** — a trial is a question, not an answer.
3. **Money** — a raise, a valuation, an efficiency announcement. Not evidence.
Most of what excites people is bucket three wearing bucket one's clothes. **And the rule I broke:** a claim that no item exists in a bucket requires you to have looked in the bucket.
**CLIP MOMENT:** "A trial is a question, not an answer. A valuation isn't even a question."

### SEGMENT 4 — Why we publish this at all (2-3 min)

The only evidence a stranger has about whether we will behave in year eleven is how we behave now, in public, when it costs us something. An ad had to be re-rendered. That is the cost, and it is small, and paying it visibly is the entire product.
**OUTRO CTA:** "No product ask. If you want the three buckets as a card, it's on the blog. And if you catch us in one of these, send it — the fastest way to be trusted is to be correctable in public."

# 2. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER (7-8 min)
## "Autophagy: The Cellular Garbage System Everyone Is Suddenly Betting On"

**Freshness check:** Never produced. The log's science explainers cover base editing, meiosis, the
second genome, sample-type stability, hachimoji DNA, ambient storage, the paternal clock, IVG
definitions, polygenic-score method, biobank accreditation, cord-blood economics, partial
reprogramming (Sep 12) and genetic nurture (Sep 13). **Autophagy has never been explained here**, and
it is the mechanism behind the trial that corrected us this morning.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55.**
**Trend-hook used:** Retro Biosciences' RTR242 in Phase 1 — the candidate whose existence falsified
our own published line yesterday.

**SCRIPT**

[B-ROLL: a kitchen bin being emptied, then the same bin left un-emptied across a time-lapse.]

"Your cells take out the garbage. There is a name for it, it won a Nobel Prize, and it is the reason several well-funded companies think they can do something about brain aging.

**The basics.** [ON SCREEN: AUTOPHAGY — 'SELF-EATING'] Autophagy is the process by which a cell breaks down and recycles its own damaged parts: misfolded proteins, worn-out organelles, general cellular debris. The cell wraps the rubbish in a membrane and delivers it to a **lysosome**, which is essentially an acid bath full of enzymes. The acid is the point. Without the right acidity the enzymes don't work and the rubbish doesn't get broken down.

[ON SCREEN: NOBEL PRIZE IN PHYSIOLOGY OR MEDICINE, 2016]

**This is not fringe science.** Yoshinori Ohsumi won the 2016 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for his discoveries of the mechanisms of autophagy, work that started with a series of yeast experiments in the early 1990s and identified the genes that run the process. That is about as established as biology gets.

**What goes wrong with age.** As cells age, and particularly in neurodegenerative disease, **lysosomes lose acidity and efficiency.** The bin stops being emptied. Damaged proteins accumulate. In Alzheimer's specifically, protein accumulation is the thing everyone has been trying to treat for decades — mostly by attacking the proteins downstream.

[B-ROLL: back to the un-emptied bin.]

**The bet.** [ON SCREEN: HYPOTHESIS, NOT RESULT] Instead of chasing the rubbish, repair the bin. Retro Biosciences' RTR242 is a small molecule designed to restore lysosomal function — reactivating the cell's own cleanup machinery rather than targeting the accumulation downstream.

**Where it actually is.** [ON SCREEN: PHASE 1 — HEALTHY VOLUNTEERS — NO RESULT] A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 1 in healthy volunteers, run at an early-phase unit in Australia, with exploratory biomarkers tied to autophagy and lysosomal biology. Retro became a clinical-stage company in 2025.

Now, precisely, what that means and does not mean. A Phase 1 in healthy volunteers is primarily a **safety** study. Its job is to find out whether the drug is tolerated and what it does to measurable biology — not whether it treats Alzheimer's. Nobody will learn from this trial whether the idea works. They will learn whether it is safe enough to ask that question later.

**A correction I owe you, in this video, because it is the same subject.** [ON SCREEN: CORRECTION] Two days ago I said in another video that every longevity bet has a date and none of the dates is now. That was wrong, and this trial is why. Several candidates are in human trials right now. **Not one of them has a published result** — which is the true version, and it is the version I should have written the first time. No reprogramming candidate has human data at all; that part was right.

[B-ROLL: the bin, emptied.]

**What this has to do with us — including the limit.** Nothing in this field operates on a record. Autophagy therapies act on living cells in a living person; they are not retroactive, and a stored DNA sample is not a patient. Preserving a record does not put you in a trial, does not get you a treatment, and does not make you a candidate for anything. Anyone in my industry implying otherwise is selling you something I won't.

What a record does is narrow and certain: the information about who you were, biologically, still exists later. Seven minutes at a kitchen table, $99 a year, stored at an accredited independent facility. The clever people are working on the bin. I'm just making sure the paperwork survives."

**Honest-labeling summary for the description:** Ohsumi's 2016 Nobel — established. Lysosomal acidity decline with age and in neurodegeneration — established mechanism. RTR242's approach and Phase 1 status — **company and trade sourcing, not peer-reviewed**. No efficacy claim is made, and a Phase 1 in healthy volunteers is identified as a safety study.
