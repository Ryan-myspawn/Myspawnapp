# 2. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER (6-8 min)

## "How To Check A Citation Somebody Hands You"

**NEW AXIS, AND IT IS THE THIRD DISTINCT VERIFICATION AXIS RATHER THAN A REPEAT OF THE OTHER TWO.** 24 October asked **does the evidence exist**. 25 October asked **who was it measured on**. **This asks whether the ADDRESS given for the evidence is real.** **Greps: `check a citation` 0, `pinpoint` 1 across `reports/social/weekly/`.**

**THE WORKED EXAMPLE IS OUR OWN NEAR-MISS FROM YESTERDAY, which is what makes it teachable rather than preachy.**

## Script

**[B-ROLL: a plain text document on screen, one line highlighted.]**
"Yesterday we were writing about how long probate takes. A source gave us a rule and a citation: California's creditor claim window, and a section number, Probate Code section 19100.

The rule was right. The section number was wrong, and it was wrong in a way that would have survived every normal check."

**[B-ROLL: two document headers side by side, no legible body text.]**
"Section 19100 exists. It is real law. It governs claims against **trusts**. The rule we were describing applies to claims against a **decedent's estate in probate**. Those are two different proceedings in the same code, and a number that belongs to one looks completely at home in the other.

**Nothing about the citation looks wrong. It is correctly formatted, it points at a real provision, and the provision is topically adjacent.** That is the whole problem."

**[B-ROLL: presenter, static.]**
"So here is the check, and it takes about ninety seconds.

**One. Does the cited thing exist?** Most bad citations die here. This one survived.

**Two. Does it say what the citing source claims it says?** This is the step almost everyone skips, because step one feels like verification and it is not.

**Three. Is it in the right division of the code, the right volume of the journal, the right year?** Adjacent provisions are where real citations go wrong, because the error is invisible to anyone checking only step one.

**Four. If you cannot complete steps two and three, say so in your own text.** Not in a footnote. In the sentence where you use it."

**[B-ROLL: text card.]**
"That last one is the part people resist, because it looks like weakness. It is the opposite. **A source that tells you which of its own claims it could not fully verify is giving you a map of where to push.** A source that verifies nothing and flags nothing is asking you to trust its filing system."

**[B-ROLL: presenter, final.]**
"We published that probate article with the rule stated and the section number explicitly disclaimed, and a line telling anyone citing us not to repeat it.

**That cost us a clean-looking citation. It is worth more than one.**

Nothing here is legal advice and I am not a lawyer. **If a section number matters to your situation, that is exactly the circumstance in which you should be paying one.**"

**HONEST LABELLING, SPOKEN: this explainer makes no scientific claim and cites no study. Its only factual content is about a citation we ourselves nearly got wrong, and the correction is already published.**
