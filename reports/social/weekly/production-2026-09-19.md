### Segment 4: What to actually do, if you use any of these (3-4 min)

**Practical, no product:**
1. **Export now, from everything.** Not when you get a notice. The notice is the end of the process, not the start.
2. **Ask where the export button is before you buy.** If the answer is "contact support," that is your answer.
3. **Keep the original files.** A service that transcribes, formats or enhances your recordings is doing something to a copy. Keep the thing it started with.
4. **Check the app's last-updated date.** It is public, it takes ten seconds, and it is the most honest health metric a small company has.

**Stance:** this advice is bad for our category and good for the listener. Say that.

## 2. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER (6-10 min)

### "How to Read a Supplement Study in Four Minutes"

**Freshness check:** The 17 September autophagy explainer contained a supplement section comparing urolithin A and spermidine. **This is a different thing: the subject is the grading method, not a compound**, and it uses plasmalogens as the worked example because the plasmalogen literature happens to contain a null trial, a positive trial and a retraction, which is the whole curriculum in one topic. No explainer has taught evidence grading.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55
**Trend / hook used:** A wave of "seafood reverses aging" coverage on 2 September 2026 sitting on top of a 2022 paper and a 2026 retraction.

**Script beats with B-roll directions:**

**[COLD OPEN: hands, desk, a supplement bottle turned so the label is not readable.]**
> I am going to teach you to do in four minutes what I spent a morning doing. The example is a supplement called plasmalogens. The method works on all of them.

**[B-ROLL: screen recording, a search result page.]**
> **Question one: when was it published?** In early September, a lot of coverage said scientists had found a compound in edible sea squirts that reverses signs of aging in old mice. True. Real research, reported accurately. **Published in 2022.** *(Front Mol Biosci, 10.3389/fmolb.2022.815320, peer-reviewed, mice.)* A four-year-old result recirculating tells you the field is quiet, not that it is moving.

**[B-ROLL: a journal abstract on screen, the participant number highlighted.]**
> **Question two: how many people, and what kind?** The biggest human trial here is Fujino and colleagues in *eBioMedicine*, 2017. **328 participants, ages 60 to 85**, mild Alzheimer's or mild cognitive impairment, **1 mg a day, 24 weeks**, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled. That is a serious trial.

**[B-ROLL: the results section, primary outcome highlighted.]**
> **Question three, and it is the one that decides everything: did it meet its primary endpoint?** No. **No significant difference from placebo on the primary outcome, and in the intention-to-treat analysis, none on any secondary outcome either.** The improvement you will see quoted on supplement pages is from a **subgroup analysis**. A subgroup finding in a trial that missed its primary endpoint is a hypothesis for the next study. Split a dataset enough ways and something looks positive.

**[B-ROLL: second paper on screen.]**
> There is a newer trial, and it is positive. *Frontiers in Nutrition*, 2026: **68 cognitively healthy adults over 40 with below-average verbal memory, 1 mg a day, 12 weeks**, verbal memory improved against placebo. *(Peer-reviewed.)* Sixty-eight people, twelve weeks, a selected group, one measure. **That is a reason to run a bigger trial, not a reason to be sure.**

**[B-ROLL: a retraction notice on screen.]**
> **Question four, the one almost nobody asks: is every paper still standing?** In January 2026 a journal issued an Expression of Concern about a human plasmalogen study, citing serious concerns about compliance with local legislation. **It was subsequently retracted.** *(Publisher notices; read the journal's own notice.)* Not one piece of the coverage I read mentioned it.

**[ON SCREEN: the four questions, listed.]**
> When was it published. How many people. Did it meet its primary endpoint. Is every paper still standing.

**[TO CAMERA, plain background.]**
> **The honest verdict on plasmalogens**, since you came for one: the mechanism is real, they are ether phospholipids concentrated in the brain and heart and they decline with age. The animal data is interesting. The human data is one null trial, one small positive trial, and a retraction. **The dose in both major trials is the same, 1 mg a day**, which is a useful thing to hold a product against. **Nobody has shown they extend human life.** Take them as an experiment with a modest prior, or do not. I am not selling them.

**Preserve-now close, without overpromising:**
> **I sell storage and it has nothing to do with any of this.** Keeping a genetic record is not a treatment, confers no health benefit and grants no access to any trial. **The only honest connection is the method.** The reason we publish where samples are kept and who keeps them is the same reason those four questions work: **a claim you can check is a different kind of claim.** Use them on us.

## 3. EVERGREEN BANK (new format)

**Format: "Backwards From Five."** The presenter states the conclusion first, then counts **down** from five to one, each number a step that makes the previous one true. **No format in the archive is structured as a reverse argument.** Recent evergreens checked individually: mirror-shot (12th), whiteboard (14th), wall-calendar (15th), Flop-Core (16th), retail-aisle-walk (17th), Read It Out Loud (18th), Cards Face Down (18th PM).

- **Hook (first 2 seconds):** "You are not buying a tube. Here are the five things you are actually buying, counting backwards."
- **Beats:**
  1. **Open on the conclusion:** "What you buy is a date that works."
  2. **Five:** a physical sample, collected correctly. Without that there is nothing. Hair and nails, seven minutes.
  3. **Four:** a facility. An independently accredited third-party biorepository, not a drawer.
  4. **Three:** a contract. What they may do with it, what happens if you stop paying, who can ask for it back.
  5. **Two:** a named person. A record with no recipient is a filing cabinet.
  6. **One:** an institution still standing on the day it matters. "This is the one nobody sells you, because it is the one nobody can promise. It is also the only one that decides whether the other four meant anything."
  7. Close: "Four of those you can check today. Ask anybody selling you the fifth how they intend to earn it."
- **Why it works for men 25-55:** it is a spec sheet, not a feeling, and it ends by conceding the weakest point rather than hiding it.
- **Risk flag:** beat 6 must not imply a guarantee. The wording above is the approved wording.
