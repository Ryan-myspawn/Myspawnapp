## 1. PODCAST EPISODE PACK

### Titles (3 options)
1. **"They Didn't Want It For After"**
2. **"The Man Who Built It For the Dead and Found the Living"**
3. **"We Were Wrong About the Graveyard"**

*Recommendation: option 1 for the platform. Option 3 is honest but points at us rather than at the finding, and the finding is bigger.*

### Cold open (word-for-word, 60-90 seconds)

> Last week I told you this category was a graveyard. Four companies built to keep people's memories, shut down or gone quiet in about two years. It was a good line and I believed it.
>
> I spent the last two days checking them one at a time, because the list came from a competitor writing about its departed rivals, and that is not a source you take on trust.
>
> Two of the four were not failures.
>
> One filed for Chapter 11 in May 2024 and came out the other side when an investor bought the assets. It is running in museums now.

>
> They wanted to use it while they were alive.
>
> That company raised ten point three million dollars in November 2025, changed its name, and went to serve the customers it actually had. I sell a product built on the other assumption. So today is not a correction episode. It is the question that finding puts to me.

### Segment 1: How we got it wrong, in public (4-5 min)

**Talking points:**
- The four-company list came from **afterlife.ai, a company in the same category, writing about rivals that had left it.** We labeled the conflict at the time and used the claims anyway.
- Then we checked them **one company per day**, which is the only reason we caught it.
- **Two of four were wrong, and wrong in a consistent direction: toward the category looking like a graveyard.** A conflicted source was not merely biased. It was inaccurate in its own favor.
- **The rule we wrote down as a result:** a competitor's account of other competitors gets verified company by company **before** an argument is built on the aggregate.

**Stance:** own it plainly, do not perform contrition, move to the interesting part inside four minutes.

**[CLIP MOMENT]** "A conflicted source was not just biased. It was wrong in its own favor, twice out of four."

### Segment 2: The finding (6-7 min)

**The facts:**
- **Eternos**, founded to preserve voices and life stories for after death. First client **Michael Bommer** created a digital replica of himself before dying in **2024**.
- Founder **Rob LoCascio**, who previously founded **LivePerson**, observed that **most users were not preparing for death; they wanted to use the technology while alive.**
- **November 2025:** rebrand to **Uare.ai**, **$10.3M seed** from **Mayfield** and **Boldstart Ventures**. New product: creators and professionals scaling expertise through AI replicas, built on a **"Human Life Model"** trained only on the user's own stories, memories and professional background rather than a general-purpose model, via text, audio and video. *(Trade press and company materials, TechCrunch 11 November 2025. **Verify before recording.**)*

**Why it matters beyond one company:** this is not a survey or an opinion. **It is what happened when somebody built the posthumous product and watched who turned up.**

**Stance:** take it seriously as evidence about our own assumptions, without treating one company's pivot as proof about everyone.

**The honest counterweight, which must stay in:** **Uare.ai's new customers are professionals buying a work tool.** That is a different market, not the same market wanting a different timing. **One company's pivot is a hypothesis, not a finding about demand for legacy products generally.**

**[CLIP MOMENT]** "He built it for the dead and the living turned up."

### Segment 3: The question it puts to us (4-5 min)

**Talking points:**
- We sell **a date in the future** and **a sample held for decades**. Both are posthumous framings.
- **The Memory Vault already has a living use and we barely talk about it:** you record a message for a sixteenth birthday, and you can sit and watch it with them on the day. **Nobody has to be dead for that to work.**
- **ECHO** is the same shape: a thing you use now.
- **The uncomfortable version:** if the demand is mostly among the living, then our best product may be the one we describe least, and we may have been selling the rarer motivation because it is the more dramatic one.

**Stance:** raise it as an open question about our own marketing, on air, without announcing a pivot we have not made.

**Say this, and not more than this:** *"I am not announcing a change. I am telling you what I read this week and that it lands on my own assumptions. If you bought from us for the posthumous version, nothing about your account changes."*

**[CLIP MOMENT]** "We may have been selling the rarer motivation because it is the more dramatic one."

### Segment 4: What is still true (3-4 min)

**Because a correction is not a collapse.**
- **One company did wind down**, and its stated retrieval route is a support email with no self-serve export. **That is still real.**
- **"The money comes in once. The obligation lasts forever."** Still true, untouched by any of this.
- **"Everybody asks what happens if they shut down. Almost nobody asks who is allowed to buy them."** Still true, and **strengthened**: one of our four was acquired and one was bought out of bankruptcy. **Transfer is now the better-evidenced risk than closure.**

**[CLIP MOMENT]** "The correction did not weaken the argument about transfers. It is the only part that got stronger."

## 2. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER (6-10 min)

### "There Are 233 Ways to Compute Your Age"

**Freshness check:** Off hold as of this morning. Two short-form scripts on 17 September used this study; **neither is an explainer and neither used the 233 figure**, which we did not have then. The angle is inverted: those were about six clocks agreeing inside a trial, this is about what it means that 233 exist.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55
**Trend / hook used:** A peer-reviewed *Cell* cover study released a system that computes biological age 233 different ways.

**Beats with B-roll:**

**[COLD OPEN: to camera.]**
> If somebody sells you your biological age, ask them which clock. There are at least 233 of them.

**[B-ROLL: the paper on screen.]**
> In September 2026, **Insilico Medicine** published a *Cell* cover study and released an open longevity AI toolkit. Inside it is an agentic platform that **predicts biological age across 233 clocks spanning six biological modalities**, using **429,165 coefficients**. Collaborators reported include **Liquid AI**, the **Buck Institute for Research on Aging**, and **Harvard Medical School / Brigham and Women's Hospital**. *(The *Cell* study is peer-reviewed.)*

**[TO CAMERA.]**
> The obvious question is which one is right. **The better question is what it means that there are 233.**

**[B-ROLL: simple diagram, many lines converging loosely.]**
> A biological-age clock is a model. You feed it measurements, methylation, proteins, blood markers, and it returns a number calibrated against a population. **Change the inputs, the training set or the target, and you get a different clock and a different number for the same person on the same day.**

**[B-ROLL: contrast shot.]**
> **Here is where it gets useful rather than depressing.** Earlier in September, *Nature Biotechnology* published a **Phase 2a** trial that built **six independently developed proteomic aging clocks** into the analysis as exploratory endpoints. **All six moved the same way.** The largest effect was at week four in the 30mg twice-daily arm, putting treated patients **2.7 to 3.5 years younger than expected.** **42 patients. Four weeks. A trial for a lung disease.** *(Peer-reviewed.)*

**[ON SCREEN: YOUNGER BIOMARKERS. NOT YOUNGER PATIENTS.]**
> That is **younger biomarkers, not younger patients.** A protein panel reading younger is a measurement of a proxy.

**[TO CAMERA.]**
> **So here is the distinction worth keeping.** Six independently built clocks, cross-checked inside a controlled trial, agreeing with each other, is a **measurement**. One clock, sold to you as a consumer product, with no cross-check and no control group, is an **opinion with a price.** Same tool. Completely different use.

**[HONEST LIMITS.]**
> **What I am not telling you:** that these clocks are worthless, or that yours is wrong. Clocks are how this field will eventually measure whether anything works. **The problem is not the science. It is the retail layer built on top of it before the science settled.**

**Preserve-now close:**
> **We do not measure your biological age. We do not affect it. We will never sell you a number about it.** We keep a record, and a record is not a score.
>
> **The reusable part:** when anybody shows you a number about your body, ask **which model, who built it, and compared to whom.** Three questions. They work on every product in this category, and they work on us.

## 3. EVERGREEN BANK (new format)

**Format: "Do It Badly, On Camera."** The presenter records an actual milestone message in one unbroken take, fumbles it, and **keeps the fumble**. **No format in the archive shows the product being used rather than explained.** Recent evergreens checked individually and excluded: Read It Out Loud (18th), Cards Face Down (18th PM), Black Screen (19th, now killed), Overlay Audit (19th), Backwards From Five (19th), The Ladder (19th PM), Two Glasses (20th), Find The Word (20th), One Object Three Owners (20th), The Shoebox Test (20th PM).

- **Hook (first 2 seconds):** "I have been telling people to record these for a month. I had not done one."
- **Beats:**
  1. Phone propped up. Presenter states the recipient and the unlock date out loud, plainly. No music.
  2. Starts. Gets four words in. Stops. "That was awful."
  3. Starts again. Gets further. A pause that runs too long.
  4. Finishes it anyway. Does not re-record.
  5. To camera: "Ninety seconds. Two false starts. That is the actual bar, and the bar being low is the entire point."
  6. "The reason people do not do this is that they are waiting to be good at it. You will not get good at it. You will get old."
  7. Close: "And you do not have to be gone for someone to watch it. Record it for a birthday and sit next to them while they play it."
- **Why it works for men 25-55:** it is competence-adjacent rather than sentimental. The demonstration is of **lowering a bar**, not of feeling something.
- **Risk flag:** **the recorded message must be genuine or clearly staged, never a fake message to a real named person.** **No child's face or name on camera.** Beat 7 carries the living-use framing from today's episode and should not be cut.
