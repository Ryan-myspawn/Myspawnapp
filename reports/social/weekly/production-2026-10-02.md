## 1. PODCAST EPISODE PACK

**Freshness check:** grepped `production-log.md`. **"Legacy contact" returns ONE hit and it was opened rather than counted: the 18 September episode "Custody Without Access"**, whose spine is that a legacy contact gets custody but **cannot log in, edit, remove posts, read messages or remove friends**, generalized in segment 2 across four named platforms. **"Digital executor" returns zero.**
**THE NEW MATERIAL IS A COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THAT EPISODE'S SPINE, AND THAT IS WHY IT IS NOT A REPEAT.** A fifth platform is reported to give its legacy contact **full account access and ownership of the account including all personal information in it.** **That is the opposite of custody without access**, and **the platform reported to do it is the one holding genetic data.**
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55, and it reaches the 40-60 estate-minded half directly.
**Trend-hook used:** this morning's platform sweep, 2 October.

### Titles (3 options)
1. **"Three Platforms, One Phrase"** (preferred: it states the problem without needing a company name)
2. "Your Legacy Contact Might Own The Account"
3. "Custody, Access, Ownership: Pick One"

### COLD OPEN (word for word, 60 to 90 seconds)

> Two words show up in the account settings of almost everything you use. **Legacy contact.** You nominate somebody, you feel like you have done a sensible thing, and you move on.
>
> **Those two words buy three completely different powers depending on which company printed them.**
>
> On one platform your person can post for you, manage the profile, download an archive, **and cannot log in and cannot read a single private message.** They are a caretaker with the door locked.
>
> On another they need **a key you handed them while you were alive, plus a death certificate**, and the access they get is reported to be time-limited.
>
> **And on a third, they are reported not to get access at all. They are reported to get the account.** Ownership. Everything personal inside it.
>
> **The third one holds genetic data.**
>
> **"Legacy contact" is a marketing phrase, not a legal standard.** Three companies used it for three different things and nobody is obliged to tell you which kind you signed up for.

### SEGMENT 1: Custody, and what it deliberately withholds (4 to 5 minutes)

**Stance:** describe the mechanism, name nobody.

- The restrictive model: **post to the timeline, respond to friend requests, update the profile and cover photo, download an archive of photos and posts.** **No login. No private messages.**
- **Marked clip moment:** *"They can speak for you in public and they cannot read anything you said in private. That is a deliberate design and it is a defensible one."*
- **Talking point:** the design protects **third parties**. Your private messages contain other people's words, and those people never agreed to anything.

### SEGMENT 2: The key and the clock (4 to 5 minutes)

**Stance:** mechanism plus an honest refusal.

- The gated model: **up to five contacts, a pre-shared access key generated while you are alive, and a death certificate.** Reported access to photos, messages, notes and files.
- **The reported time limit is NOT stated on camera in any form.** **BINDING: say "finite" and move on.** The figure has been blocked in our files since 25 September pending the company's own documentation, **and it picked up two more secondary sources this week without clearing**, because corroboration is not verification.
- **Marked clip moment:** *"I know a number for this. I am not going to say it, because the only source that would settle it is the one I have not been able to open."*
- **Talking point:** the pre-shared key is the interesting part. **It is the only model here that requires an action from you AND an artefact held by them.**

### SEGMENT 3: Ownership, and why the exception is the story (5 to 6 minutes)

**Stance:** this is the payoff and it must be delivered with its uncertainty attached.

- The third model, **reported**: on verification of death, the contact **receives full account access and assumes ownership of the account including all personal information contained within it.**
- **Say the epistemic status out loud, in the segment, not in the description:** this comes from a provision record of a published privacy statement, **read through a search index. We did not open the company's own page.**
- **Marked clip moment:** *"Custody and ownership are not degrees of the same thing. One of them ends. The other one transfers, and then it is theirs to decide about."*
- **The practical turn, which is the part a listener uses:** **the question to ask of your own nominations is not "have I named someone". It is "what does the specific company I named them with actually let them do".**
- **The paragraph that qualifies our own earlier work, and it is one paragraph:** **we made an episode on 18 September whose argument was that a legacy contact gets custody without access.** **That generalization was drawn from four platforms and this is a fifth that appears not to fit it.** **We are not withdrawing the episode, because the four it described still behave that way. We are saying the generalization was narrower than it sounded.**

### SEGMENT 4: What to actually do, in ten minutes (3 to 4 minutes)

1. **List the accounts that hold something irreplaceable.** Photos, recordings, genetic results. Usually three or four, not thirty.
2. **For each one, find the legacy or inactive-account setting and read what the contact can do.** Not whether the feature exists. **What it permits.**
3. **Write the answers down in one place**, because the person you nominated will not know which model applies.
4. **Tell them.** **A nomination nobody knows about is a nomination that fails at the only moment it matters.**
- **Marked clip moment:** *"Ten minutes, four accounts, and the only hard part is reading instead of assuming."*

### OUTRO CTA

**Zero product.** "We keep a written record of what we can and cannot verify, and the unverified things stay unverified out loud. If you want the long version of any of this, it is written down."

2. **Segment 2's refusal**: *"I know a number for this. I am not going to say it."*
3. **Segment 4's four steps**, cut tight.

### BINDING FLAGS

## 2. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER (6 to 10 min): "The Clause That Decides"

**Freshness check:** **"Embryo disposition", "clinic clos" and "abandoned embryo" all return ZERO in `production-log.md`**, and this morning's blog article on the same subject is its only sibling. **This is the two-channel treatment that worked for the FDA vocabulary on 29 September and 1 October**, and it is deliberate rather than accidental.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55; this one reaches couples who have been through IVF, which is a group our channel has never addressed directly.
**Trend-hook used:** none, and stated. **The mechanism is evergreen and the article was written today.**

### Script with B-roll

- **[COLD, 0:00 to 0:40]** [B-ROLL: a printed consent form on a table, pages turning, no face.] **"If the clinic holding your embryos closes, the decision has already been made. You made it, probably years ago, in a document you signed before anything was frozen."**
- **[0:40 to 1:40] What the document is.** [B-ROLL: close-up of a checkbox grid, generic, not a real form.] It is the **consent to disposition of cryopreserved embryos**. It asks you to choose, in advance, what happens in each of a list of circumstances.
- **[1:40 to 3:00] The list.** **Death of a patient. Separation or divorce. Successful completion of treatment. A decision to discontinue. Failure to pay storage fees.** Each gets a choice: continue storage, donate to a person or couple, donate to research, or thaw and discard.
- **[3:00 to 4:30] The clause nobody reads.** [B-ROLL: an unopened envelope, a card-expiry date.] **Most forms have no line for "this clinic ceases to operate."** **The non-payment clause does that work instead**, and published forms commonly provide that where a patient fails to preserve their choice through non-payment, **the clinic is authorized to discard and destroy.**
  **"Read that as a mechanism rather than a threat. The clinic is holding material it cannot move, cannot destroy and cannot fund. The contract is the only thing that breaks the deadlock."**
- **[4:30 to 6:00] Abandoned.** The literature's own word for embryos whose owners cannot be reached. **Clinics are left choosing between discarding without clear authorization and storing indefinitely**, with the outcome depending on the consents, state law and the clinic's risk tolerance. **Label on screen: peer-reviewed and institutional literature, read via search index.**
- **[6:00 to 7:30] The three live options.** Transfer to another clinic. Ask for discard, in writing, and keep the confirmation. Move to a dedicated long-term storage company. **"The thing that is not on the list is doing nothing."**
- **[7:30 to 8:30] The honest boundary.** [B-ROLL: hands sealing a plain mailer.] **"We are not a fertility service. We do not store embryos, eggs or sperm and we offer no reproductive service. If you have embryos in storage, this video is the useful part and our product is not relevant to it."**
- **[8:30 to 9:15] The close, without overpromising.** **"The pattern repeats across every category of stored biological material: the contract decides, non-payment is a decision, and being unreachable is the real risk. That is true of an embryo, a tissue sample and a genetic record, and almost nobody reads the clause until an email arrives that they were not expecting."**

**Peer-review labeling, binding:** the abandonment material is **peer-reviewed and institutional**, the disposition lists are **published clinic consent forms**, and **none of it was opened at its own domain.** Say so on screen a single time.
