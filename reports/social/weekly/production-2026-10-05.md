## 1. PODCAST EPISODE PACK (18-22 minutes)

### Title options
1. **"Nine Million Samples"**
2. **"The Heel Prick and the Twenty-Three Years"**

### COLD OPEN (word for word, ~80 seconds)

> Almost every baby born in this country gets a heel prick in the first days of life. A few drops of blood onto a card. That card gets screened for a list of serious conditions that are treatable if you catch them early and devastating if you don't.
>
> It is one of the great public-health programs of the last hundred years. I am not going to spend this episode undermining it and you should not let anybody use this episode to do that.
>
> Here is the part almost nobody is told.
>
> **The card does not get thrown away.**
>
> In New Jersey it was kept for **twenty-three years**. Parents found out from a lawsuit.
>
> In Michigan, officials agreed to destroy **more than three million** of them. Texas had previously destroyed **over five million**. Minnesota, **one point one million**.
>
> That is more than nine million biological samples destroyed, not because anybody proved they were misused, but because of **how people were asked.**
>
> And there is a sentence in a federal ruling on this that I have not been able to stop thinking about. I will read it to you in segment three, and I think it is going to change what you assume about who controls a sample once it exists.
>
> Here is the distinction this whole episode turns on: **screening is a test. Retention is storage. They were sold to you as one decision, and they are two.**

### SEGMENT 1 (~5 min): What actually happens to the card

**Talking points with receipts.**
- The screen itself runs in days. **The retention runs in decades.** The gap between those two numbers is the entire story.
- **New Jersey's retention period was 23 years** *(as reported in state and legal coverage)*.
- **The Institute for Justice, a public-interest law firm, sued New Jersey in November 2023** on behalf of parents who said they had not been told *(litigation record)*.
- **In June 2024, New Jersey Attorney General Matt Platkin issued a directive** shortening retention, restricting permitted uses and requiring clearer communication with parents *(reported; the directive is the primary document)*.
- **STANCE:** the directive is a real improvement and should be said so. **A state that fixes something after being sued has still fixed it.**

**CLIP MOMENT:** *"The screen takes days. The storage takes decades. Nobody told you those were two different decisions, because nobody had to."*

### SEGMENT 2 (~5 min): Nine million samples, and what the settlements were actually about

**Talking points with receipts.**
- **Michigan: more than 3 million spots destroyed** as a partial settlement, in litigation brought by parents arguing they had not been given enough information to consent to **research use** *(reported; the month was given in coverage without a year we could confirm, so no year is stated on air)*. The case is **Kanuszewski et al v Michigan Department of Health and Human Services**.
- **Texas: over 5 million. Minnesota: 1.1 million.** Both following similar challenges *(reported; years not independently confirmed)*.
- **THE PART TO LAND:** none of these settlements turned on somebody proving harm. **They turned on consent.** The samples were destroyed because of how the question was asked, or not asked.
- **STANCE:** that is the correct remedy and it is also a tragedy, because **destroyed samples are destroyed research and destroyed future answers.** Both things are true. Do not pick one.

**CLIP MOMENT:** *"Nine million samples were destroyed and not one of them because somebody proved harm. They were destroyed over a consent form."*

### SEGMENT 3 (~5 min): The sentence from the ruling

**Talking points with receipts.**
- **A federal judge partially dismissed the New Jersey case.** The holding, as reported: **the Fourteenth Amendment protects a parent's right to direct the care of their child, and that right does not extend to controlling the state's retention of residual dried blood spots** *(court ruling, as reported; read on air as reported rather than quoted verbatim, because we are working from coverage and not the opinion)*.
- **In October 2025 the parents filed an amended complaint** *(Institute for Justice, 6 October 2025)*.
- **STANCE, and it is the episode's thesis:** if the constitutional route is narrow, **the answer is legislative, state by state, and slow.** That is unglamorous and it is where this is actually decided.
- **EPISTEMIC LABEL SAID ON AIR:** "We are reading this from coverage of the ruling, not from the opinion itself. If you want the exact language, go to the docket."

**CLIP MOMENT:** *"A court has now said your rights as a parent cover the care of your child and not the custody of what came out of them. Sit with that for a second."*

### SEGMENT 4 (~4 min): Our own stake, declared

**Talking points.**
- **Declare it plainly: we sell DNA storage. We are the last people who should be sanctimonious about somebody else keeping a sample.**
- **So judge us by the same test we just applied to a state government.** Four questions, and they are the ones to put to anybody holding anything biological of yours: **Who holds it? For how long? What are they allowed to do with it? And who is told when any of that changes?**
- **The honest admission: our own published answers to the last two are incomplete.** We say it on air because a company that runs this episode and does not say it has made an advertisement.
- **$99 a year, billed annually, first 1,000 members lock the founding rate. GenVault, New Jersey, independent CAP-accredited, ISO 9001 and 20387, FDA-registered, ambient temperature. Storage only: we never screen, rank, select or promise anything about a descendant.**

### OUTRO CTA
> If you take one thing: **screening is a test and retention is storage, and nobody is obliged to tell you which one you agreed to.** Go find out what happened to your own card. Depending on where you were born, it may still exist.
