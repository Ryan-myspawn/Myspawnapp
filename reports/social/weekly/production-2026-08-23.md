### Segment 1 — The 2016 trial, told straight
- **Facts (verbatim-grade):** JCEM, published online Oct 27, 2016; 200mg norethisterone enanthate + 1,000mg testosterone undecanoate; 320 men aged 18-45; ~96% spermatogenesis suppression; 4 pregnancies; enrollment stopped in 2011 by a safety review over adverse events (mood disorders, acne, injection-site pain) ([Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/male-contraceptive-injection-effective-at-preventing-pregnancy), [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/10/161027143337.htm)).
- **Stance:** correct the record without scoring points: the "men are wimps" framing was debunked by fact-checkers ([Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/male-birth-control-study/), [PopSci](https://www.popsci.com/did-that-male-contraception-study-prove-men-are-wimps-not-exactly/)); the committee stopped it, and 75%+ of participants wanted the product.
- **CLIP MOMENT:** "Ninety-six percent effective. Seventy-five percent of the men wanted it. Cancelled anyway. Sit with that."

### Segment 2 — Why fifty years late (the institutional story)
- **Stance:** the honest structural answer, no villains: the risk math is asymmetric (a contraceptive for healthy men is judged against zero medical benefit to the user's own body, unlike the pill's pregnancy-risk offset); liability fears; thin commercial incentive against a female-contraception market that already exists. Result: the male side of the field ran on academic grants for two generations.
- **Talking points:** every "male pill soon" headline of the last 20 years vs zero approved products; what changed recently: nonhormonal targets (like meiosis-stage compounds) sidestep the 2016 trial's hormonal side-effect profile.
- **CLIP MOMENT:** "It's not that science couldn't. It's that nobody was paid to."

### Segment 3 — The switch (JQ1, labeled)
- **Facts:** Cornell, PNAS, April 7, 2026: JQ1 blocks prophase 1 of meiosis; sperm production off for the treatment window, full recovery ~6 weeks, healthy fertile pups. **Labels on air: peer-reviewed, mice, six-year study, the compound itself is not a viable drug (known side effects); it's proof the switch exists** ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/04/breakthrough-takes-big-step-toward-safe-reversible-male-contraception)).
- **Stance:** why this is different in kind: nonhormonal, targeted at the one biological step, reversible by design. The same step, meiosis, is where the lab-made-eggs field is stuck, which tells you where reproductive biology's real frontier sits.
- **CLIP MOMENT:** "For fifty years the answer was 'the biology is too hard.' The biology just got a light switch. In mice. But a switch."

What exists today: testing (know your numbers; clinics and at-home options exist), preservation decisions if fertility specifically matters to you (clinical, talk to a doctor), and the layer we sell, said plainly: the record itself.
