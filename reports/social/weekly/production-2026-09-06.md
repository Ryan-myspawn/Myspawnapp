### Script

**[COLD OPEN — B-roll: freezer door opening, frost; cut to a plain archive drawer]**
When people hear their DNA sample is stored at room temperature, some of them flinch. Isn't cold better? Freezers feel like technology; drawers feel like neglect. This video is the honest engineering answer, including the parts that cut against intuition, and since my company stores DNA at ambient temperature, you should audit every claim I make. Sources below.

**[SECTION 1: WHAT ACTUALLY DESTROYS DNA — B-roll: the three enemies as icons]**
From our earlier deep-dive on DNA longevity: the killers are water, enzymes, microbes, heat swings, and UV. Notice what's NOT on the list: room temperature itself. A dry, dark, chemically stable environment removes almost every attack vector. Cold is one way to slow chemistry. Removing the water is another.

**[SECTION 2: THE FIFTY-MILLION-CARD EXPERIMENT — B-roll: newborn screening card imagery]**
Here's the receipt most people have never heard of: nearly every baby born in America since the 1960s-80s had blood spotted onto a paper card, the Guthrie card, for newborn screening. Many states archived them, at ambient temperature, for decades; collections run into the millions. And modern labs keep going back to them successfully: peer-reviewed studies have pulled genome-wide SNP genotypes from decades-old spots without amplification [PLOS One], and DNA methylation profiles from 10-year-old cards that correlate excellently with fresh samples [BMC Biotechnology / PMC]. Room-temperature paper archives, holding readable genomes across decades: not a startup claim, a public-health fact.

**[SECTION 3: THE HEAD-TO-HEAD — B-roll: the NAR study on screen]**
It gets stronger. An October 2025 study in NAR Molecular Medicine tested ensilication, sealing DNA in a glass-like silica shell, against cryostorage [peer-reviewed]. Ambient matched frozen for preservation, and here's the counterintuitive part: the FROZEN samples showed more artifactual mutations, the damage freeze-thaw cycles quietly introduce. Every power outage, every door opening, every transfer is a thermal event. A freezer is a machine that must never fail for decades. A properly dried, protected sample in a stable vault has no compressor to die.

**[SECTION 4: THE HONEST LIMITS — checklist on screen]**
Now the boundaries, because they matter. One: this is true for DNA AS A RECORD. Living cells, eggs, sperm, embryos, absolutely require cryostorage; nobody should store viable tissue in a drawer, and companies that freeze gametes are doing it right. Two: ambient only works with preparation and environment control: dried, protected, monitored: the drawer is engineered, not neglected. Three: no storage method excuses bad custody: the best-preserved sample in a company that vanishes is still lost, which is why the who-holds-it questions matter more than the thermometer.

**[CLOSE — direct to camera]**
So that's why our vault, GenVault's accredited, FDA-registered facility, runs ambient: it's the method the dried-blood-spot decades and the newest head-to-head data support for DNA records, with fewer single points of failure. $99 a year, storage only, and the sources are in the description for your own audit. Cold isn't wrong. For a record, dry and stable is simply the better engineering. myspawn.me.
