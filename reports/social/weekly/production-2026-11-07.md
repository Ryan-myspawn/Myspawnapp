### COLD OPEN, word for word, 60 to 90 seconds

"We keep a file on our competitors. Every day, a routine sweeps a list of company names and writes down what changed. On a quiet day it writes one line: no dated item for any of these, and then it lists them.

**Thirty-nine mentions.**

Every single one of them inside a list. Not one price. Not one dated item. Not one finding. **Thirty-nine appearances in our own records and we have never once actually looked that company up.**

**A roll call manufactures the appearance of coverage.** If you read our file you would say that name was one of the most consistently monitored in the landscape. ### SEGMENT 3: How to tell whether anybody has actually looked

**STANCE:** turn the whole thing into something the listener can use on any custodian, including us.

**TALKING POINTS, and this is the transferable tool:**
- **Ask for a finding, not a status.** "Is everything fine" has one answer. **"What is the most recent specific thing you found, and when" has an answer that cannot be faked by a routine.**
- **Ask what the last change was.** A monitoring process that has never reported a change has either monitored a perfectly static thing or has not monitored.
- **Ask what would have to happen for you to hear from them.** **If nobody can name the trigger, there is no trigger.**
- **And the uncomfortable one, which applies to us: ask when they last checked that the thing they are watching still exists.**

**COUNTERWEIGHT, because this is otherwise too pleased with itself:** most quiet reports are genuinely quiet. **The failure mode is not that nothing happened. It is that the record looks identical whether or not anybody looked, and that identical appearance is the problem.**
