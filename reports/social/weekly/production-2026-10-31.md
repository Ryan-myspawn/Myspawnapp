**SEGMENT 1: What was actually built (about 5 min)**

- **Human embryos AND blastoids implanted and reached post-implantation milestones. Single-cell RNA sequencing at day 14 mapped the molecular conversation between embryo and lining.**

**SEGMENT 2: What it is not (about 5 min)**
- **Stance: this is not an artificial womb and it must never be filed as one.** It is a model of one stage, running for a bounded number of days, in a dish.
- **Every experiment stopped before 14 days.** Not because the model failed. Because of a rule.
- **The distinction that matters for anyone reading this field: a model that lets you OBSERVE a process is not a technology that lets you COMPLETE it.** Observation and capability get collapsed in headlines constantly and the collapse is almost always in the same direction.
- **REFUSAL PERFORMED ON AIR:** "I am not going to tell you this brings artificial gestation closer, because I do not know that, and neither does anyone who told you it this week."
- **CLIP MOMENT:** "They did not run out of science at fourteen days. They ran out of permission."

**SEGMENT 3: The number that is stranger than the biology (about 6 min)**
- **The 14-day limit comes from the UK's Warnock Report, 1984, one of 64 recommendations from a committee chaired by Mary Warnock.**
- **And the report itself said the limit rested on consensus rather than scientific fact.** The number was chosen as a defensible line, not discovered.
- **The 2016 ISSCR guidelines prohibited culture beyond 14 days or formation of the primitive streak, whichever came first.**
- **Then on 26 May 2021 the ISSCR relaxed it.** Research beyond 14 days moved out of the prohibited category entirely, to be assessed case by case with multiple phases of review, with national academies, funders and regulators urged to lead public discussion.

**[0:00-0:40] B-roll: a calendar page, then a hand drawing a single vertical line through it.**
"Almost every story about human embryo research contains the same number. Fourteen days. It is quoted like a physical constant, the way you would quote the boiling point of water.

It is not one. It is a line somebody drew, in a specific country, in a specific year, and the people who drew it said at the time that it was a line rather than a discovery."

**[0:40-2:30] B-roll: a bound government report on a desk; pages turning; hold on a table of contents.**
"The number comes from the United Kingdom. **In 1984 a committee chaired by the philosopher Mary Warnock published a report on human fertilisation and embryology, and among its sixty-four recommendations was a fourteen-day limit on keeping a human embryo in culture.**

**The report itself acknowledged that the limit rested on consensus rather than scientific fact.** That is the part that almost never travels with the number.

The biological hook it hangs on is the primitive streak, the first structure that marks out the body's axis and the point after which an embryo can no longer split into twins. **The 2016 guidelines of the International Society for Stem Cell Research prohibited culture beyond 14 days OR the formation of the primitive streak, whichever came first.**"

**[2:30-4:30] B-roll: a document with a paragraph crossed out and rewritten in the margin.**
"**Then, on the twenty-sixth of May 2021, the ISSCR relaxed it.**

Research beyond 14 days stopped being a prohibited category. **It moved to case-by-case assessment, with several phases of review, and the society urged national academies, funders and regulators to lead public discussion.**

**LABEL, and it carries the whole video: ISSCR guidelines are NOT law.** They are a professional body's recommendations. **The sources I could reach describe the rule's status inconsistently, some as a strict legal restriction and some as an official guideline, and legal status genuinely varies by country.** **I could not establish which jurisdictions have changed their own law since 2021, and I am not going to tell you that they have.**

**The criticism is part of the record too: bioethicists objected that a change made in the name of public engagement was itself made without public input.**"

**[4:30-6:00] B-roll: a laboratory dish under even light; a clock face deliberately absent from frame.**
"Which brings us to why this matters right now.

**A paper in *Cell*, published 23 December 2025, built an engineered endometrium and watched human embryos implant into it, with single-cell sequencing at day 14.** Every experiment stopped before fourteen days.

**So a guideline relaxed in 2021 and experiments still stopping at the old number in 2025.** That is not a contradiction. It is what happens when a professional guideline, national law and institutional ethics review are three different things, and the strictest one governs.

**The useful habit: when you read that research is 'allowed' or 'banned', ask by whom.** A society's guideline, a funder's condition, a national statute and a university's review board are four different authorities, and only one of them can send anyone to court."

**[6:00-7:30] B-roll: plain exterior of an industrial building; a sealed container on a shelf.**

**The reason this video exists is that it is the same literacy problem in both directions.** The number 14 gets quoted as physics. Accreditation logos get quoted as proof. **In both cases the fix is the same question: who set this, and what does it actually bind?**"

**[7:30-8:00] Close: the report on the desk, static.**
