# 1. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER (7 to 9 minutes)

## "It Worked In Mice. Here Is The Denominator."

**THE RE-SOURCING YESTERDAY DEMANDED WAS DONE, AND IT CHANGED THE ANSWER. THIS IS THE WHOLE REASON THE FLAG EXISTED.**

Yesterday's watchlist banked a figure from a **2024 review: "only approximately 4 percent of transferred embryos derived from iPSC gamete procedures result in a live birth."** It carried a written instruction: **re-source against the primary literature before any on-screen use.**

**Re-sourced today. The 4 percent is a FIELD-WIDE ESTIMATE in a review, not a result.** The primary figure is better, older and far more useful.

### The number this script is built on

**Hikabe O, Hamazaki N, Nagamatsu G, et al., *Nature* 539, 299-303 (2016), DOI 10.1038/nature20104.** As cited in a **2024 *EMBO Journal*** paper: **full-term development from two-cell embryos made from in vitro-derived MII oocytes was 0.9 percent, which is 26 out of 2,753.** **The control in the same comparison, embryos from superovulated mice, was 61.7 percent, 37 out of 60.**

**FOUR LABELS, ALL SPOKEN ON AIR AND NOT ONLY WRITTEN HERE.**
1. **This is MICE. 2016. Ten years old.**
2. **The 0.9 percent figure reaches us through the EMBO paper CITING Hikabe. We did not open the Nature paper. Say so.**
3. **Hikabe used both embryonic stem cell and iPSC lines, so 0.9 percent covers pluripotent-stem-cell-derived oocytes generally rather than iPSC alone.**
4. **A Nature commentary notes the protocol appears much less efficient again when applied to primordial germ cell-like cells as in Morohaku and colleagues.** So 0.9 percent is not a floor.
