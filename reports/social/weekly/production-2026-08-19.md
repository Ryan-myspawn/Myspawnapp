- **Honest asterisk, said out loud:** an embryo is living reproductive tissue; a DNA sample is a record. One can become a person today; the other cannot, with any technology that exists. We store the second kind. Anyone who blurs those is selling you something.

## 2) YOUTUBE LONG-FORM SCRIPT (6-10 min) — queued explainer, now produced

### "When Legacy Companies Die: 5 Questions That Decide If Your Stuff Survives"

**[SHOT: talking head, desk. Lower third: "Everything here is sourced — links in description."]**

Two companies promised to preserve pieces of people forever. This summer, one is quietly dying, and the other already went through a bankruptcy auction. Nobody's memories or genomes had to be involved, and yet 15 million people's were. Here are the five questions that would have told you in advance, and will tell you now, whether any preservation service, ours included, deserves your family's history.

**[B-ROLL: HereAfter AI homepage shutdown notice, slow zoom]**

Case one. HereAfter AI sold "interactive memory" apps: record your parents' stories, talk to them later. As of July 2026 its homepage is a shutdown notice. No deadline published, no refund policy, users emailing support to rescue recordings of people who have since died. Source: the shutdown notice itself and industry coverage; link below.

**[B-ROLL: 23andMe headlines montage: March 2025 filing → auction → sale]**

Case two, the big one. 23andMe filed Chapter 11 on March 23, 2025. The genetic data of roughly 15 million customers became an asset. Regeneron bid 256 million dollars; the auction reopened; founder Anne Wojcicki's nonprofit TTAM won at 305 million; the sale closed July 14, 2025. More than two dozen state attorneys general sued over genetic data changing hands in bankruptcy. Before all that, an October 2023 credential-stuffing breach touched 6.9 million profiles and ran five months before detection; settlements have reached 46.75 million dollars in the bankruptcy plus an 18 million dollar 42-state deal. Every number is in the description.

**[SHOT: whiteboard. Title: THE 5 QUESTIONS]**

Question one: **where does the actual thing live?** Not the app, the thing: the sample, the recordings, the data. If the answer is "on our servers" full stop, the thing dies with the company. What you want: independent, accredited custody. For biological samples that means an accredited biorepository: ISO 9001, ISO 20387, CAP, FDA registration. Accreditations belong to the facility; check the facility, not the brand.

**[B-ROLL: close-up of accreditation certificates, generic lab]**

Question two: **can you take it out?** Download, transfer, destruction on demand, in writing, with a timeline. 23andMe customers could delete, and regulators had to remind them to. Do it before the bankruptcy, not during.

Question three: **what binds the successor?** Companies get sold. The question is whether the buyer inherits obligations or just assets. Ask for the actual policy language about acquisition and liquidation. If it does not exist, that is your answer.

Question four: **is your data a product line?** Research sharing should be opt-in, not a default buried on page nine. The 23andMe fight was ugly precisely because consent given in 2015 met a bankruptcy court in 2025.

Question five: **does the law have your back?** Partially. GINA, 2008, stops health insurers and employers from using genetic data against you. It does not cover life, disability, or long-term care insurance. Know the gap before you spit in anything.

**[SHOT: talking head, slower]**

Honest close. We sell DNA preservation, so grade us with the same five questions: our samples sit with GenVault, an independent accredited biorepository in New Jersey, not on our premises; destruction on request; custody documented. That is not a boast, it is table stakes, and if we ever fail the checklist you should leave. The preservation industry does not have a technology problem. Freezers work. Silica works. It has a custody problem, and custody is paperwork, and paperwork is where families lose things.

**[END CARD: the 5 questions as a list + "Full sources in description" + myspawn.me]**

Preserve-now thesis, no overpromise: your genome and your stories are the two archives your descendants cannot reconstruct later. Store them somewhere built to outlive the brand that sold them to you.
