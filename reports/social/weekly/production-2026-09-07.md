**SEGMENT 1 — The seeds (the existence proofs).**
- Receipts: Methuselah (radiocarbon 155 BC–64 CE, planted 2005, Arava Institute; genetic analysis of germinated ancient seeds published in *Science Advances*, 2020 — [science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aax0384]). Lotus: ~1,300-yr fruit, Xipaozi, China; ~80% germination across 10 dated fruits (*American Journal of Botany*, Shen-Miller et al. — [bsapubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.3732/ajb.89.2.236]). All peer-reviewed.
- Stance: awe with an engineer's eye — dryness + low metabolism + stable chemistry, not magic.
- Talking points: what a seed actually is (a paused organism with its own packed lunch); why desiccation is the trick (water is the solvent of decay); the germination rate as the stat that matters (80% is a PRODUCTION line, not a miracle).
- **CLIP MOMENT:** "A two-thousand-year outage, and the system booted."

**SEGMENT 2 — The egg (the pattern, measured in humans).**
- Receipt: *The EMBO Journal*, July 16, 2025 (CRG Barcelona + Dexeus Mujer; 100+ freshly donated human eggs, 21 donors aged 19-34; peer-reviewed). Eggs throttle lysosome/proteasome activity as they mature — housekeeping itself burns energy and emits reactive oxygen species — and live imaging caught eggs jettisoning lysosomes entirely in the final hours before ovulation.
- Stance: the same design pattern as the seeds, running warm: *minimize activity, minimize damage*. A 40-50 year hold at body temperature, achieved by doing less on purpose.
- Talking points: dormancy ≠ freezing — nature's longest-tested stasis protocols run at ambient and 37°C; the maintenance-is-a-hazard inversion (every cleanup cycle costs energy and throws off damaging molecules — the deepest anti-intuition in the whole story).
- RAIL (hard): this segment is pure biology — no fertility advice, no product tie. We do not store gametes and nothing here implies reproductive risk management.
- **CLIP MOMENT:** "The best-preserved cell in your body survives by firing its own cleaning crew."

**SEGMENT 3 — The limits (where the pattern stops).**
- Receipts: DNA's measured half-life in bone ~521 years (*Proceedings of the Royal Society B*, 2012, moa-bone study — verify exact citation before recording); oldest sequenced environmental DNA ~2 million years, Greenland (*Nature*, 2022) — but that's fragments read by force, not anything alive.
- Stance: the honest line every hype merchant skips — dormancy preserves VIABILITY for centuries at best; INFORMATION survives longer but only as fragments needing reconstruction. Different physics, different promises.
- Talking points: why the lotus works at 1,300 years but nothing germinates at 10,000; what "readable" means vs "alive"; where cryo genuinely owns the viability problem (embryos — the ~30-year record baby, born July 2025) and where dry/ambient owns the information problem.
- **CLIP MOMENT:** "Nature preserves life for centuries and information for millennia. Pick which one you're solving for, and say it out loud."

**SEGMENT 4 — The vault (what humans add: custody).**
- Receipt: Svalbard Global Seed Vault — opened 2008; 70th deposit brought it past 1,401,285 samples (Feb 2026; FAO/Crop Trust — [croptrust.org milestone release]). New depositor genebanks from Africa/Asia/Europe this year.

[OPEN — desk, the actual acronyms written on cards: ISO 9001, ISO 20387, CAP, "FDA-registered."]
"Every DNA storage company, including mine, waves acronyms at you. Today I'm going to tell you what each one actually certifies, what it doesn't, and the four questions that matter more than any of them. By the end you'll be able to audit anyone selling biological storage. Including us. ESPECIALLY us."

[BLOCK 1 — ISO 9001. B-roll: generic factory-floor audit footage.]
"ISO 9001 is a quality MANAGEMENT standard. It certifies that an organization documents its processes and follows them. It is real, it is audited, and it is generic — a paperclip factory can hold ISO 9001. In a biobank it means the place runs on procedures, not vibes. Necessary. Not sufficient."

[BLOCK 2 — ISO 20387. B-roll: lab sample racks, chain-of-custody paperwork.]
"ISO 20387 is the one written FOR biobanks — published in 2018, it covers competence, impartiality, and consistent operation of biobanking specifically: sample integrity, traceability, storage conditions, chain of custody. (Standard text: iso.org — verify the scope line on screen before recording.) If a storage vendor holds 20387, someone audited their actual biobanking. This is the acronym to look for first."

[BLOCK 3 — CAP. B-roll: inspection checklist pages.]
"CAP is the College of American Pathologists — their accreditation program inspects laboratories against checklist requirements, with peer inspectors, on a cycle. (cap.org — verify current biorepository checklist name before recording.) It's the American clinical-lab gold standard applied to the facility."

[BLOCK 4 — 'FDA-registered.' B-roll: the phrase on a marketing page, circled.]
"Now the honest one. 'FDA-registered' means the facility is REGISTERED with the FDA — it's on a list, subject to inspection. It does NOT mean the FDA approved, endorsed, or evaluated the service. Any company using 'FDA-registered' as if it were 'FDA-approved' is hoping you don't know the difference. When we say it about our vault's biorepository, this narrow meaning is the claim. Registration, not endorsement."

[BLOCK 5 — the four questions that beat every acronym. B-roll: typing an email.]
"One: WHO holds my sample — the brand I bought from, or a named, independently accredited facility? Get the facility's name. Two: what happens to my sample if the brand goes bankrupt — is there a contractual continuity plan, in writing? Three: can I get my sample BACK, and what does retrieval cost? Four: who audits the audit — which accreditations, verifiable on the accreditor's own site? Ask all four in one email. A serious vendor answers in a week. Evasion IS the answer."

[CLOSE — thesis, no overpromise.]
"Our answers, on the record: our members' samples sit with GenVault, an independent biorepository in New Jersey — ISO 9001, ISO 20387, CAP-accredited, FDA-registered in exactly the narrow sense I just described. Ninety-nine dollars a year, storage only; we promise custody, not miracles. Audit us with the four questions — that's what they're for."
