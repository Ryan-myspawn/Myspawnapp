## 1. PODCAST EPISODE PACK: "The Receipt Check", pilot

**Freshness check:** The Receipt Check was **proposed 12 September and has never been produced**; the backlog entry is still open. No episode in the log is a recurring-segment pilot. **The worked example completed today** (Competitor Watch dive #27) and is four hours old. **Deliberately distinct from two adjacent episodes:** 14 September used our own error to talk about **rhetoric versus fact-checking**, a communications subject; 19 September was **killed** precisely because it ran an unchecked aggregate. **This episode is about the checking procedure itself and the number it produced.**
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55.
**Trend / hook used:** Our own audit, completed today, of a category-failure claim we were one asset away from publishing.

### Title options
1. **"One Out of Four"**
2. **"We Checked the Claim We Were About to Publish"**
3. **"The Receipt Check: Episode One"**

*(Recommend 1. It states the finding, it is the whole story, and it does not open with "we".)*

### Cold open, word for word, 75 seconds

> Three days ago we killed an episode. It was recorded-ready. The argument in it was the best argument we had made all month, and it was wrong.
>
> Here is the claim. A grief-technology company published a summary of its own market saying that in roughly two years, most of the category's best-known names had shut down, pivoted or paused. It named four companies. We read it, we believed it, and we built a base-rate argument on top of it: this category fails, so ask who is holding your things.
>
> Then somebody asked the obvious question. Who wrote that summary?
>
> A competitor. Not a journalist. Not a regulator. A company selling in the same category, describing the fate of its rivals.
>
> So we checked all four. One at a time. It took four sittings across three weeks and it finished this morning.
>
> One of the four claims holds. Three do not. And every one of the three is wrong in the same direction: the direction that makes the company who wrote it look better.
>
> That is not bias. Bias is a tilt. This is a measurement. **Twenty-five percent.**
>
> This is the Receipt Check. The idea is simple and we are going to do it on a schedule: before a claim goes out, somebody asks who benefits from it being true, and then checks it anyway.

### Segment 1: the claim, and who wrote it (4 to 5 min)

**Stance:** name the mechanism, not the company. We are not here to dunk.

- The source is **afterlife.ai**, a company in the same category. **We do not name it on camera.** The point is structural and naming it makes this a fight instead of a lesson.
- **Talking point:** a competitor's market summary is not a neutral document and is not dishonest either. It is **marketing**, and marketing is allowed to select. The error was ours: **we treated a selected account as a survey.**
- **The tell we missed:** the claim had a shape that was *useful to us.* "The category fails, so custody matters" was the argument we already wanted to make. **A claim that arrives pre-fitted to your thesis deserves more checking than one that contradicts it, and we gave it less.**
- **CLIP MOMENT:** *"A claim that arrives pre-fitted to your thesis deserves more checking than one that contradicts it. We gave it less."*

### Segment 2: the four, one at a time (6 to 8 min)

**Read them as a scoreboard, in the order they were checked. Do not editorialize between them; the pattern does the work.**

| Company | The account | What the check found |
|---|---|---|
| **HereAfter AI** | shut down | **Holds.** Shutdown notice, homepage not loading as of August 2026, iOS app not updated since September 2023. |
| **StoryFile** | shut down | **False as framed.** Chapter 11 filed **5 May 2024**, White Plains NY, case 7:2024bk22398, **$1.5M assets against $10.5M liabilities**. It **emerged**. Assets acquired by **Key 7 Investment Company**, reported in trade coverage **3 March 2025**. **Operating and expanding in 2026.** |
| **Eternos** | category exit | **False as framed.** **Raised $10.3M** from Mayfield and Boldstart, **November 2025**, and **rebranded to Uare.ai**. It left because its founder found **most users were not preparing for death; they wanted to use it while alive.** That is a company reading its customers correctly. |
| **You, Only Virtual** | waitlist, no public pricing | **Unsupported.** The "behind a waitlist, pre-revenue" line traces to a **StartEngine offering document for a 2022 round**: company-authored investor material, four years old, presented as a July 2026 status. A **December 2024** source reports **$10 for a constrained chat**. **No evidence of shutdown.** |

**Epistemic labels, stated on screen:** the StoryFile bankruptcy details are **court record**. The Eternos raise is **reported funding news**. The YOV waitlist line is **a company's own investor document**. **Three different evidence grades inside one four-item claim, and the original summary presented them as one fact.**

- **CLIP MOMENT:** *"Three of four were wrong. All three were wrong in the same direction."*

### Segment 3: what we were about to publish (4 to 5 min)

**Stance:** own it specifically, not performatively.

- The killed **19 September** episode. The two short-form drafts that used the line. **Longpost #84**, whose second paragraph still carries it and is still on hold today.
- **The honest replacement, which is a better argument:** *one failed, one reorganized, one raised money and changed markets, one is still going. The category is volatile, not doomed. And volatility is still a custody question, because a company that reorganizes, gets bought or changes markets is a company whose terms can change while your things are inside it.*
- **Say the uncomfortable half out loud:** the corrected version is **less alarming** and therefore **less useful to us commercially.** Publish it anyway. **That is the entire reason anyone should believe the next thing we say.**
- **CLIP MOMENT:** *"The corrected version is worse marketing. That is how you know we mean it."*

### Segment 4: the standing rule this creates (3 to 4 min)

- **New hard gate, in force from today:** **no aggregate claim about competitor outcomes ships until every company in it has been checked individually.** Not the source. Each company.
- **Generalize it for the listener, because this is their takeaway not ours:** when you are shown a pattern rather than a fact, ask **how many cases it rests on** and **who counted them.** Four is a small number and it was still enough to be wrong three ways.
- **Turn it on us, and mean it:** *we publish a base rate about this category. Ask us the same question. Ask which companies, ask who checked, ask when.*

### Outro CTA

> MySpawn stores a DNA record for ninety-nine dollars a year. It is storage. We are not going to tell you the industry is collapsing, because we checked and it is not. What we will tell you is that companies change hands, and that the question worth asking any of us is what happens to your things when they do.

### Three Shorts cut points
1. Cold open, from *"Here is the claim"* to *"Twenty-five percent."*
2. Segment 2 scoreboard, read straight, ending on *"All three were wrong in the same direction."*
3. Segment 3, *"The corrected version is worse marketing. That is how you know we mean it."*

## 4. THE RECOVERED SLOT: SAMPLE-CONTINUITY PAGE, FIRST DRAFT

**Why this is here.** Recommended independently by the competitor dives, the ad lane, the Reddit playbook, the 20 September script package, last night's export research and today's episode. **Six routes.** It blocks **Ad_WhoIsAllowedToBuyThem_145** from running, it was forward-committed to on camera in the 15 September episode, and it has sat undone since 18 September.

**WHAT THIS DRAFT IS AND IS NOT.** It is structure and language. **Every place where a real commitment is required is a marked blank, because those are the founder's to make and I will not invent them.** Inventing a chain-of-custody claim would be the single worst thing this operation could do, and it is explicitly against our rails.

> ### What happens to your sample if MySpawn stops operating
>
> You are paying us to keep something for a long time. You are entitled to know what happens if we do not last that long. This page is the answer, and we will keep it current.
>
> **Where your sample actually is.** Your sample is not held by MySpawn. It is held by **GenVault LLC**, an independent biorepository in New Jersey. GenVault holds **ISO 9001 and ISO 20387 and CAP accreditation, and is FDA-registered.** To be precise, because you should hold us to precision: **those are GenVault's credentials, not ours, and FDA registration means the facility is registered with the agency, not that the FDA has approved or endorsed anything.**
>
> **Why that separation matters.** It means the company you buy from and the facility that holds your sample are two different organizations. If one has a bad year, the other is still a building with your sample in it.
>
> **[BLANK 1: THE ARRANGEMENT.]** *State what contractual arrangement exists between MySpawn and GenVault covering cessation of MySpawn's operations. If there is a written continuity or escrow provision, say what it provides and for how long. **If there is not one, say that.** A page that admits a gap is worth more than a page that implies a protection we do not have, and a reader who later discovers the difference is gone forever.*
>
> **[BLANK 2: WHAT YOU WOULD BE TOLD, AND WHEN.]** *State the notice commitment. Recommended default: **written notice to the email on file at least 90 days before any interruption**, with the specific options available to you set out in that notice.*
>
> **[BLANK 3: YOUR OPTIONS.]** *State what a member could actually do. The realistic set is: continue storage by paying the facility directly if the facility will accept it; have the sample transferred to another facility you nominate; or have it destroyed on request. **Only list the ones that are actually available. Confirm each with GenVault before publishing.***
>
> **[BLANK 4: ACQUISITION, WHICH IS THE MORE LIKELY EVENT.]** *Companies are bought far more often than they close. State what a buyer would and would not be able to change. If our terms currently permit assignment of the agreement on a change of control without member consent, **say so plainly**, because a reader can find that clause themselves in about ninety seconds and finding it after reading this page would be fatal.*
>
> **[BLANK 5: WHAT WE WILL NOT DO.]** *Recommended and, unlike the others, this one we can already commit to: we do not sell or license member samples or data to third parties, and we will not begin. Confirm against the current privacy policy before publishing.*
>
> **How to check this page is still true.** It carries a last-reviewed date. If it has not been reviewed in twelve months, email us and ask why.
>
> *Last reviewed: **[DATE]***

**WHAT THE FOUNDER HAS TO DO:** answer five blanks, four of which require a conversation with GenVault and one with whoever wrote the terms. **Nothing else about this page needs writing.**
