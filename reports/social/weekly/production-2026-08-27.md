## 2. YOUTUBE LONG-FORM SCRIPT (6-10 min) — "Why Bats Barely Get Cancer (And What It Says About DNA)"

**Freshness check:** This topic has not been used in the last 7 days as an explainer (this week's Nature Myotis paper appeared as a 60-second PM script and an X longpost; production-log lists no bat/DNA-repair explainer; format and depth are new).
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55.
**Trend-hook used:** Nature (peer-reviewed, this week): near-complete genomes for eight Myotis bat species, UVM/Penn State-led.

**[0:00-0:45] Cold open.** [B-ROLL: night sky, bat silhouettes] "In 1964, a Siberian field biologist banded a bat that weighed less than a AA battery. Forty-one years later, researchers found it alive. Same cave. Same band. To put that in perspective: a mammal that size should live about four years. This one lived beyond forty. It out-performed its body's warranty by roughly ten times — the biggest overshoot ever recorded in a mammal. This week, scientists finally read the instruction set behind it."
**[0:45-2:00] The record-holder.** Brandt's bat: 4-8 grams; longevity quotient 9.8, highest known (peer-reviewed: 2005 field record, J. Mammalogy via PubMed; 2013 Nature Communications genome). Humans, for comparison, score ~4.5. [B-ROLL: scale graphic, LQ chart]
**[2:00-4:00] The new study.** Nature, this week: eight near-complete Myotis genomes. Findings, labeled peer-reviewed: a distinctive gene-copy mechanism for DNA repair; genome-wide positive selection on proteins that interact with DNA viruses; elevated copy-number variation for RNA-virus-interacting proteins. Verbatim co-lead quote (via UVM announcement): "Pathogen adaptation, longevity, and cancer resistance — they are fundamentally linked." [B-ROLL: helix animation, paper on screen]
**[4:00-6:00] Why viruses built a cancer shield.** Plain-language mechanism: viruses damage DNA → 50M years of viral pressure forced extraordinary repair machinery → the same machinery suppresses tumors and slows aging. The honest caveats, on camera: correlation across species is not a human therapy; nobody can port bat repair genes into people; no product exists, and anyone selling one is ahead of the science by decades.
**[6:00-7:30] The preserve-now close (no overpromising).** The pattern across this entire week of genomics news — a complete diploid human benchmark in Cell, bat repair genomes in Nature — is that the *reading* side of DNA science improves every single year on a public schedule. The one thing the reading tools can't fix is a sample that no longer exists. We don't sequence, we don't interpret, and we can't make anyone live like a bat; we keep the record intact for whatever the tools become. $99/year, accredited US biorepository. [B-ROLL: kit, vault shelving] End card + description links to all three papers.
