### SEGMENT 1 (~5 min): The number, and the number everybody gets wrong

**Receipts.**
- **Cornell sociologist Karl Pillemer ran the first large-scale national survey of family estrangement in the United States**, published in his **2020 book *Fault Lines: Fractured Families and How to Mend Them*** *(original national survey reported at book length; SIX YEARS OLD, and no newer large-scale US prevalence measure was located in our sweep. Say the year on air.)*
- **27 percent of US adults had cut off contact with a family member.** Roughly 67 million people.
- **THE CORRECTION, and it leads the segment: the 27 percent is routinely misquoted as the share estranged from a PARENT. It is not. It covers any relative. The parent-or-child figure is about 10 percent**, roughly 25 million.
- **STANCE:** the inflated version gets used to sell things, and the real number is quite large enough. **One adult in ten is not a fringe case needing a special plan. It is a normal planning input that planning content pretends does not exist.**

**CLIP MOMENT:** *"Twenty-seven percent is the number you have seen. It is the wrong number. The one that matters is ten, and ten is plenty."*

### SEGMENT 2 (~5 min): What the statute actually does

**Receipts.**
- **Intestacy allocates by relationship category and does not assess closeness.** An estranged child inherits with the class.
- **Exclusion generally requires explicit testamentary language.** Attorneys name the excluded person because an ambiguous document invites a court back toward the default.
- **Succession is STATE law and varies, and that is the one thing never to drop.** **Louisiana is the clearest illustration because it is the only US civil-law state: under Civil Code article 1618 a disinherison must be made in a form prescribed for testaments, and current Louisiana law provides no authority to disinherit a child without a will at all.**
- **STANCE:** Louisiana is named as Louisiana and never generalised. **Generalising from the one civil-law state to the other forty-nine would be the kind of error this show exists to avoid.**

**CLIP MOMENT:** *"The statute does not have a field for how it went. It has a field for what you were to each other on paper."*

### SEGMENT 3 (~4 min): A state is actively studying this right now

**Receipts.**
- **The Louisiana Legislature has authorized and directed the Louisiana State Law Institute to study intestate succession and mechanisms to prohibit an intestate successor from inheriting when estranged. Findings are due no later than 4 February 2027** *(legislative instrument: a STUDY DIRECTIVE, not a change in law. Say that sentence on air, twice.)*
- **STANCE:** this is worth watching and worth nobody planning around. **Nothing has changed. A study is a study.**
- **The broader point: the rules here are moving slowly, in one state at a time, and the thing that protects you in the meantime is a document with names in it.**

**CLIP MOMENT:** *"A state is formally asking whether somebody you have not spoken to in nine years should still inherit. The answer is due in February 2027, and until then the answer is yes."*

### SEGMENT 4 (~4 min): What we do not do

**Talking points.**
- **Declare the stake: we sell DNA storage. So here is the part that is against our own interest.**
- **A preserved sample does not repair an estrangement. A recorded message is not a reconciliation. Anyone selling you one as though it were is selling you something else.**
- **What a record actually does is narrower: it removes a question from the future.** A grandchild you have never met, asking in 2070 what ran in the family, gets an answer that does not require anyone to have forgiven anyone. **A biological record is decision-neutral: it serves a reconciliation and a permanent silence equally well.**
- **And the honest downside, said out loud: if you are estranged BECAUSE of something, a record you leave is a record somebody may eventually read or test.** That is a real consideration and it is the listener's to weigh. **We are not going to pretend preservation is costless in every family.**
