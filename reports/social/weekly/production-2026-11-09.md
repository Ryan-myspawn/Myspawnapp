"Those three sentences are the whole video, so let me spend the rest of it on why each one matters.

**A patent application is a claim about what you think you can do.** It is written early, deliberately, because the point of filing is to get a date on the idea before someone else does. **Filing is evidence of ambition and of a lawyer. It is not evidence of a result.**

**A granted patent is a different thing and still not what you want.** It means an examiner agreed the claim was novel and non-obvious against what was already published. **It does not mean the method works reliably, at scale, in humans, or at all in the way the title suggests.**

**And a working method is the only one of the three that changes anything for a person**, and it is the one that produces papers, trial numbers and regulators, none of which are produced by a filing."

**Seven years, and the named obstacle has not moved.**

That is not a criticism of anybody. Hard problems are hard, and the honest reading of a stable bottleneck is that the people working on it are being straight with us about where they are.

**But it does tell you how to read the next headline.** When you see a story about lab-grown eggs or sperm, the question is not whether cells were made. **It is whether anything matured.** If the piece does not say, the answer is almost certainly no, and the omission is doing work."
