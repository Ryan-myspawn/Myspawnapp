### Cold open, word for word (about 85 seconds)

> Here is a test you can run on any market, and it takes about ten seconds.
>
> Ask whether a comparison site exists. Not whether it is any good. Just whether somebody thought it was worth building.
>
> Because a comparison site is a business built on a gap. It only has customers if buyers cannot work out, from the sellers themselves, what a thing actually costs. Nobody has ever built a price-comparison engine for a loaf of bread.
>
> So when you find one, you have learned something about the category before you have read a single number on it. You have learned that the sellers' own numbers did not settle the question.
>
> Today I want to look at one of those businesses, in a market next door to ours. And the single most interesting thing on its site is not a price at all. It is a percentage, about what the prices leave out.
>
> And then I want to turn the test around, because it works on us too, and I am going to tell you what it says.

### Segment 1: The test, and why it is not cynical

**Stance:** the existence of a comparison layer is information, not an accusation.

**Talking points.**
- **A comparison business is evidence of a gap, and a gap is not the same as dishonesty.** Prices can be genuinely hard to state: variable inputs, local costs, things that happen only sometimes.
- **What it reliably tells you is that the buyer could not finish the arithmetic alone.**
- **Say on air: this is a test about CATEGORIES, not about companies.** No individual seller is accused of anything by it.

**CLIP MOMENT:** "Nobody built a price-comparison engine for a loaf of bread. When one exists, the category has told you something."

### Segment 2: The example, and the percentage

**Source:** the published pages of a US egg-freezing comparison service, read **through a search index and not opened at its own domain.** **The label is spoken on air.**

**Talking points.**
- The service publishes **metro-by-metro guides** and a clinic comparison tool across the US market.
- Its own national figure: **an average of $14,364 for one cycle, as of January 2025, based on research into more than 500 US clinics.** **State the date on air. That figure is twenty-two months old at the time of recording and it is still the number on the page.**
- Metro examples it lists: **one city at $8,150 to $11,250**, another city's lowest listed cycle at **$7,400.**
- Its comparison tool is built to show **"one complete price for the entire cycle, which includes all costs (except medications)."** **Note the parenthesis out loud. The product that exists to make prices comparable still has to carve something out.**
- **AND THE NUMBER THE WHOLE EPISODE IS FOR: of the clinics it holds that data for, 91 percent DID NOT include pre-cycle labs in the cycle price.**
- **That is the missing column. One company exists, in part, because nine out of ten sellers quote a price that stops short of the thing the buyer thinks they are buying.**

**CLIP MOMENT:** "Ninety-one percent. That is not a scandal. That is a convention, and a convention is harder to see than a scandal."

### Segment 3: Turning it on ourselves, on air

**Stance:** run the test on MySpawn and say what it returns.

**Talking points.**
- **Nobody has built a comparison site for DNA storage.** Two readings are available and we are not going to pick the flattering one.
- **The flattering reading:** our price is one number, annual, with nothing carved out, so there is nothing to compare.
- **The unflattering reading, and it is at least as likely: the category is too small to be worth building one for.** **A market with no comparison layer may be transparent or may simply be ignored.**
- **The test that distinguishes them is not ours to run.** What we can do is behave as though the comparison site existed: **publish the one number, say what it includes, and name what it does not.**
- **Our own carve-out, stated plainly: $99 a year, billed annually, covers storage of the record. It is not a test, not a treatment, and not a promise about what the record will support later.**

**CLIP MOMENT:** "A market with no comparison site is either clear or ignored, and the difference is not something the seller gets to decide."

### Segment 4: The habit

**Stance:** practical, transferable, no fear.

**Talking points.**
- Three questions that work on any quoted price: what does it exclude, when was it last updated, and who checked it other than the seller.
- **The second one matters more than people expect.** A price page with no date is a price page you cannot audit, **and the example in this episode carries a twenty-two-month-old national average on a live site.**
- **Close on the inversion: the most useful thing on a comparison site is usually not the comparison. It is the column the comparison had to invent.**

### Outro CTA

> If a market has a comparison site, read the footnotes before the figures. If it does not, ask yourself whether that is because the prices are simple or because nobody is watching.
>
> MySpawn stores a DNA record for $99 a year, billed annually. The first 1,000 members lock the founding rate. One number, and we will keep telling you what it does not include.

## YOUTUBE LONG-FORM EXPLAINER (7 to 9 minutes)

### Title: "Preserving Cells To Use Them Later: What That Actually Means Right Now"

**Freshness check:** **`secretome` returns ZERO across the production log, the weekly directory and all 86 blog articles.** The subject has never been covered by this fleet in any form.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55.** **The 29-year-old optimizer is the one most likely to be sold this, and the 50-year-old estate planner is the one who will ask what the evidence is.**
**Trend-hook used:** two 2026 clinical studies by a cell-preservation company, newly discovered in today's competitor sweep and not previously in our files.

**THESIS: there are two different businesses that both describe themselves as preserving your cells, and the difference is what they intend to do with them.** One keeps a record. The other keeps an input for a treatment it hopes to sell you. **Both can be honest. They are not the same purchase and they do not carry the same risk.**

**SCRIPT**

**[B-ROLL: a plain laboratory corridor, no people, no branded equipment.]**

Some companies store biological material so that a record exists. Others store it because they plan to make something out of it. **That second business has been moving this year, and almost nobody outside it has noticed.**

**[B-ROLL: a hand closing a storage drawer. No faces.]**

**The material in question is called a secretome.** Cells in culture release a mixture of proteins, signalling molecules and small vesicles. **Collect what the cells put out rather than the cells themselves, and that mixture is the secretome.** Exosomes, which you may have seen advertised, are one component of it.

**[ON SCREEN: "secretome: what the cells release, not the cells"]**

**A company in the cell-preservation category is running trials on exactly this.** **In January 2026 it reported the first patients treated in a randomised hair-regeneration study using its secretome.** **On 10 March 2026 it announced an injectable study for facial rejuvenation, using personalised vials made from each patient's own preserved cells.** **LABEL, and say it on camera: both of those are COMPANY ANNOUNCEMENTS, not published results.**

**There is also a registered trial, and a registration is a useful thing because it is a public commitment to a protocol.** **ClinicalTrials.gov lists NCT07107841 for an autologous hair follicle secretome in androgenic alopecia.** The listing describes injections at baseline and at days 30, 90, 180, 270 and 365. **A one-year follow-up schedule tells you the honest thing about the timeline: the people running it do not expect to know quickly.**

**[B-ROLL: a calendar with six marks, no text legible.]**

**Now the part that matters most, and it is the part marketing pages skip.**

**As of October 2026, there are no FDA-approved exosome products for treating hair loss or any other medical condition.** **LABEL: that statement comes from a patient-advocacy association, and it is consistent with the regulatory picture below.**

**A 2026 review in a dermatology journal, peer-reviewed, sets out why.** Products intended to affect biological function, including tissue regeneration, inflammation or wound healing, **are regulated as drugs or biologics under Section 351 of the Public Health Service Act and require premarket review and approval.** **That is the bar. Nothing in this category has cleared it.**

**The clinical evidence is early, and the field says so about itself.** A 2026 paper on alopecia areata describes clinical evidence for exosome therapy in alopecia as **scarce**. Early studies in androgenetic alopecia and acne scars **report favourable safety without serious adverse events**, which is genuinely encouraging and is a different claim from working. **And the advocacy association states there is no established human clinical evidence that exosome treatment can reliably create entirely new, fully functional scalp hair follicles.**

**[ON SCREEN: "safe so far" and "works" are different findings]**

**TWO THINGS WE FOUND AND ARE NOT TELLING YOU.** A clinic-run page reports that the FDA issued warning letters to exosome clinics in three states earlier this year. **We could not verify that against an FDA source, so this video does not state it.** A second clinic page cites a 2023 systematic review finding sixteen studies with varying efficacy. **That figure reaches us only through a marketing page, so we are not repeating the number.**

**[B-ROLL: a screen scrolling a document, text not legible.]**

**So what should you take from this?**

**Running an early trial with a one-year follow-up is the honest way to do this.** **A company that registers a protocol and waits a year for an endpoint is behaving better than one that sells the same idea off a website today.** **This explainer is not a criticism of anybody doing the trial. It is a warning about everybody who will sell you the conclusion before the trial finishes.**

**[CLOSE. B-ROLL: the storage drawer again, closed.]**

**And here is where we sit, said plainly.** **MySpawn is the other business.** We store a DNA record. **We do not culture cells, we do not produce a secretome, we run no trials, and we sell no treatment.** **$99 a year, billed annually, and the first 1,000 members lock the founding rate.**

**That is a smaller promise and it is the only one we can keep.** **If what you want is an input for a future treatment, the companies above are the category to look at, and the thing to ask them is which endpoint, at what time point, published where.** **If what you want is a record that exists, that is us, and the honest limit is that a record is all it is.**
