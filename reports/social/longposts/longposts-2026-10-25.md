## #162 [X] The tax that exists to stop you skipping a generation

**Lane: practical legacy guide. Product mention: one line.**

Most people have never heard of the generation-skipping transfer tax. It is a 40 percent flat rate, and it exists for one reason: to stop you doing something you probably did not know you could do.

**Leave money straight to a grandchild, and United States tax law calls it a skip.** The estate tax assumes wealth gets taxed once per generation on the way down. Skipping a turn skips a tax, so Congress built a second one to catch it.

**The number that matters in 2026 is about $15 million per person**, roughly $30 million for a married couple. It rose from $13,990,000 in 2025 under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed 4 July 2025, which amended the basic exclusion amount; the generation-skipping exemption tracks it. *(Law-firm and advisory commentary, not an IRS page we opened. Verify against Form 709 instructions before acting.)*

**Which means: this tax is irrelevant to almost every American family.** If you are leaving a grandchild $20,000, stop reading about the tax and read the next three paragraphs instead.

**Separately, the annual gift exclusion is $19,000 per recipient for 2026**, unchanged from 2025, per Revenue Procedure 2025-32, issued October 2025. *(Advisory commentary.)*

### Three ways a transfer gets caught

**A direct skip** is a gift or bequest straight to a skip person. **A taxable termination** is an interest in a trust ending so that only skip persons remain, which classically arrives when your child dies, decades after you signed anything. **A taxable distribution** is everything else a trust pays a skip person.

The second and third arrive after you are no longer available to be asked anything. **A direct skip is a decision you make and see. A taxable termination is one a trustee administers against facts you never knew.**

### The rule that promotes a grandchild, and the trap in it

Under section 2651(e) of the tax code, **if your child is already dead at the relevant moment, your grandchild moves up a generation and it is not a skip at all.** The provision replaced a narrower one under the 1997 Act and was broadened to reach taxable terminations and distributions, and collateral heirs.

**The timing is strict and it is where people get hurt.** The test is whether the parent is dead when the transfer is FIRST subject to estate or gift tax, and where there is more than one such moment, the earliest. **Fund a trust for your child with a remainder to your grandchild, and if your child dies afterwards, the exception does not reach back.** *(That trap is practitioner commentary, not authority.)*

Treasury also closed a side door in 2005: **a person who disclaims an inheritance is not treated as a predeceased parent** merely because state law pretends a disclaimant died first.

**Same trust. Same family. Different answer, decided entirely by the order in which two things happened.**

Every document you sign assumes a sequence nobody gets to choose. That is true whether you are moving fifteen million dollars or a box of photographs. MySpawn stores a physical DNA record for $99 a year, billed annually, and it is not an estate plan, a tax strategy or a legal instrument. It is one object that survives the handover intact.

This is general information about how a tax works, not legal or tax advice.

## #163 [FACEBOOK] Your power of attorney probably stops at your inbox

**Lane: trust and custody. Product mention: one line. Ends with a question.**

**FRESHNESS DECLARATION, WRITTEN INTO THE FILE RATHER THAN HIDDEN: this operation has covered the fiduciary-access hierarchy before, on 29 August, and a later run killed an executor-side topic for being too close to it.** This article is the other side: **the agent, while you are still alive.** The three-tier hierarchy is not re-explained here.

Here is a distinction almost nobody makes until the week they need it.

**An agent under a power of attorney acts while you are ALIVE.** Their authority starts immediately or on incapacity, **and it ends the moment you die.**

**An executor acts after you die.** Their authority starts where the agent's ended.

So picture the gap. **You have a stroke at 58. Your executor has no authority at all, because you are not dead.** Your agent is the only person holding anything, and what your agent can actually reach depends on a clause most powers of attorney do not contain.

### Catalogue and content

Digital-asset law splits your accounts in two.

**The catalogue is the metadata.** Who communicated with whom, on what date, under what subject line. **The content is what was actually said.**

**For content, the sources agree: an agent reaches it only where the power of attorney EXPRESSLY authorises it.** A general "all my property" clause is described as insufficient. *(Law-firm commentary on state enactments.)*

**For the catalogue, two states are described as answering differently, and that disagreement is the most useful thing here.** One Pennsylvania firm's write-up says a general grant of authority DOES reach the catalogue, with content still needing express words, citing 20 Pa.C.S. section 3909. One Connecticut guide says a general grant reaches NEITHER, with content governed separately at section 45a-334j.

**We cannot resolve that from here and we will not pretend to.** Both are secondary descriptions of enacted state variants of a model act, **and state variants are exactly where a model act stops being uniform.** The practical answer is the same either way: do not rely on a general clause.

### Why this bites harder on a genetic account

Think about what the catalogue tier actually gives you on a DNA testing service. **You learn that a message was exchanged with a match, on a date.** You do not learn the match, the relationship, the health reports, or the raw file.

**On a DNA account, almost everything of value sits on the content side of the line.**

Then the second wall, which is not legal at all. **Consumer genetic companies do not generally recognise fiduciaries. They recognise a product setting.** Some offer a named legacy contact. Some offer nothing, and the widely repeated advice is that you hand over your password. **A statute that entitles your agent to something is of limited use against a support queue with no process for it.**

The fix is unglamorous: put an express digital-assets clause in the document, name the content of electronic communications in terms, set the company's own tool where one exists, and write down which services hold what. An agent with perfect authority and no inventory still has nothing to act on.

MySpawn stores a physical sample for $99 a year, billed annually, which removes the login problem and replaces it with a custody agreement you should read with exactly this much suspicion. Naming us does not name an agent.

Not legal advice. Have an attorney in your state draft the clause against your state's enacted version.

**So: does the person you would trust with your life actually have the paperwork to act for you, or just the relationship?**

## #165 [FACEBOOK] Possession and custody are not the same product

**Lane: trust and custody, story-driven. Product mention: one paragraph. NO COMPETITOR IS NAMED, and that is deliberate.**

There are two completely different things people mean when they say they are "keeping" someone's DNA, and the difference decides what happens in thirty years.

**POSSESSION is an object you hold.** A sealed capsule, collected at need, often through a funeral provider, handed back to the family. It lives in a drawer, a safe or a shelf. **Nobody is monitoring it. Nothing is logged. There is no third party to fail, because there is no third party.**

**CUSTODY is an arrangement.** Somebody else holds the material in a facility, under a documented chain, usually for a recurring fee. **There is a third party, and that third party can fail.**

**Both are real. Both can fail. They fail in opposite directions.**

The possession model is more engineered than people assume. In the version we have studied, **the sample is collected by the funeral provider using supplies in a kit, and collection has to happen BEFORE cremation or embalming.** The material is then extracted, purified and preserved, and **the capsule comes back to the family in triple-sealed 316L stainless steel with a humidity regulator inside, typically in 10 to 14 business days.** *(Company's own public pages, read through a search index and not opened at source.)*

That is a serious object. **It is also an object whose entire future depends on one family remembering what it is.**

A well-kept object in a careful family can outlast a badly run company. That is not a concession we enjoy making and it is true. **An object has no renewal date, no billing failure and no corporate parent.** What it also has is no temperature control, no inventory system and nobody who notices when the house is sold.

### The comparison we got wrong, and are correcting in public

Since August this operation has been carrying a line comparing our annual fee with a competitor's one-off price. **We killed it this week.**

**It was not a like-for-like comparison and it should never have been drafted.** One is a recurring fee for an arrangement; the other is a single payment for an object. **A thesis that depends on one-time versus recurring is not a pricing argument, it is a category error with a number attached.**

**The honest frame is custody RISK against possession RISK, and it is not a slam dunk for us.**

- **Hold it yourself:** you are the single point of failure, and nobody is checking.
- **Let an institution hold it:** you depend on that institution's continuity, and on your family being able to reach it.

### What to ask either way

**Which facility actually holds it. At what temperature. Under which accreditations. And can you see the certificate.**

Four questions. Use them on anyone selling you a future, including us.

## #166 [X] Four questions that work on any biotech claim

**Lane: contrarian discourse take. PUNCHING AT A METHOD, NOT AT A PERSON OR A COMPANY. Product mention: one line.**

Here are four questions. They are not mine. I took them from a paragraph someone wrote about a viral biology claim, and the paragraph was better reporting than most of what gets written about this industry.

**Which laboratory did the work. Where are the results. Is there an assay certificate. Is there an independently reviewed paper.**

The useful thing about them is that they do not require you to understand the science. **They require the person making the claim to have done ordinary paperwork.**

### Score it out of four. Not true or false.

This matters. **Nobody reading a feed can adjudicate true or false.** What anybody can do is count how much of the ordinary paperwork exists.

**Zero out of four does not mean someone is lying. It means nobody can check.** Those are different accusations and only one of them is available to you.

### Try it on three things, in increasing order of discomfort

**One: a viral claim from July 2026.** The post read, verbatim: "I just cloned myself... as a newborn," with the result said to live in a petri dish for now. The same post added: "With this clone, I can become my own blood boy, test therapies on the clone, grow organs for transplantation, develop new treatments and inject young cells."  **The procedure described is induced pluripotent stem cell reprogramming.** No embryo, no fetus, no organ and no second person was created. Commentary at the time noted no laboratory identified, no results published, no assay certificate and no independently reviewed paper. **Zero of four, and still circulating three months later.** The checklist did not need to know any cell biology to get there. It only needed to notice what was missing. *(Epistemic status: reported, with a published debunk. We punch at the word "cloned", never at the man.)*
