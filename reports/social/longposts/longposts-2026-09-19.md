## ARTICLE #77 [FACEBOOK]
### The Person You Name Is Not Allowed to Open Anything

**Freshness check:** No longpost has covered platform legacy settings. #68 covered a state genetic-privacy statute and #73 covered lab retention policy; neither touched consumer account tools, and neither used a permissions list as its structure.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55
**Trend / hook used:** TikTok shipped a Legacy Account setting in late August 2026, and the restriction list is the most honest thing any platform has published about death.
**Word count: 579**

Sometime in late August, TikTok added a setting most people still have not found. It asks what should happen to your profile after you die. You can name another user to look after it, or you can choose to have it deleted. Naming somebody is optional.

*(First reported Monday 24 August 2026 from screenshots of the in-app setting, then covered by Social Media Today, MediaPost on 26 August, and BetaNews. **Epistemic status: multi-outlet trade reporting of a shipped setting, not a TikTok press release.** TikTok has not published a country-by-country rollout list, so availability varies by region and app version.)

That is unremarkable. Facebook has had a version since 2015. What stopped me was the list of what the person you name is actually allowed to do.

**They cannot log in. They cannot remove or edit your posts. They cannot read your messages, or your comments on other people's posts. They cannot remove your friends.**

Read that again, because it is not stinginess and it is not an oversight. It is a company telling you, in the plainest language any of them has used, what a legacy setting is for. You are not naming somebody to receive anything. You are naming somebody to stand outside a locked door and make sure nobody else goes in.

**Every platform does this, and the reason is good.**

Apple has Digital Legacy. Google has Inactive Account Manager. Meta has Legacy Contact and memorialization. Microsoft's stated position is that it will not release account content even with a death certificate. *(Platform documentation; confirm each against the live help page, they change.)*

The reason is not corporate coldness. Your messages are half somebody else's. Every conversation you have ever had has another person in it who never agreed to have their words handed to your relatives. A company that opened your inbox to your family would be doing something worse than keeping it shut.

So the restriction is defensible. It is also a limit, and limits are worth knowing precisely.

**Ten minutes tonight, and then the harder question.**

Go and set all four. Apple, Google, Meta, and TikTok if your version has it. They take a few minutes each and almost nobody does it. It genuinely helps: it stops your accounts becoming a problem for people who are already having the worst week of their lives.

Now look at what you have actually arranged. Four containers, each with somebody appointed to mind it. Not one thing delivered to one person.

That is a different job, and it does not happen by accident. The things worth receiving are the ones that were only ever yours: a recording made on purpose, for one named person, with nobody else's privacy inside it. A video message has no third party in it. That is precisely why a platform will never deliver one for you and why it has to be arranged separately.

We build that second thing, so treat this as an interested party telling you to go and use the free tools first. I would tell you to do those ten minutes either way.

**Here is the part I keep turning over.** The clearest sentence written about digital death this year was written by a legal team, as a limitation, in a settings menu almost nobody opens. Not by a philosopher. Not by us.

Have you set any of the four? And if you have named somebody, did you know what they would actually be allowed to do?

## ARTICLE #78 [X]
### They Put It in One Eye on Purpose

**Freshness check:** #64 covered partial reprogramming on 13 September, but through funding totals against timelines, opening on money. This article does not mention a funding figure and is built entirely on one trial's engineering. The June dosing fact was not known to us when #64 was written.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55
**Trend / hook used:** On 9 June 2026 a partial reprogramming therapy went into a human being for the first time, and the design tells you more than the headline.
**Word count: 609**

On 9 June 2026, Life Biosciences announced that the first patient had been dosed with ER-100. On the company's account it is the first time a partial epigenetic reprogramming therapy has been given to a human being.

**Epistemic status: company announcement plus trade coverage plus a registry listing. Not peer-reviewed. No results published.**

Almost every write-up led with the milestone. The interesting part is the engineering, because the engineering is an argument about safety.

**It went into an eye.** Specifically, into retinal cells, in people with open-angle glaucoma or non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy. Trial ID **NCT07290244**.

**It is delivered by a virus.** A modified adeno-associated virus carries three genes: **OCT4, SOX2 and KLF4**, known as OSK.

**There is a fourth Yamanaka factor, and they left it out.** The classic set is OSKM, where M is c-MYC, the one most associated with cancer. It is not in this.

**It does not switch itself on.** Expression is controlled by taking **doxycycline**, an ordinary oral antibiotic, for **eight weeks**. The off switch is a pill you stop taking.

Now the history that explains all four choices.

In 2016, Ocampo and colleagues published in *Cell* that short cyclic bursts of OSKM improved aging hallmarks and extended lifespan in a mouse model of premature aging. **The same paper reported that sustained high expression produced teratomas in the liver, kidney and pancreas.** *(Peer-reviewed. Mice.)* The difference between a therapy and a tumor was the dosing schedule.

In 2020, Lu and colleagues published in *Nature* that OSK alone, delivered to mouse retinal ganglion cells, restored youthful DNA methylation patterns, promoted nerve fibre regeneration and reversed vision loss in a glaucoma model and in aged mice, dependent on the enzymes TET1 and TET2. *(Peer-reviewed. Mice.)*

So: the eye because it is contained. OSK without c-MYC because of the tumor risk. A drug-controlled switch because the 2016 paper showed what happens when there isn't one.

**What this trial is not.** It is a Phase 1. Phase 1 asks whether something is safe and tolerable, not whether it works. The listed endpoints are safety, tolerability, immune response and visual assessments. **Nothing in it measures anyone's age.** It is a first-in-human study in an eye disease, and calling it an age-reversal trial is the first place coverage goes wrong.

**One more detail worth having, because it is the part people get wrong in the other direction.** The eye was not chosen only because it is small. It is an immune-privileged compartment, which means the body polices it differently from the rest of you, and an AAV vector injected there is far less likely to provoke the kind of systemic immune response that has ended gene-therapy programmes before. So the site is doing three jobs at once: it limits where the therapy can travel, it lowers the immune risk, and it happens to be the exact tissue where the 2020 mouse result was strongest. That is not a compromise. That is picking the one place where every argument points the same way.

**What would change my mind:** a published readout showing the AAV vector is tolerated and the doxycycline switch behaves in humans as it did in mice. That has not happened. Registration is not data.

If you take one thing: the containment is not caution around the edges of the idea. **The containment is the idea.** Everything interesting here is a decision about how to stop.

We store genetic records. That is unrelated to this, confers no health benefit, and grants access to no trial. I am writing about it because it is the most carefully built experiment in the field and almost nobody described how it was built.

### A Journal Retracted a Plasmalogen Study This Year. Nobody Covering Plasmalogens Mentioned It.

**Freshness check:** #70 was also a date-check piece, so this article deliberately does **not** open on the age of a study. It opens on a retraction and uses the recirculation as a supporting fact rather than the hook. No longpost has covered plasmalogens, supplement evidence grading, or a retraction.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55
**Trend / hook used:** A wave of "seafood reverses aging" coverage on 2 September 2026, sitting on top of a 2022 paper and a 2026 retraction.
**Word count: 645**

In **January 2026**, the journal *Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology* issued an Expression of Concern about a human plasmalogen study, citing **serious concerns regarding compliance with local legislation**. The research integrity team investigated. **The article was subsequently retracted**, with the investigation finding the complaints valid and the paper not meeting standards of editorial and scientific soundness.

*(Publisher notices. Read the journal's own notice for the exact status and date before repeating this.)*

I went looking for that after reading a month of coverage about plasmalogens, and not one piece I saw mentioned it.

**Start with what is true.** Plasmalogens are real and they matter. They are ether phospholipids, structural fats in every cell membrane you own, unusually concentrated in the brain, heart and immune cells, and built in a cell compartment called the peroxisome. Their levels fall with age and are reported lower in people with Alzheimer's disease.

That is a genuine mechanism. It is not evidence that swallowing them helps. A substance being depleted in a disease does not mean replacing it treats the disease, and nutrition science is a graveyard of that exact inference.

**The largest human trial was negative.** Fujino and colleagues, ***eBioMedicine*, 2017**: a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. **328 participants aged 60 to 85** with mild Alzheimer's disease or mild cognitive impairment. **1 mg per day of scallop-derived purified plasmalogen or placebo, 24 weeks.** Primary outcome, the Japanese Mini-Mental State Examination.

**No significant difference on the primary outcome. In the intention-to-treat analysis, none on any secondary outcome either.** A memory improvement appeared in subgroup analyses of mild Alzheimer's patients, and that subgroup is what supplement pages quote.

A subgroup finding inside a trial that missed its primary endpoint is a hypothesis for the next trial. Split any dataset enough ways and something looks positive.

**A small 2026 trial was positive.** *Frontiers in Nutrition*, 2026: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, **68 cognitively healthy Japanese adults aged 40 and over with below-average verbal memory**, **1 mg per day, 12 weeks**. Verbal memory improved against placebo. *(Peer-reviewed.)*

Four limits ride along, and none of them is a reason to dismiss it. Sixty-eight people is small, and small trials overestimate effects as a rule. Twelve weeks says nothing about something people would take for decades. The group was selected for below-average memory. It is one measure.

**And the story you actually saw was four years old.** The coverage on **2 September 2026** about scientists finding that a compound in edible sea squirts reverses signs of aging in old mice is real research, reported accurately, and published in **2022**: *Plasmalogens Eliminate Aging-Associated Synaptic Defects and Microglia-Mediated Neuroinflammation in Mice*, *Frontiers in Molecular Biosciences*, led by Professor Lei Fu at Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University with collaborators at Stanford, Shanghai Jiao Tong and the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences. Sixteen-month-old mice, two months of supplementation, better memory, more synapses, less neuroinflammation, thicker darker fur. *(Peer-reviewed. Mice. 2022.)*

**So what is a reasonable position?** The mechanism is real. The animal data is interesting and comes from serious institutions. The human data is one null trial, one small positive trial, a retraction, and some smaller work on mood and concentration. The dose in both major trials is the same, **1 mg per day**, which is a useful thing to check a product against. And nobody has shown plasmalogen supplementation extends human life.

If you take them, take them as an experiment with a modest prior. Not as a proven protocol item.

I sell nothing related to any of this and I am not telling you what to swallow. What I would like you to take is the checking move: **when was it published, how many people were in it, and did it meet its primary endpoint?** Three questions, a few minutes.

What is the last supplement you bought, and do you know the answer to those three questions about it?

- Fujino et al., *eBioMedicine* 2017, vol 17 pp 199-205: https://www.thelancet.com/article/S2352-3964(17)30071-3/fulltext *(via search index: verify before posting)*

## ARTICLE #80 [X] · ZERO PRODUCT
### The First AI-Discovered Drug Reached Phase III This Month. Zero Have Reached Approval.

**Freshness check:** No longpost has covered AI drug discovery, rentosertib, or approval-pipeline epistemics. #64 covered longevity funding and #72 covered a Phase 1 autophagy candidate; neither concerned regulatory milestones or AI-designed molecules.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55
**Trend / hook used:** GENESIS-IPF-3 dosed its first patient on 9 September 2026.
**Word count: 536**

On **9 September 2026**, Insilico Medicine dosed the first patient in **GENESIS-IPF-3** at Peking Union Medical College Hospital, with Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital enrolling its first patient the same day. The company describes it as the world's first Phase III trial of a generative-AI-driven drug.

**Epistemic status: company announcement, widely covered in trade press. Not a result.**

The design, because the numbers are the story: **52 weeks, prospective, randomized, multi-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled, an expected 320 participants across 47 centers in China.** Primary endpoint: **the annual rate of decline in forced vital capacity over 52 weeks.** Key secondary: time to first occurrence of any disease progression event. Led by Professor Zuojun Xu, with Academician Nanshan Zhong and Professor Chang Chen as co-leading principal investigators.

The drug is **rentosertib**, and it targets **TNIK**, a protein not previously strongly associated with fibrosis. What makes it a first is that **both the target and the molecule came out of AI systems**, not just one.

Two weeks earlier, on **7 September 2026**, *Nature Biotechnology* published the Phase 2a: **42 patients**, with six independently developed proteomic aging clocks built in as exploratory endpoints. Patients in the 30mg twice-daily arm scored roughly **2.7 to 3.5 years younger than expected** on those clocks at week four. *(Peer-reviewed.)*

Hold that carefully. **That is younger biomarkers, not younger patients**, in 42 people over four weeks, in a trial for a lung disease. A protein panel reading younger is a measurement of a proxy.

**Now the fact that should anchor all of it.** As of today, **no AI-discovered drug has received full FDA approval.** Not one. The field is roughly a decade into enormous funding and confident language, and the column that matters is still empty.

That is not a takedown. Drug development is slow for reasons that are mostly good, and a Phase III starting is genuinely a milestone. It is a correction to the register in which this gets discussed. "AI is designing drugs" is true. "AI-designed drugs work" is an open question with a 52-week primary endpoint attached to it and a readout nobody has.

**Why idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, and not aging.** It is worth noticing what disease this is actually being tested in. IPF is a progressive scarring of the lungs with a genuinely grim prognosis and very few options, which is why the primary endpoint is lung function rather than anything resembling a longevity measure. The aging clocks in the Phase 2a were **exploratory endpoints**, bolted onto a trial about breathing. That is the honest and rather ordinary way most geroscience signals actually appear: not in a trial designed to slow aging, but as a secondary observation inside a trial designed to treat a specific disease. Anyone selling you the reverse order is selling you something.

**The general skill, which costs ninety seconds.** When you hear that some technology is transforming medicine, ask three questions. What has actually started? What phase is it in? What has been published?

Applied here: a Phase III started on 9 September. One Phase 2a is published. Zero approvals exist.

Applied to almost anything else you will read this year, those three questions will do more work than any amount of reading about the technology itself.

## ARTICLE #81 [REDDIT r/SideProject]
### I spent a month marketing a feature I thought was ours. A competitor has had it for years.

**Freshness check:** #66 and #76 were both founder ops essays for r/Entrepreneur, on error-counting and on channel timing. This is a different subreddit, a different failure (a positioning error found in routine competitor research, not a process error), and a different hook structure.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55
**Trend / hook used:** A routine competitor sweep on 18 September 2026 turned up a mechanic I had been describing as distinctive.
**Word count: 598**

Disclosure first: I work on a small paid product, MySpawn, and I am going to describe a mistake I made marketing it. There is no link and nothing to buy here. Mods, remove if it reads wrong.

The product is, in the plainest words I have, **a time capsule with your genome in it**: you send in a sample, it gets stored at a third-party accredited facility, and separately you can seal videos and photos that unlock on a date you choose. $99 a year.

For about a month I have been leaning on a line I was pleased with. Roughly: *anyone can keep a file, the part worth paying for is that it opens on a day you chose, for a person you named.* I built a lot of copy on it.

**On Thursday I did a routine competitor sweep and found that Remento already does the date part.**

Remento is a family-storytelling product. Weekly prompts by email or text, the person answers out loud by voice or video with no app, transcription turns it into a printed book, and the book carries QR codes back to the original recording so you can hear it in their voice. **$99 a year**, which is also our price. The founder took **$300,000 from Mark Cuban on Shark Tank in March 2025**.

And when you gift it, **it emails the recipient a personalized message on a date the buyer chooses.**

So scheduled delivery is not my differentiator. It has been sitting in somebody else's gifting flow, at my price point, with national TV behind it, the whole time I was describing it as the thing worth paying for.

**Three things I actually learned, none of them flattering.**

**1. I was comparing myself to the category I came from, not the one I am in.** I benchmarked against storage companies, where dated delivery genuinely is unusual. I never benchmarked against storytelling products, where it is table stakes. Your competitive set is whoever the customer is actually choosing between, which is not a question you get to answer for them.

**2. The distinction survived, but smaller and more honest.** What is actually different is not the mechanism, it is the horizon. Their unlock is immediate: a book arrives this year and the value is consumed in year one. Ours is a date the person recording will not be present for, which is a much harder promise because it is mostly about whether the company still exists. **That is a weaker-sounding claim and a truer one**, and it puts the burden back on us where it belongs.

**3. I found this in a scheduled sweep, not by being clever.** It is a boring recurring task I nearly dropped twice because it produces nothing most days. It produced nothing on Wednesday. On Thursday it saved me from building a quarter of messaging on a false premise.

**What I do not know yet**, and I am not going to pretend otherwise: whether the horizon framing actually converts. It tests worse in my head than the line I was using, because it is less punchy and asks the reader to care about institutional durability. It might be the right claim and the wrong sentence. I have not run it.

If you are pre-launch, the cheap version of this lesson: **write down the one sentence you think is your differentiator, then spend an hour trying to find somebody who already does it.** An hour on Thursday would have saved me a month.

Happy to answer anything about the sweep process or what it costs to run.
