## Why this file exists
MySpawn's core narrative for the 25-60 audience: **the science that could one day use a preserved sample is moving fast, and preservation is the only step that can't be taken retroactively.** Every watchlist item below is a potential "front row seat" story. Accuracy is the moat: competitors will overhype; we newsjack with receipts.

**Hard accuracy rules:** (1) MySpawn is storage only: never promise descendant creation. (2) IVG has produced NO mature human gametes; no human born from a lab-made gamete. (3) Artificial wombs: partial ectogenesis (premature-infant rescue) is real and near-term; full ectogenesis (conception to birth outside a body) is speculative, decades out. (4) Always label: peer-reviewed vs company press release vs rumor.

- 2026-09-20 (EVENING): **CORRECTING MY OWN ENTRY FROM THIS MORNING. Second pre-publication fix today, sixth this month.**
  **What this morning's entry said:** that *"testosterone is dropping 1% every year"* describes a **population-level decline across cohorts, "not an individual man losing 1% a year."**
  **That was too clean, and the clean version is wrong.** **There are two separate findings and both are near one percent.**
  **(1) Individual decline.** Total testosterone falls gradually within a man as he ages, on the order of **about 1% per year from roughly his thirties**, with the exact rate varying by study and by which measure is taken. **Peer-reviewed longitudinal ageing literature. This one IS about him.**
  **(2) Cohort decline, and this is the interesting one.** **Travison et al., *Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism*, 2007**, *"A Population-Level Decline in Serum Testosterone Levels in American Men"*, using the **Massachusetts Male Aging Study** across three waves (**1987-89, 1995-97, 2002-04**), found an **age-matched decline of roughly 1.2% per year**: a **50-year-old in 1988 had higher testosterone than a comparable 50-year-old in 1996.** The age-matched declines **remained essentially unchanged** after controlling for chronic illness, general health, medications, smoking, BMI, employment and marital status. **Peer-reviewed.** Companion 2007 JCEM paper on the relative contributions of ageing, health and lifestyle; **Nyante et al., *Int J Androl* 2012** examined US national trends 1988-91 to 1999-2004.
  **APPROVED WORDING, replacing this morning's:** **"There are two different one-percent findings. One is individual ageing, which is about you. One is an age-matched decline across birth cohorts, which is about the decades, and it survived every obvious explanation the researchers tested. People quoting the number almost always mean the second and argue as if they meant the first."**
