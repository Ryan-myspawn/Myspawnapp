## Science Watch

### 1. TikTok Legacy Account: the platform change we missed
**What happened.** TikTok added a **Legacy Account** setting letting a user either nominate another user to look after the account after their death, or choose **"Delete after death."** First reported **Monday 24 August 2026** from screenshots of the setting inside account settings; covered subsequently by Social Media Today, MediaPost (26 August) and BetaNews. **Epistemic label: multi-outlet trade reporting of a shipped in-app setting, not a TikTok press release. TikTok has not published a country-by-country rollout list, so availability varies by region and app version.**

**The detail that matters.** A TikTok legacy contact is **restricted from logging into the account, from removing or editing posts, from reading the user's messages or comments on other posts, and from removing existing friends.** Selecting a contact is optional.

**So what.** This is the strongest third-party validation our framing has ever received, and it arrives as a limitation rather than as a feature. TikTok has now told a billion people that the person you name gets **custody of a profile, not access to its contents.** That is exactly the line between what a platform tool does and what an archive does. Our Memory Vault's whole proposition is the opposite arrangement: the people you choose receive the **contents**, as video messages, on a date you set. **Preserve-now angle: platform legacy settings are worth ten minutes tonight and they are not an archive, because the person you name cannot open anything.**

**Also banked from the same sweep, and checkable:** **Microsoft's stated policy forbids release of account content even with a death certificate.** Apple Digital Legacy, Google Inactive Account Manager and Meta Legacy Contact remain the three tools already cited in our Sept 4, 10 and 11 assets. **Flagged, not used:** a TechPolicy.Press essay, *"Big Tech is Becoming the Executor of the Dead,"* with no date established. **Date it before citing.**

**The miss, recorded plainly.** Our radar reported "event-driven (digital legacy / de-extinction): no waves" on **29, 30 and 31 August**. The TikTok reporting was live on **24 and 26 August**. The event-driven watch fires only when a wave is loud enough to surface in a general sweep, and a settings-screen change covered by trade press is not loud. **Process fix: the digital-legacy keyword gets an explicit named-platform query (Apple, Google, Meta, TikTok, Microsoft, X) once a week rather than relying on ambient wave detection. Added to the sweep list.**

### 2. Junevity: new company, added to the tracked list
**What.** **Junevity** announced peer-reviewed research in ***PNAS*** reporting that precise modulation of individual transcription factors can reverse core hallmarks of cellular aging and restore tissue function. Company materials describe a **"RESET" platform** and a lead **siRNA candidate, JUN_01**, aimed at type 2 diabetes and obesity, plus a mouse result described as restoring "18-year-old metabolism," with mice on a high-fat diet losing fat while retaining muscle.

**Epistemic labels, kept separate because they are different grades of evidence:** the transcription-factor mechanism paper is **peer-reviewed (*PNAS*)**; the RESET platform, the JUN_01 pipeline and the metabolism framing are **company announcement (Businesswire, 12 January 2026)**; the animal result is **mouse stage**. **Dated: January 2026, so it is a receipt, not news.**

**So what.** It matters to us for one reason: it is the cheap counterexample to the idea that reprogramming means Yamanaka factors. A single-target approach sits in the same lane as OSK with a very different risk profile. **Added to the tracked-companies list. Preserve-now angle: none, and say so. This is therapeutics, and storage is not a treatment.**
