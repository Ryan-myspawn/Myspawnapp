**The question, from last night:** whether "Longevity Claw," reported on 18 September with a striking 233-clock figure, was a separate release from the open longevity AI toolkit in Insilico Medicine's *Cell* cover study of 17 September, which we had already scripted.

**The answer: they are the same thing.** Longevity Claw is the agentic research platform released as part of that *Cell* study. **We were right not to ship a second script on it.**

**What is genuinely new to our file, and it is specific:**
- The platform predicts biological age across **233 clocks spanning six modalities**, using **429,165 coefficients**.
- Collaborators reported include **Liquid AI**, the **Buck Institute for Research on Aging**, and **Harvard Medical School / Brigham and Women's Hospital**.
- **Epistemic status:** the *Cell* study is **peer-reviewed**. The claim circulating separately that a compact model **"beats every frontier system tested"** is a **result on the developers' own benchmark** and should be labelled that way every time.

**So what, and this is the useful part.** On 17 September we opened a precision-watch with the approved line: *a biological-age clock sold to you as a consumer product is an opinion; a panel of six independently developed clocks cross-checked inside a controlled trial is a measurement.* The 233 figure **sharpens that rather than contradicting it**. Six clocks agreeing inside a trial is a signal. **233 clocks existing at all is a statement about the field: "your biological age" is not a number, it is a distribution, and which number you are shown depends on who is showing it.**

**Rail unchanged and it applies to this item with force: MySpawn does not measure, affect or reverse biological age, and no asset may imply it.**
