## X DRAFTS

**1.** The first epigenetic reprogramming therapy cleared to enter human trials has four dates, and almost every retelling uses none of them properly.
FDA authorisation to proceed: **15 January 2026**.
Press release announcing it: **28 January 2026**.
First participant dosed: announced **9 June 2026**.
Trial: **NCT07290244**, Phase 1, open-angle glaucoma and NAION. Primary endpoint is **safety and tolerability**, because it is first-in-human.
"Cleared to try it in people" is not "it works." Both things can be exciting.
*(Company announcements and secondary coverage. FDA records and the registry entry not opened directly.)*

**2.** HIPAA does not say how long your doctor keeps your records.
It says six years, for HIPAA's own paperwork.
Your actual file is governed by state law. Florida: at least two years after the practice closes. Massachusetts: seven years from your last visit.
Both are "the law." They are just different laws.
Ask yours before you need something from 2009.

**3.** A useful habit for reading any science headline: ask which side of the gap it is standing on.
Possible is a laboratory.
Available is a waiting room, a price, an approval, a clinician who has heard of it, and you still being there.
Most headlines describe the first and are read as the second.

## THREADS POSTS

**1.** For about forty years a man could donate sperm, sign a form promising he would never be identified, and leave. That promise was the product.
It stopped being deliverable around 2005, when a fourteen-year-old used a consumer DNA test, some public records and a search engine to find his biological father in nine days.
Nothing was leaked. The donor had not tested. A relative of his had, and that was enough.
The promise was not broken. It was obsoleted. Those are different, and only one of them is anybody's fault.
Worth asking of anything you have been promised about your own biological material: is this assurance still deliverable with the tools that exist now?

**2.** Three copies of a document is a backup.
One person who knows where the three copies are is not a backup.
That is the part almost everybody gets right and then leaves inside their own head, where it is useless to the person who will need it.
Ninety seconds, out loud, to one other human being. It is the cheapest thing on the entire list and it is the one nobody does.
