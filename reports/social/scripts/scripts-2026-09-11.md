## SCRIPT 1 — "The Word for What We Don't Do" (~75s)

**Freshness check:** the boundary exists as an ad (116), an episode segment (Sep 10) and a long explainer (today's pack), but never as a short, and never opening on the critics' own vocabulary. Typing-and-deleting cold open is a new visual device for the channel.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55.**
**Trend-hook used:** honest and stated in-script — a term consolidating in the 2026 literature and commentary, not an event that broke today.

[SHOT 1: plain screen, close. The words DIGITAL NECROMANCY being typed, one keystroke at a time. Then the cursor deletes them, slowly.]
VO: "There's a name now for the thing people are afraid of. It's aimed at companies building chatbots of the dead. I run a preservation company, so let me take it seriously instead of pretending it's about somebody else."

[SHOT 2: to camera, counting on three fingers.]
VO: "Three arguments, and all three are basically right. One: the dead can't consent to a new conversation. Two: the data was theirs, and inheriting a hard drive isn't inheriting permission. Three: living with a simulation might stretch grief out instead of letting it move."
ON-SCREEN: "consent · ownership · grief"

[SHOT 3: hold up a phone playing a real recorded voice for two seconds, then set it down.]
VO: "Here's the line that actually matters, and it isn't about how good the technology gets. A recording can only ever repeat what someone really said."

[SHOT 4: beat. Flat.]
VO: "A model will eventually say something they never said. That's not a bug you can engineer out. That's what the thing is for."

[SHOT 5: to camera, plain.]
VO: "So, ours, out loud, where you can hold us to it. We store DNA records. We store the video and the voice notes a member actually recorded, sealed to a date they picked. We do not generate speech in anyone's voice. We do not build a model of a person. We do not sell a conversation with the dead."

[SHOT 6: half-smile, no push.]
VO: "That choice costs us a product line that would probably sell. Ninety-nine a year, storage only. And if we ever cross that line, the word for it is right there in the first shot."

SOUND NOTE: keystrokes are the only sound until SHOT 3; the recorded voice is the single audio event; no music anywhere.
**Platform cuts:** TikTok full 75s; Reels same; Shorts open on SHOT 3 (the recording-vs-model turn). 7-second teaser: the typing/deleting shot + "There's a name now for the thing people are afraid of."

**CAPTIONS:**
- TikTok v1: "There's a word for it now. Here's exactly where we stop."
- TikTok v2: "A recording repeats what they said. A model eventually says what they didn't."
- Reels v1: "Taking the 'digital necromancy' critique seriously, as someone with skin in the game."
- Reels v2: "Three criticisms of griefbots, and why all three are right."
- Shorts v1: "The line we don't cross, stated out loud."
- Shorts v2: "It costs us a product line. That's the point."
- Hashtags: #ethics #ai #grief #legacy (trim per platform)

**Footnotes:** the three critiques are drawn from the 2026 griefbot ethics literature across grief studies, phenomenology and memory/media journals (attributed to the literature generally, never to a misquoted individual). **RAILS (hard):** never mock people who use these products — the critique targets design and incentives; the "costs us a product line" line stays, because it is the only thing that makes the boundary credible; zero product beyond the storage-only statement.
