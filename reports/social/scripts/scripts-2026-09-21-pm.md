## DELTA SWEEP

### Item 1: UK Biobank has been shut to researchers for five months, and we never had it on file

**Dates first, because the honest version matters more than the dramatic one.** This is **not today's news.** The incident is from **April 2026**. What is in-window is the reopening.

**What is reported:** in **April 2026**, UK Biobank found that **deidentified participant-level data that had been exported from its Research Analysis Platform** was being offered for sale on Chinese e-commerce sites, reported as **Xianyu**, owned by Alibaba. The listing was removed with cooperation from Alibaba and the UK and Chinese governments. UK Biobank **paused researcher access to the platform**. Coverage dated **30 April 2026** puts the number of affected participants at **around 500,000**; **that figure is from security-press coverage, not from UK Biobank's own statement, and should be labeled that way on every use.**

**What is in-window, and it is the useful half:**
- The Research Analysis Platform has been **closed since April, close to five months**, with a **phased reopening from September 2026**.
- Applications for **new** projects are suspended and are **not expected to restart until late 2026**.
- **Every existing project is being extended by six months**, and so are compute credits.
- Reopening is **conditional on external experts assuring the platform is secure**, and access is limited to researchers and institutes **fully compliant** on mandatory training, annual reports, and **data deletion for projects that have ended**.
- **At reopening, researchers will not be able to download results at all.** A **manual Output Checking System** with limited capacity arrives later in 2026, and an **automated** one is targeted for **early 2027**.
- An **airlock** is being built and rolled out with the reopening.
- A **£200,000 security review** was commissioned, reported **13 July 2026**, and the recommendations have been accepted.

**Why this is the best thing the sweep has produced in a fortnight, and it is not the scare angle.** The interesting fact is not that a big database had an incident. It is **what they decided the fix was.** They did not buy a bigger lock on the front door. They built an **airlock and an output checker**: come in, compute, and **you cannot carry anything out until something checks what you are carrying.**

**That is the missing half of every digital-legacy question we have ever asked.** Every service answers *who can get in*: legacy contact, access key, death certificate, inactive-account timeout. **Almost none of them answers *what can leave, who approved it, and whether there is a record of it.*** We have spent a month teaching people to ask the first question. This gives us the second one, from an institution that learned it the expensive way.

**And the fairness note, which is also the more persuasive read:** UK Biobank disclosed, got the listing taken down, paused access rather than carrying on, commissioned an independent review, accepted its recommendations, extended every affected project by six months and published a staged reopening plan with dates. **That is a good institutional response and we say so.** A scare frame here would be both unfair and weaker.

**Our own file, corrected.** UK Biobank appears three times in the archive: a *Science Advances* diet-and-longevity cohort study (1 Sept), and twice as the **Greenheys building move** banked under the angle *samples outlive buildings, so ask every storage company what its moving plan is*. **We wrote about their new freezer and never knew their research platform had been dark since April.** Logged as a gap in the sweep, not as a result.
