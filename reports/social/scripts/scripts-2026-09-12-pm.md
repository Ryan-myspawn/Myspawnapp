[SHOT 1: a phone held up, a comment visible on screen. He reads it out loud, deadpan, no defensiveness.]
VO: "Best comment this week. 'Why would anyone in 2126 actually care about your DNA?' Genuine question, and I don't think the honest answer is the one you'd expect me to give."

[SHOT 2: he sets the phone down. To camera.]
VO: "Here's what I'm not going to say. I'm not going to tell you your descendants will be desperate to bring you back. They won't be. People in 2126 will be busy with 2126."

[SHOT 3: flat.]
VO: "And I'm not going to tell you the science will definitely arrive. I don't know that. Nobody selling you anything in this category knows that, and the ones who say they do are the reason the category has a credibility problem."

[SHOT 4: beat.]
VO: "So here's the actual answer. I'm not preserving a record because I think the future wants me. I'm preserving it because the cost of being wrong in one direction is ninety-nine dollars a year, and the cost of being wrong in the other direction is that the information doesn't exist and can't be recreated."

[SHOT 5: to camera, plain.]
VO: "That's it. That's the entire argument. It isn't a prophecy, it's an asymmetry. You already make this trade somewhere else, probably without calling it that."

[SHOT 6: half-smile.]
VO: "If that reasoning doesn't move you, genuinely don't buy it. I'd rather you didn't than have you buy it off a promise I can't keep. But if it does move you, the version of you that can still provide the sample is the one reading this."

*Rail:* names no company; punches at the effect size, never at parents.

**X2 — funding is not evidence**
> A thing I keep having to say out loud:
>
> NewLimit raised $435M in June. First human study expected 2027.
> Retro announced 50x better reprogramming efficiency in Aug 2025. That's throughput, not a clinical result.
> The vision-loss reprogramming therapy is in humans right now with no published outcome.
>
> All real. None of it is evidence that it works yet.
>
> Valuation is not evidence. I'd say that about my own company.

*Labels:* company/investor announcements, not clinical results; the human trial is in progress.
*Rail:* no disparagement — a trial without a result is a trial working normally.

**X3 — the archive failure mode**
> Archives almost never fail by decay.
>
> They fail because the one person who knew it existed died, moved, or just forgot to mention it.
>
> Whatever you've preserved — photos, documents, a safe, a record — go tell one human being out loud this week. Not in a document they'll find later. Out loud.
>
> That step is free and nobody sells it to you.

*Rail:* zero product. The instruction works whether or not anyone ever buys anything.

**T2 — the regulatory gap**
> California just wrote the first real rulebook for AI companions: crisis protocols, age checks, a parent gets told if a kid switches the safeguards off.
>
> Every one of those protections is for someone who is alive. That's the right place to start.
