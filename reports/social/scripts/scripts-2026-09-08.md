**Trend-hook used:** the 2026 over-optimization backlash / "joyful wellness" counter-positioning, logged in today's radar. Stated on screen as a trend, not as a news event.

[SHOT 1: bathroom counter, morning light. Hand sets down objects one at a time, fast, clacking: supplement bottles, a smart ring, a scale, a CGM box, a red-light panel, a sleep-tracker charger.]
VO (dry, unbothered): "The longevity industry has sold you a part-time job. Let's total up the hours."

[SHOT 2: hand taps each item, counting off.]
VO: "Sleep score you check before you're fully awake. Ring you charge. Rings you close. Supplements, morning stack and evening stack. Blood panel every quarter. Zone two, four times a week. A cold plunge you talk about more than you use."
ON-SCREEN: running tally ticks up: "~6 hrs/week" ... "~310 hrs/year"

[SHOT 3: to camera, no judgment in the delivery.]
VO: "I'm not here to tell you any of that is wrong. Some of it clearly works, and the guys doing it are usually happier and stronger than the guys who aren't. Do it. That's not the point I'm making."

[SHOT 4: sweep the whole counter, camera follows the objects.]
VO: "The point is that every single item on this counter optimizes a body that ends. Not one of them is inheritable. Your VO2 max doesn't pass down. Your sleep score doesn't pass down. Three hundred hours a year, and the entire portfolio zeroes out on one date."
ON-SCREEN: "310 hrs/yr · 0 inheritable"

[SHOT 5: one object placed alone in the cleared space: the kit tube.]
VO: "This is the anti-protocol. No daily habit, no dashboard, no streak to keep. One decision a year, ninety-nine dollars, and a record sits in an accredited vault doing nothing. Nothing is the feature. It's the only thing on this counter that still exists when the body stops."

[SHOT 6: back to camera, half-smile.]
VO: "Keep your protocol. Just notice which item on your counter your grandkids could actually use. How many tracking apps are on your phone right now? Just the number."

[SHOT 1: handheld, walking into a hardware-store aisle. Wall of safes and lockboxes fills frame.]
VO: "Aisle seven. Every single thing on this wall exists to protect something replaceable."

[SHOT 2: walking the aisle, camera panning items as named.]
VO: "Fireproof document safe. Waterproof bin. Cash box. Gun safe. Key lockbox with the four-digit code you'll forget."

[SHOT 3: stop, pick up one small safe, turn it over.]
VO: "Think about what actually goes in these. The deed: the county has the original. The passport: the State Department can reissue it. Birth certificate, insurance policy, title, will — every one of them has an authority somewhere that can print another."

[SHOT 4: set it back. Walk to the end of the aisle. Frame a plain wall or empty endcap.]
VO: "So here's the thing I can't unsee. There's a whole aisle for things that can be reissued, and no shelf anywhere in this store for the one thing that can't. There's no reissue process for a person."

[SHOT 5: to camera, wry, still in the aisle.]
VO: "And it turns out the answer was never a better box. The safe doesn't help if nobody opens it, and a fireproof rating doesn't cover fifty years of someone forgetting it exists. What you actually need isn't a container. It's an institution that's still holding the thing in year thirty."

[SHOT 6: walking out, over the shoulder.]
VO: "Ninety-nine a year, accredited vault, storage only. Cheaper than most of aisle seven. Comment the most useless thing you're currently protecting."
