## SCRIPT 1 — "It Won't Open Today" (~70s)
*Radar Direction 2, carried from yesterday and still the strongest unbuilt Memory Vault angle.*

**Freshness check:** Every Vault asset the fleet has made is about **sealing** a video. Nothing has
ever covered the day one **opens**, and nobody has explained the fixed date as a *design decision*.
Device is new: a physical combination lock set to a date. The 7-day format run is document-read,
static long-take, audio-led voicemail, retail-aisle walk, tape-measure demo, deadpan unboxing,
glovebox monologue, typing screen, printed page, two-chair staging, zero-prop outdoor walk, and
today's mirror shot and index cards — a combination lock is none of them.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55** (38-year-old father lead; carries the 40-60 lane without sentimentality).
**Trend-hook used:** none — evergreen mechanism explainer, declared. Radar found no fresh hook and
I am not inventing one.

[SHOT 1: close on hands holding a four-dial combination padlock. He spins it to 2 – 0 – 3 – 3. Pulls. It does not open.]
VO: "This isn't how our vault works. But it's exactly what it's for, so let me explain the part nobody asks about."

[SHOT 2: he sets the lock down on a plain table. To camera, flat.]
VO: "Everyone asks what to record. Nobody asks what happens on the day it opens. That's the actual design problem, and we got it wrong twice before we got it right."

[SHOT 3: three fingers.]
VO: "Option one: it unlocks when someone dies. We threw that out — it turns a birthday present into an event nobody wants to trigger, and it makes the company decide when your family is ready. Option two: it unlocks whenever the recipient asks. Threw that out too. A thing you can open any time is a folder, not a vault. You'd watch it once, at 2am, in the wrong mood."

[SHOT 4: he picks the lock back up.]
VO: "Option three is the boring one, and it's the one that works. You pick a date. A birthday. A graduation. A Tuesday in 2041 for no reason you have to justify. And then nobody — not you, not them, not us — can move it."

[SHOT 5: to camera, plain.]
VO: "That constraint is the product. It means the video arrives on a day that already means something, instead of on the worst day of someone's life. It means you can record it badly and not be tempted to re-record it forty times, because you can't go back in. And it means the thing they get is a message from a man at a specific age, not a summary of a whole life."

[SHOT 6: beat.]
VO: "I should say plainly: nobody has opened one of ours yet. The company isn't old enough. So I'm not going to describe a scene I've never seen. I'm telling you why the lock has a date on it."

[SHOT 7: he sets the lock down, doesn't touch it again.]
VO: "Record the video. Pick the date. Then leave it alone. Ninety-nine a year, and the leaving-it-alone is free."

SOUND NOTE: the dial clicks are the only sound design — let all four clicks land in SHOT 1. Room tone otherwise. No music.
**Platform cuts:** TikTok full 70s; Reels same; Shorts open on SHOT 1 (the failed pull is the hook). 7-second teaser: SHOT 1 clicks + failed pull + "It won't open today. That's the point."

**CAPTIONS:**
- TikTok v1: "Why the date can't be changed — not even by us."
- TikTok v2: "We got the unlock day wrong twice before we got it right."
- Reels v1: "Everyone asks what to record. Nobody asks what happens when it opens."
- Reels v2: "A thing you can open any time is a folder, not a vault."
- Shorts v1: "It won't open today. That's the design."
- Shorts v2: "Pick a date. Then nobody can move it — including us."
- Hashtags: #memoryvault #legacy #timecapsule #dads (trim per platform)

**Footnotes / RAILS (hard):** (a) **no invented unlock scene** — the product is too young for anyone to have witnessed one, and SHOT 6 says so out loud on purpose; that line is non-negotiable and cannot be cut for length; (b) VIDEO-message framing only, no letter/diary framing per the binding rules; (c) no death-trigger language beyond naming it as the option we rejected; (d) no sentimentality beat, no "dear child" address; (e) if the live product does not actually make the date immovable by the company, **fix the product or cut SHOT 4** — do not ship the claim.

## SCRIPT 2 — "Two and a Half Points" (~60s)
*Radar Direction 1, narrowed to a single number. Format-distinct cascade with today's episode.*

**Freshness check:** The episode written at 15:07 today makes this argument at length; this short takes

that was a flat-lay of objects arguing *effort*; this is a weighing action arguing *effect size*.
Distinct from Ad_NotAPrediction_112 (Sep 9), which stated a ceiling without a comparison.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55** (29-year-old optimizer lead).
**Trend-hook used:** the convergence logged in today's radar — embryo-screening firms marketing into
the male self-optimisation circuit — read against the peer-reviewed effect size.

[SHOT 1: kitchen scale, zeroed. A supplement tub beside it. He scoops, weighs, reads the label. No greeting.]
VO: "You've done this. You know what a scoop weighs and you know why. You've also thrown a product out because the only study behind it was in rats."

[SHOT 2: he taps the scale off. Looks up.]
VO: "So here's a number for you, from a peer-reviewed paper in Cell. If you screen a batch of five embryos and pick the top-scoring one, the expected gain is about two and a half IQ points."

[SHOT 3: flat. No graphic, no emphasis.]
VO: "Two and a half. Best of five. And if you could somehow choose from fifty — which nobody can — it goes up to about four and a half."

[SHOT 4: he puts the lid on the tub.]
VO: "Same paper checked it against real families. In families with ten or more kids, the one with the highest score usually wasn't the tallest one."

[SHOT 5: to camera.]
VO: "I'm not telling you what to do about your embryos. I'm telling you that if a supplement company showed you that effect size, you would close the tab without finishing the page. Use the standard you already have."

[SHOT 6: beat, half-smile.]
VO: "For the record, I sell none of this. We store a DNA record, ninety-nine a year. We don't rank anything, and I'd tell you if two and a half points was our number too."

SOUND NOTE: the scale's beep and the tub lid are the only accents. No music. Keep the read of the label inaudible — we are not naming a supplement brand.
**Platform cuts:** TikTok full 60s; Reels same; Shorts open on SHOT 2 (straight to the number). 7-second teaser: SHOT 2 + SHOT 3 — the number and "best of five."

**Footnotes:** Karavani et al., *Cell*, 2019, "Screening Human Embryos for Polygenic Traits Has Limited Utility" — https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(19)31210-3 (peer-reviewed): ~2.5 IQ points / ~2.5 cm selecting top of five, ~4.5 at fifty, and the 28-large-family real-world check.
