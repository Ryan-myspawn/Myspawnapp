## PODCAST SEGMENT ONE-PAGER (Reaction Fuel)
**Item:** the **spermmaxxing** trend. **This is the long version and it deliberately carries material Script 1 does not**, so the two are not the same asset at different lengths.

**Cold open line:**
> "I want to defend the impulse behind spermmaxxing for about ninety seconds, and then explain why two of the things it promises are arguing with each other."

**The claim being reacted to, stated honestly:** a named optimization trend promising to raise sperm count, motility and testosterone. **No individual is quoted and none should be.** **Epistemic status: trend reporting, undated in our sweep. Say "a trend called spermmaxxing", never "this week's trend".**

**Talking points:**
1. **Concede properly.** Male factor is present in a large share of infertility cases, commonly cited at **40 to 50%**, and men are frequently not tested first. **Verify the figure before airing.**
2. **The conflict.** **Exogenous testosterone suppresses spermatogenesis.** A protocol raising one can lower the other.
3. **The two one-percents.** Individual aging versus the **age-matched cohort decline of roughly 1.2%/yr** (Travison, *JCEM* 2007, MMAS, three waves, surviving controls). **Peer-reviewed.**
4. **The pattern, not the trend.** This is the same shape as viral claims touting **GLP-1 drugs as fertility boosters**, covered by the *Genetic Literacy Project* on **21 July 2026**. **Two months old, so a receipt rather than a hook.** **Verify before citing.**
5. **What actually answers the question.** A semen analysis, read by a physician. Cheap, specific, about you.
6. **The rail, said out loud:** MySpawn stores a genetic record. **It is not a fertility product, it does not touch sperm quality or testosterone, and anyone in our category implying otherwise should be ignored.**

**Clip-able 30-second close:**
> "The thing I do not want to do is laugh at men for caring about this, because the concern is real and the medical system is not exactly rushing to test them. What I will say is that a protocol promising to raise your testosterone and your sperm count in the same sentence is not a shortcut, it is a contradiction. The test is twenty minutes and a doctor reads it. Start there."

**Shorts pull-quotes:** "Two of the three things it promises pull against each other." · "A protocol is not a measurement." · "I am not going to laugh at men for caring about this."
