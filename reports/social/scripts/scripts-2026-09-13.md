## SCRIPT 1 — "The Number That Went Backwards" (~55s)
*Radar concept, executed as specified. **ZERO PRODUCT — this is a hard constraint, see rails.***

**Freshness check:** Never made as a short. The material entered the fleet yesterday (blog +
longpost #63) and today (episode). Distinct from the Aug 30 family-photo name-quiz, which tests what
you *remember*, and from the Sep 6 estate-checklist desk audit, which audits a *document*. This is
about a behaviour that measurably declined. Device — a phone contacts list scrolled to a name, thumb
hovering, not tapping — is unused: the 7-day run is document-read, static long-take, audio-led
voicemail, retail-aisle walk, tape-measure, deadpan unboxing, two-chair, zero-prop outdoor walk,
mirror, combination lock, kitchen scale, comment-reply and cloud-drive screen record.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55** (38-year-old father lead; strongest 40-60 crossover this month).
**Trend-hook used:** honest and stated — not an event, a dated statistic pair new to us this week.

[SHOT 1: phone screen, close. A contacts list scrolling. It slows. Stops on a name — "Dad", or "Uncle Ray". The thumb hovers. It does not tap.]
VO: "In 2004, about thirty percent of Americans had written down their family's health history. In 2024 it was fifteen."

[SHOT 2: still on the hovering thumb.]
VO: "It halved. Across the exact twenty years when spitting into a tube became a Christmas present."

[SHOT 3: he sets the phone face-up on the table and looks at camera.]
VO: "I don't think people stopped caring. Ninety-five percent still say it matters. I think the test felt like it replaced the conversation."

[SHOT 4: flat.]
VO: "It didn't. Your dashboard doesn't know your father was fifty-two when they found it. And that number is the one that changes what a doctor does about you."

[SHOT 5: to camera, direct.]
VO: "If a first-degree relative had colon cancer before sixty, guidelines move your own first screening to forty. Or ten years before their diagnosis, whichever is sooner. That's a fifteen-year head start, triggered by a fact you either know or you don't."

[SHOT 6: he picks the phone back up.]
VO: "Not the diagnosis. The age. Write down the ages."

[SHOT 7: the thumb taps the name. Ringing starts. Cut before anyone answers.]
VO: "Costs nothing. I'm selling nothing here."

SOUND NOTE: room tone, the small tick of a thumb on glass, and the ring tone at the very end. **No music.** Cut on the second ring — do not let anyone pick up.
**Platform cuts:** TikTok full 55s; Reels same; Shorts open on SHOT 4 (the dashboard line). 7-second teaser: SHOT 1 + "In 2004 it was thirty percent. In 2024 it was fifteen."

**CAPTIONS:**
- TikTok v1: "A health habit that went backwards while the technology got better."
- TikTok v2: "Not the diagnosis. The age. Write down the ages."
- Reels v1: "In 2004, 30% of Americans did this. In 2024, 15%."
- Reels v2: "Your DNA dashboard doesn't know how old your father was."
- Shorts v1: "The free thing the expensive thing didn't replace."
- Shorts v2: "Fifteen-year head start, triggered by one fact."
- Hashtags: #familyhistory #health #dads #screening (trim per platform)

**Footnotes:** 2004 CDC HealthStyles Survey — 96.3% called family health history important, 29.8% had collected one. Survey run Jan-Feb 2024 — 94.8% important, **15.2%** collected. Both as reported in the family-medicine literature on barriers to collection: https://www.jabfm.org/content/39/1/157915 **(via search index — confirm both figures against the primary article before filming; if the 2024 number does not hold, re-cut on the 2004 figure alone rather than fudge it).** Colorectal screening guidance: American College of Gastroenterology, summarised https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2022/0300/p327.html
**RAILS (hard):** (a) **ZERO PRODUCT. No MySpawn mention, no kit, no price, no end card.** Attaching the product to a free public-health message converts it into an ad and burns the trust it earns — the radar flagged this and it is not negotiable in the edit; (b) not medical advice, and the screening guidance is paraphrased from professional-society guidelines, not quoted; (c) no mortality-scare beat and no sentimentality — the register is practical throughout; (d) do not stage a fake call; if he films the call, the person on the other end consents or the shot ends before they answer.

## SCRIPT 2 — "I Set a Timer and Tried to Find the Answer" (~75s)
*From today's Competitor Watch finding. The Daily Review asked for this as a script, not just an ad.*

**Freshness check:** New. The custody-questions lane exists (Ad_FourQuestions_99, Sep 7;
Ad_TransferOfOwnership_108, Sep 9) but every prior asset tells you *what to ask*. Nothing has ever
measured **how hard the answer is to get**, which is a different and more falsifiable claim. Device —
a visible running stopwatch as the spine of the video — is new; lineage noted to the Aug 29
timer-challenge kit demo, 15 days out and a different subject (that timed a collection, this times a
search).
**Primary audience: Men 25-55** (50-year-old estate planner lead; optimizer secondary).
**Trend-hook used:** none — this is a method, declared. It is prompted by today's competitor check,
which found sample-retention terms across the adjacent category resolve only into terms-of-service
documents rather than any public page.

[SHOT 1: desk. He starts a physical stopwatch and sets it down, running, in frame. It stays visible for the whole video.]
VO: "One question, three companies that hold biological samples. How long do you keep it, and what happens to it if you shut down? I'm timing how long it takes to find the answer on each website."

[SHOT 2: over-shoulder, laptop. He searches. Stopwatch visible.]
VO: "Rules: only what a normal person would do. Homepage, FAQ, search the site. I'm not emailing anyone and I'm not opening a legal document unless there's nothing else."

[SHOT 3: cut. Stopwatch showing elapsed time. He reports the first result out loud, no company named.]
VO: "[REPORT ACTUAL RESULT — where it was found, or that it was only in the terms, or that it wasn't found at all.]"

[SHOT 4: same for the second and third.]
VO: "[REPORT ACTUAL RESULTS.]"

[SHOT 5: he stops the watch and turns it to camera.]
VO: "And then I did ours, with the same rules, and I'm reporting that number whatever it is. If ours is the worst one here, that's the video."

[SHOT 6: to camera, plain.]
VO: "Here's why this is the test that matters. Everyone in this industry will tell you to ask the custody questions. Nobody grades themselves on whether the answer is actually reachable. A policy you can only find inside a terms-of-service document is a policy written for a court, not for you."

[SHOT 7: beat.]
VO: "Go do this with something you already pay for. Set a timer. Post the number."

SOUND NOTE: stopwatch tick under everything, trackpad and typing. **No music.** The tick is the tension; do not replace it with a score.
**Platform cuts:** TikTok full 75s; Reels same; Shorts open on SHOT 1 (starting the watch). 7-second teaser: SHOT 1 + "One question, three companies. I'm timing it."

**CAPTIONS:**
- TikTok v1: "I timed how long it takes to find out what happens to your sample."
- TikTok v2: "If the answer is only in the terms, it wasn't written for you."
- Reels v1: "Everyone says ask the custody question. Nobody grades the answer's findability."
- Reels v2: "I ran the test on my own company too, and I'm reporting that number whatever it is."
- Shorts v1: "One question. Three companies. A stopwatch."
- Shorts v2: "Set a timer on something you already pay for."
- Hashtags: #consumer #trust #custody #duediligence (trim per platform)

**Footnotes / RAILS (hard):** (a) **The results in SHOTS 3-5 are placeholders and must be filled with what actually happens on camera** — this script cannot be pre-written to a conclusion, and if the real results are boring or favourable to everyone, that is the video and it ships that way; (b) **name no company** — describe them by category ("a biological-sample service", "a testing company") and show no logos or identifiable page chrome; (c) **do not characterise any competitor's policy as bad** — report only how long it took to find and where it lived, which is a fact about findability, not a claim about their conduct; (d) **our own result is mandatory and reported honestly**; if MySpawn's answer turns out to be hard to find, the fix is the website before the video ships, and the video says the old number and the new one; (e) no price claim, no kit.
