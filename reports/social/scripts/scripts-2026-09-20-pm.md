> Open the terms of any service holding your photos, recordings or files.
>
> Search for the word "assign."
>
> Most let your account transfer to whoever acquires the company. Then try "merger." Then "bankruptcy."

## THREADS POSTS (2)

**T-1:**
> A correction, and it is the more interesting half of the story.
>
> I said four companies built to keep people's memories had shut down, pivoted or paused in about two years. Three of them had. The fourth filed for Chapter 11 in May 2024 with roughly $1.5m of assets against $10.5m of liabilities, an investor bought what was left, and today it is operating and expanding.
>
> So the range of outcomes is not one thing, it is two. And I am not sure the second one is the comfortable one.
>
> When a company like that survives, your material survives as an asset somebody purchased. Nobody signed up with an investment firm they had never heard of. They signed up with a small team who wanted to keep their father's voice. The obligation transferred. The relationship did not.
>
> Which is why the question I have started asking, of everyone including us, is not "what happens if you shut down." It is "who is allowed to buy you."

**T-2:**
> Ten seconds, and it works on every service you use.
>
> Open its terms. Search for the word "assign."
>
> You will usually find a clause saying the agreement can transfer if the company is acquired, merged or sold. That single sentence is doing more work than the entire privacy policy above it, because it decides who ends up holding your things.
>
> A closure is visible. Somebody emails you, the site goes down, you notice. A sale is not. The logo changes and the terms are "updated."
>
> I am not telling you to panic about it. I am telling you to know it is there before you hand anything to anybody, including us.
