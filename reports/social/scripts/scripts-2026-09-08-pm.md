## NEWSJACK SCRIPT — "They Found 1,260 Genes for Your Personality. You Are Still Not a Prediction." (~85s)

**Freshness check:** personality genetics has never appeared in any format fleet-wide (scripts, longposts, ads, production log all grepped). Distinct from the embryo-IQ takedown (longpost #15, Aug 23 — that was a product critique) and from the KCL aging-trajectories episode (Sep 4 — different study, different claim). Format: two-column desk sort with index cards, not used in the last 7 days (recent: phone screen, tape measure, desk letter-cards, counter sweep, aisle walk).
**Primary audience: Men 25-55** (optimizer lead; this is the week's most quotable science).
**Trend-hook used:** *Nature*, published this week (coverage Sept 7, 2026), peer-reviewed.

[SHOT 1: desk. A thick stack of blank index cards. To camera, brisk.]
VO: "A study came out this week that is going to get misread for the next ten years, so let's get ahead of it. Scientists found one thousand two hundred and sixty genetic variants linked to personality. The big five: how outgoing you are, how anxious, how agreeable, how open, how conscientious."
ON-SCREEN: "Nature · 2026 · peer-reviewed"

[SHOT 2: deal cards into a pile, fast, counting.]
VO: "The scale is the story. Forty-six cohorts. Between six hundred thousand and one point one million people, depending on the trait. Eight hundred and twenty-four of those variants had never been found before. This is not a small study, and it is not a preprint."
ON-SCREEN: "1,260 variants · 824 new · 46 cohorts"

[SHOT 3: hold up one single card between two fingers.]
VO: "Now the part that decides whether you understand this study or get fooled by it. Twelve hundred and sixty variants sounds like a blueprint. It is the opposite. You need that many precisely because each one does almost nothing. One card. That's the effect size of a single variant on whether you're the guy who talks at parties."

[SHOT 4: sweep the whole stack into a loose pile, deliberately messy.]
VO: "And even all of them together explain a modest slice of the difference between people. The rest is everything else: what happened to you, who raised you, what you practiced, what you chose. Anyone who tells you this study means personality is genetic is selling something. Anyone who tells you it means genes don't matter is also selling something."

[SHOT 5: to camera, flat and direct.]
VO: "Here's what I actually take from it, as a guy who runs a DNA storage company and therefore has every incentive to overhype this. Nothing in this paper predicts a person. Nothing in it should make you screen an embryo or make a decision about a kid. What it shows is that the map of what's readable in a genome got a lot bigger this week, and it will get bigger again next year."

[SHOT 6: pick one card back out of the pile, set it aside cleanly.]
VO: "Which is the only reason we exist. We don't read anything. We keep the record intact so it can be read by better tools later, ninety-nine dollars a year, storage only. This study didn't change what we sell. It changed how much there is to eventually look at."
ON-SCREEN: "myspawn.me · $99/yr · storage only"

[SHOT 7: tap the pile.]
VO: "Twelve hundred and sixty variants and you are still not a prediction. Comment if you've ever been told you got a trait 'from your father.'"
