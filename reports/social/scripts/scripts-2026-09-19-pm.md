## THREADS POSTS (2)

**T-1:**
> Something uncomfortable I worked out this week, about my own industry.
>
> Four of the best-known companies built to keep people's memories have shut down, pivoted or paused in roughly two years. None of them did anything wrong. No scandal, no breach, nobody ran off with anything.
>
> The structure is the problem. The money arrives once. The obligation lasts forever. A small team selling a promise measured in decades is standing in that gap by definition.
>
> I am also standing in it. We store genetic records and we are a small team. So the fair thing is not to point at the companies that closed. It is to say that the question you should ask them is the question you should ask us, and that our written answer does not exist yet.

**T-2:**
> Practical, and it is against my own category's interest.
>
> If you use any service that holds recordings, photos or letters for your family, export everything this weekend. Not when you get a notice. The notice is the end of the process, not the start.
>
> Before you buy the next one, find the export button. If the answer is "contact support," that is your answer.
>
> And check the app's last-updated date. It is public, it takes ten seconds, and it is the most honest health metric a small company has.
