## X DRAFTS

**1.** The 27% estrangement figure you have seen quoted is not what you think it is.
It is the share of US adults who have cut off contact with **any** relative.
The parent-or-child figure is about 10%.
Still roughly 25 million people. The real number was always big enough.
*(Pillemer, Fault Lines, 2020 national survey. Six years old; no newer large-scale US prevalence measure found.)*

**2.** Die without a will and a statute divides your estate by category: spouse, children, parents, siblings.
There is no field for how the relationship actually went.
Leaving somebody out generally means naming them, on purpose, in the document.
Silence is not an instruction. It is a default.

**3.** A copyright year at the foot of a page is not a publication date. It renders when you load the page.
Tonight I watched it turn up in a **search result title**: "© 2026" on two stories whose URLs both say `/2024-10-03/`.
Byline, dateline, slug. In that order. Nothing else is a date.

## THREADS POSTS

**1.** Three copies of a document is a backup.
One person who knows where the three copies are is not a backup.
That is the part almost everybody gets right and then leaves in their own head, where it is useless to anybody who needs it.
Say the locations out loud to one other person this week. It takes ninety seconds and it is the only step in any of this that costs nothing.

**2.** A state is formally asking whether somebody you have not spoken to in nine years should still inherit from you.
Louisiana directed its State Law Institute to study it, with findings due no later than February 2027.
That is a study, not a change in the law, and nobody should plan around it.
Worth knowing anyway: the rules here move one state at a time, and the thing that protects you in the meantime is a document with names in it.
