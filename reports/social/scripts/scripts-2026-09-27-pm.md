## TWO THREADS POSTS

**1.**
> I got asked this week why we charge annually instead of just taking one payment and being done with it, which would obviously be easier to sell.
>
> Here is the honest answer, and it is not flattering.
>
> An annual fee means you get a chance to leave every year. That is a real weakness. People forget, cards expire, life happens, and a thing that needs renewing is a thing that can lapse.
>
> The alternative is worse in a quieter way. If you pay once for thirty years of storage, you have handed money to an institution for a service it delivers over three decades. If it does not reach year thirty, you are a creditor with a very strange claim. And nobody ever mentions year thirty-one.
>
> Both are real answers to the same problem. They just put the risk in different places, and almost nobody selling either will tell you where it went.
>
> Which one would you rather be carrying?

**2.**
> A box sits somewhere for forty years. Here is what happens around it.
>
> Three house moves. A loft, then a garage, then a different loft. Two broadband providers. Four phones. A password manager you stopped paying for. Somewhere in there, statistically, a household gets divided by people who are not enjoying the process.
>
> And at the end somebody who is not you stands in a room and decides what the box is.
>
> Nothing in that list is a disaster. Nobody did anything wrong. It is just what four decades look like from the point of view of an object that cannot move itself.
>
> Which is the actual difference between keeping something somewhere safe and keeping it somewhere institutional. One of those depends on your attention. The other does not have to.
