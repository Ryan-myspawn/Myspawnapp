## SCRIPT 1 — "Two Dishes" (~85s)

**Freshness check:** the iPSC-vs-testicular-stem-cell distinction has never been in any short; adjacent explainers (polygenic Aug 31, meiosis wall Sep 2) cover different mechanisms. Two-prop comparison format last used Sep 2 ("The Two Passports") — 8 days, outside the window, and the props and argument are entirely different.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55** (IVG-for-men is a standing preferred rotation direction).
**Trend-hook used:** a company announcement from May 13, 2026 that most people missed, and the vocabulary problem it exposes.

[SHOT 1: two empty petri dishes on a desk, masking-tape labels: "SKIN CELL" and "TESTICLE". To camera, brisk.]
VO: "Two labs. Two completely different starting points. The press calls both of them the same thing, and that word is about to be used to sell you something."

[SHOT 2: hand taps the SKIN CELL dish.]
VO: "Dish one is what IVG actually means. Take an ordinary cell, skin, blood, whatever, reprogram it backwards into a stem cell, then push it forward into an egg or a sperm. Status, unchanged: researchers have made primordial germ cell-like cells from human iPSCs. Nobody has finished the job. No lab has produced a mature, functional human gamete from an iPSC."
ON-SCREEN: "iPSC → gamete: not done"

[SHOT 3: hand taps the TESTICLE dish.]
VO: "Dish two is a different problem entirely. Some men make no mature sperm but still have the stem cells that are supposed to. Nobody is reprogramming anything here. You're trying to finish, in a lab, a maturation the body didn't finish."
ON-SCREEN: "existing stem cells → mature sperm"

[SHOT 4: to camera, flat, holding a printed press release.]
VO: "In May, a company said it did exactly that. Functional human sperm grown outside the body from a testicular biopsy, used to make early test embryos as a safety check. Here's the weight to put on it: it's a company announcement. Not peer reviewed. Not independently replicated. Not cleared for clinical use. That's not an accusation, that's the actual status, and the reporting said so too."
ON-SCREEN: "company announcement · unpublished · unreplicated"

[SHOT 5: slide the two dishes apart on the desk.]
VO: "Why be this pedantic? Because dish one, if it ever works, decouples reproduction from having usable gametes at all. Dish two is a treatment for a specific medical condition. Same headline word. Wildly different futures."

[SHOT 6: to camera.]
VO: "So when someone tells you IVG is here, you now have two questions. From what starting cell. And published where."

[SHOT 7: push both dishes out of frame.]
VO: "And to be clear, none of this is our business. We store DNA records, ninety-nine a year, storage only. No gametes, no reproductive services, nothing in this video is a reason to buy anything from us. Comment the next term you keep hearing and can't define."

SOUND NOTE: glass-on-desk taps are the beats; no music until SHOT 5.
**Platform cuts:** TikTok full 85s; Reels same; Shorts open on SHOT 4 (the epistemic-status beat). 7-second teaser: SHOT 1 + "Two dishes. One word. Completely different futures."

**CAPTIONS:**
- TikTok v1: "IVG doesn't mean what the headlines think it means. Two dishes, explained."
- TikTok v2: "From what starting cell, and published where. Two questions that survive any biotech headline."
- Reels v1: "No lab has made a mature human gamete from an iPSC. One company claims something different."
- Reels v2: "Company announcement, unpublished, unreplicated. That's the honest status."
- Shorts v1: "The distinction the coverage keeps collapsing."
- Shorts v2: "Same word. Two completely different technologies."
- Hashtags: #IVG #fertility #biotech #science #genetics (trim per platform)

**Footnotes:** Paterna Biosciences announcement, May 13, 2026 (BioSpace/PRNewswire release; coverage: Gizmodo, Newsweek — all reporting the claim, none verifying). IVG status per 2026 field reviews: human PGC-like cells from iPSCs yes, mature functional gametes no. **RAILS (hard):** the "company announcement / unpublished / unreplicated / not cleared" line may not be cut for time; the company is never mocked; zero product beyond the storage-only disclaimer; do NOT imply MySpawn has any role in fertility.
