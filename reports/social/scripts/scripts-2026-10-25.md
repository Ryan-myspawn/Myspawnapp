# SCRIPT 1: "Ten Thousand British People"

**Length: 46 seconds.** **One person, one table, one prop. Shootable alone.**
**FORMAT NOVELTY: the only graphic in the piece is a plain outline map with a single dot on it, and the dot is in the wrong place for the claim being made.** **`outline map`, `pin on a map` and `wrong country` all return ZERO across `reports/social/scripts/` and `reports/ads/`.**
**Structurally distinct from all seven preceding script days: not three chairs, not reverse playback, not a headline edited live, not black screen with type, not beans, not a thermometer, not a read-aloud page, not an elevator, not ice.** **The argument IS the graphic: a dot that is in the wrong country does the work that a paragraph would otherwise have to do.**

### Script

**[SHOT 1 - presenter mid-frame, plain background. A printed outline map of the United States lies flat on the table in front of them, unmarked. Hold two seconds]**
VO: "You have seen the statistic. One in ten of Gen Z had a child by twenty-three. One in four of the generation before them."
ON SCREEN: TEN THOUSAND BRITISH PEOPLE

**[SHOT 2 - presenter picks up a marker, hovers over the US map]**
VO: "It gets quoted at American men constantly. As a fact about here."

**[SHOT 3 - presenter sets the marker down WITHOUT marking the map, slides the US map aside, and puts a second printed outline map in its place: the United Kingdom. Marks a single dot]**
VO: "It was measured on almost ten thousand people born in the United Kingdom between 2000 and 2002, and followed ever since."
ON SCREEN CARD, held through shots 3 to 5: UK BIRTH COHORT. NOT A US STATISTIC. PUBLISHED FEB 2026.

**[SHOT 4 - static on the UK map with its one dot]**
VO: "And here is the uncomfortable part. That is a better kind of study than most of what you read. Same people, followed from birth. Not a poll."

**[SHOT 5 - presenter leans back, hands off the table]**
VO: "Which is exactly why it fooled people. A good method makes you forget to ask who was in it."

**[SHOT 6 - static, no movement]**
VO: "The same study found something almost nobody quotes. More than two thirds of them are still living with their parents at twenty-three. About three times the rate a decade earlier."

**[SHOT 7 - presenter, direct to camera, flat]**
VO: "That is not a story about not wanting children. The same study says most of them still plan to have them. It is a story about when adulthood starts."

**[SHOT 8 - cut hard on the last syllable. The last frame is the UK map with one dot, the US map still visible at the edge of the table, blank]**
VO: "Before you feel anything about a number, find out who got counted."

**Sound:** no music. **One marker-cap click on the single dot in shot 3 and nothing else.** Room tone under the voice. Cut dead on the final syllable.

**BINDING ON THE SHOOT AND THE EDIT.**
- **THE CARD IS NOT OPTIONAL AND CANNOT BE SHORTENED.** The radar's first binding flag on this concept was that the script may never let the figure read as American. **If the card goes, the piece goes.**
- **THE US MAP IS NEVER MARKED.** That is the whole visual argument: a blank American map next to a British one with a dot on it. **If a presenter marks the US map "for symmetry", the take is unusable.**
- **NO MOCKERY OF ANYONE, FOR LIVING WITH PARENTS OR FOR NOT HAVING CHILDREN.** The radar's second binding flag. **The target is the retelling, never the people counted.** Shot 7 exists to make that explicit and may not be cut for time.
- **NO PRODUCT CARD, NO PRICE, NO URL, NO LOGO END-PLATE.** The radar marked brand fit LOW deliberately. **The value of this piece is that it demonstrates the method; a product card would convert it into the thing it is criticising.**
- **Never state the millennial comparison as a precise percentage.** Our own file records one secondary source giving 24 percent by age 25 where UCL's pages say one in four by 23. **Say "one in four", never a percentage, and never attach an age to the millennial figure.**
