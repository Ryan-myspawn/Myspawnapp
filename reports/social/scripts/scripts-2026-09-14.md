*From today's blog. Never scripted, and it implicates our own marketing.*

**Freshness check:** New fleet-wide. The custody lane asks *what to ask* (Ad_FourQuestions_99, Sep 7)

Device is new: a rubber stamp. *(Noted adjacency: "stamp" appears as a graphic motif in three
pre-photo-mandate illustrated ads from Aug 20-25; this is a physical prop in a script, a different
thing, and those creatives are retired-style.)*
**Primary audience: Men 25-55** (50-year-old estate planner lead; optimizer secondary).
**Trend-hook used:** none — evergreen consumer-protection explainer, declared. Cascades with today's
blog article.

[SHOT 1: a blank sheet of paper. A rubber stamp comes down on it: **REGISTERED**. He lifts it, looks at the page.]
VO: "Three companies I looked at this week describe themselves as FDA-registered. So do we. Here's what that phrase actually means."

[SHOT 2: he holds the stamped page up.]
VO: "It means a facility told the FDA it exists."

[SHOT 3: beat. He sets it down.]
VO: "That's it. That's the whole thing. The FDA's own position is that a registration number does not convey approval or endorsement of the facility or its products. The agency doesn't approve facilities at all. Registration is for establishments. Approval is for products. Two different words doing two very different jobs."

[SHOT 4: to camera, flat.]
VO: "It's not nothing. It means they're on a list and they're inspectable. But nobody at the FDA has looked at your sample, and nobody has blessed anything."

[SHOT 5: he taps the stamp on the table, doesn't stamp.]
VO: "I'm telling you this while using the phrase myself, which is the point. Our storage provider is FDA-registered. It also holds ISO 20387 and CAP accreditation, which are the ones that actually involve somebody inspecting how the place is run. Those are the credentials I'd want you to check. Not the stamp."

[SHOT 6: to camera.]
VO: "When any company leans hard on 'FDA-registered' and quietly on everything else, that ordering is telling you something. Ninety-nine a year, and the accreditations are somebody else's to earn, not ours to borrow."

SOUND NOTE: the stamp hitting paper in SHOT 1 is the hook and must be loud and clean. Nothing else. No music.
**Platform cuts:** TikTok full 65s; Reels same; Shorts open on SHOT 2 (the four-word answer). 7-second teaser: SHOT 1 stamp + "It means a facility told the FDA it exists."

**CAPTIONS:**
- TikTok v1: "FDA-registered means a facility told the FDA it exists. That's it."
- TikTok v2: "Registration is for establishments. Approval is for products."
- Reels v1: "We use this phrase too, so here's what it actually means."
- Reels v2: "The credential everyone leans on is the weakest one."
- Shorts v1: "Registered is not approved."
- Shorts v2: "Nobody at the FDA has looked at your sample."
- Hashtags: #consumer #fda #duediligence #trust (trim per platform)

**Footnotes:** FDA's position that assignment of a registration number means only that the facility is registered and **does not convey FDA approval or endorsement of the facility or its products**; FDA does not approve establishments. Registration applies to establishments; approval, clearance or authorisation applies to products. Full sourcing in today's blog article, `biobank-accreditation-explained`.
**RAILS (hard):** (a) **name no company** — "three companies I looked at" stays generic, and the script alleges nothing about anyone's conduct, only explains a term; (b) **the accreditations are GenVault's, not MySpawn's**, and SHOT 5 says so — that line is non-negotiable; (c) not legal advice; (d) do not imply FDA registration is worthless — SHOT 4 exists to keep it accurate.
