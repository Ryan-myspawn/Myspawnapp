## EVERGREEN SCRIPT — "The Saved Voicemail" (~70s)

**Freshness check:** audio-led format never used (last 7 days: phone-screen UI, tape measure, desk cards, counter sweep, retail aisle, printed-page markup). Distinct from The Group Chat Archaeologist (Aug 29) — that was a screen-scroll of text; here the camera never shows a screen and the subject is sound.
**Primary audience: Men 25-55.**
**Trend-hook used:** none needed (evergreen).

[SHOT 1: phone face-down on a table, speaker up. A voicemail begins playing. Camera holds on the man's face, not the phone. Let four seconds run, then a thumb pauses it.]
VO: "That's a voicemail from 2019. It's eleven seconds long and it's about a dentist appointment."

[SHOT 2: still on his face, flat.]
VO: "It is also the only recording of that voice that exists anywhere in the world."

[SHOT 3: hands, counting on fingers on the table.]
VO: "Do the audit on your own phone. Whatever audio you have of your parents is almost certainly accidental. A voicemail you never deleted. Background noise in a birthday video. Somebody laughing off-camera in a clip that was about something else."
ON-SCREEN: "accidental archive"

[SHOT 4: back to camera, dry.]
VO: "Which is strange, because we record everything else on purpose. Every meal. Every trip. Every set at the gym. The one thing nobody has ever deliberately recorded is the voice they'd most want to hear again."

[SHOT 5: pick up the phone, hold it like a phone, not like a prop.]
VO: "So the fix is embarrassingly simple. Call them. Say you want to record it. Ask three questions you don't know the answer to. Ten minutes, and it stops being accidental."

[SHOT 6: set the phone down, thumb unpauses. Let the old voicemail run to the end and cut on the beep.]
VO (over the last seconds): "Then put it somewhere with a name on it, and a date if you want one. Ninety-nine a year does that. But honestly, the recording is the part that matters, and that part is free."

SOUND NOTE: the voicemail audio is the sound design; no music at all. The beep is the cut.
**Platform cuts:** TikTok full 70s; Reels same; Shorts open on SHOT 2 (the only-recording line). 7-second teaser: SHOT 1's four seconds + "It's about a dentist appointment. It's also the only recording that exists."

**CAPTIONS:**
- TikTok v1: "Eleven seconds about a dentist appointment. The only recording of that voice."
- TikTok v2: "Check your saved voicemails. Comment the year of the oldest one."
- Reels v1: "Your audio archive of the people you love is entirely accidental."
- Reels v2: "We record every meal. Nobody records the voice."
- Shorts v1: "The accidental archive."
- Shorts v2: "Ten minutes and it stops being an accident."
- Hashtags: #family #memory #voicenote #legacy #parents (trim per platform)

**Footnotes:** no external claims. **PRODUCTION RULES (hard):** (a) use only your own family's audio, and only with explicit permission from the living — never a third party's voice; (b) if the voice belongs to someone who has died, keep the register archival, not grieving; (c) the delivery stays flat, the facts do the work; (d) the free-part-matters line stays in — it is what keeps this from being a pitch.

## X DRAFTS (3)

**X1 (cord blood guidance, low product, routes to the article):**
"Two facts the cord blood brochure leaves out.

The American Academy of Pediatrics does not recommend routine private cord blood banking unless a sibling has a medically indicated use.

ACOG puts the odds a privately banked unit gets used for that child or family at about 1 in 2,700.

And the one that reframes it: a child's own cord blood generally can't treat that child's genetic disease or leukemia, because the stored cells carry the same variant.

Public donation is free. Nobody earns a commission telling you that."
(Reply with sources: AAP policy statement in *Pediatrics*; ACOG Committee Opinion. Full guide on the blog.)

**X2 (the four questions, brand heuristic):**
"Four questions for anyone who wants to store something of yours for fifty years:

1. Who physically holds it, you or an independent accredited facility?
2. What happens to it if you're acquired or you fail?
3. Can I get it back, how fast, and at what cost?
4. Which accreditations, verifiable on the accreditor's own site?

Send all four in one email. A serious vendor answers within a week.

Evasion is the answer."
(No product mention needed; if asked, we answer all four.)

Engineered bacteria, letters that exist in no wild organism, no human application anywhere near.
