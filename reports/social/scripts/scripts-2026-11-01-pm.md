## THREADS POSTS

**THREADS 1.**
I spent a while today trying to answer one simple question, and I want to be honest about how it went.

The question: if you name a child on an account, how old are they when the money is actually theirs?

I expected a number. What I got was a default of 21, a range across the states of 18 to 25, and two published state-by-state tables from outlets that do this professionally which do not agree with each other on some states.

I am not going to tell you which table is right, because I do not know. The useful finding is that the table is not the authority. The statute is, in your state, as enacted today.

That is a less satisfying answer than a number. It is also the only one I can stand behind.

**THREADS 2.**
There is a sentence I keep seeing and it is doing a lot of quiet work: "the new rule is 25."

In September and October this year the American Bar Association's Probate and Property reported a revised uniform act that would set the age at 25. A uniform act is a model law. It is a draft handed to legislatures. Until a legislature enacts it, it governs nothing.

So "a group proposed 25" is true and "the rule is now 25" is not, and in a headline those two look the same.

We are a storage company, ninety-nine dollars a year, billed annually, and the same test applies to us. When we tell you something is required, ask which instrument requires it.
