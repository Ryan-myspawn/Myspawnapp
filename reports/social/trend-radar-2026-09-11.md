### Direction 1 — Get ahead of "digital necromancy"
The critique now has a name and a moral charge attached: commentators accuse griefbot developers of **profiting from digital necromancy**, and the scholarly worry is specific — the dead cannot consent, the data is theirs, and living alongside a simulation may prolong grief rather than resolve it (multiple 2026 journal treatments; *ScienceDirect*, *Memory, Mind & Media*, *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences*).
**Why this matters to us:** when a category gets a pejorative, adjacent categories get swept in by lazy coverage. "DNA storage" sits one careless sentence away from "digital immortality startup."
**The move:** publish our boundary *before* anyone asks. We already have the line (records, not resurrection) and the ad (116). What we do not have is a piece that engages the critique on its own terms, concedes what is right about it, and states plainly which parts of it apply to us. ### Direction 2 — The unlock day, not the sealing day
Every Memory Vault asset we have made is about the moment of **sealing** a video. Nobody has covered the moment it **opens** — what it is like to be eighteen and receive three minutes of your father from when he was thirty-four, and why the date matters more than the message.
**Why it is fresh:** it is the same product surface from the opposite end of the timeline, it is inherently VIDEO-message framed (binding-rules compliant), and it gives the 40-60 lane a genuine emotional payload without sentimentality — the story is the mechanism, not the tears.
**Risk flag:** must be written as a mechanism explainer, not a fantasy; we have never seen an unlock (the product is too young), and pretending otherwise would be fabrication. Frame it as design reasoning: why we chose a fixed date, what happens on the day, what we deliberately do not do.
