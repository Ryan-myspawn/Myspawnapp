## The recommendation has changed, and this is the substance of this week's report
For five weeks these reports have ended by proposing unverified keywords. **On 17 September I recorded a rule against stacking a fifth week of guesses, and I am not going to break it in week six by writing a sixth.**

**What I am proposing instead: until units return, drive the SEO queue from a documented editorial method rather than from invented volume figures.** We have a better signal than guessing, and this week demonstrated it three times.

**The method, in one line: write the article that answers the question our own research kept having to ask.**

Evidence from the last seven days, all of it real:
- **`platform-legacy-settings-explained` (20 Sep)** came from a competitor sweep, not a keyword list. It answers *"what happens to my accounts when I die"*, a question with obviously real and perennial demand, and it **corrected our own framing in the process**. No volume figure was needed to know it was worth writing.
- **`how-long-labs-keep-dna-sample` (16 Sep)** came from the literal sentence behind a competitor finding.
- **`cellular-reprogramming-explained` (18 Sep)** and **`plasmalogen-supplement-evidence` (19 Sep)** both existed because a claim we were about to repeat turned out to be wrong.

**Four of the week's six articles were generated by research friction rather than by the rank tracker.** That is not an argument against the tracker, which we should restore. It is an argument that **the queue does not need to stall while we wait**, and that labelling editorial picks honestly is better than dressing them as data.
