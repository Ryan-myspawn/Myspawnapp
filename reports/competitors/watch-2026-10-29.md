# MySpawn Competitor Watch: 2026-10-29

**NOT A QUIET DAY. One material finding, and its most important feature is the state of its sourcing rather than the claim itself.**

## FINDING: HEREAFTER AI IS REPORTED TO HAVE SHUT DOWN. EVERY SOURCE IS ONE COMPETITOR'S WEBSITE.

**The claim.** HereAfter AI, the voice-interview legacy-tech company, is reported to have ceased operating. A farewell notice is said to have replaced its homepage during 2026; by **18 August 2026** that notice was reportedly gone and `hereafter.ai` returned a page-not-found error; `app.hereafter.ai` is reported to have still loaded afterwards. Account holders were reportedly directed to **support@hereafter.ai** as the only retrieval route, **with no export deadline announced at any point**. The iOS app is reported as still listed and free to download, **last updated September 2023**, while a Google Play listing could not be found. The separate domain `hereafter.app` is reported to redirect to a parking page offering it for sale.

**NOW THE PART THAT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OF THAT.** **Two independent searches returned, between them, one substantive source: `afterlife.ai`.** Every article describing the shutdown, the timeline, the pricing and the app's status is published by **a company that competes in the same category and stands to receive HereAfter's displaced customers.** **No mainstream outlet, no trade publication, no company statement and no regulatory filing appeared in either sweep.**

**That is not a reason to dismiss it.** A competitor can report something true, and the specificity here (a dated notice, a named support address, an app last-updated date) is the kind of detail a fabrication usually lacks. **It is a reason to label it precisely, and to refuse to use it until somebody without a commercial interest confirms it.**

**OUR OWN PROBE COULD NOT TEST IT, AND THE REASON IS OURS RATHER THAN THEIRS.** A direct request to `hereafter.ai`, `www.hereafter.ai` and `app.hereafter.ai` returned **`CONNECT tunnel failed, response 403` on all three**. **That is this session's proxy refusing the host, not the host refusing us.** **It is the identical signature corrected in the founder brief on 26 October, when a bare status code was first misread as evidence about a domain.** **So: we cannot confirm or refute the 404 from here, and nothing in this file should be read as having checked it.**

**Epistemic label, stated once and binding on any downstream use: REPORTED, SINGLE-SOURCE, INTERESTED PARTY, UNCONFIRMED.**

## SO WHAT FOR MYSPAWN

**Threat level: none. Strategic significance: high, and uncomfortable.**

This is, if true, the exact scenario our custody argument is built on, and we have a published article about it: [what happens when a DNA company shuts down](/blog/what-happens-dna-company-shuts-down). **A legacy company holding irreplaceable recordings of dead people stops operating, and the retrieval route becomes one email address with no deadline.**

**BINDING CONDITION ON USE, WRITTEN NOW SO A LATER RUN CANNOT SOFTEN IT.** **This finding may NOT be used in any ad, script, longpost, newsletter or blog article while its only source is a competitor's website.** **The temptation is obvious and it is exactly the behaviour we criticise in others: a single interested source, a satisfying conclusion, and no independent confirmation.** **If an outlet, a filing or a company statement confirms it, the condition lifts and this becomes a legitimate teaching example.** **Until then it stays in this file.**

**What we may act on immediately, because it requires no claim about anybody: our own practice.** If a company of ours ever stops, the honest thing is a published export route with a deadline, decided before it is needed rather than during. **We do not have one written down. That is a founder decision and it is new today.**
