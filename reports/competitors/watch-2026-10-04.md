## FINDING 2: THE QUESTION NOBODY ASKS A MEMOIR COMPANY, AND WE COULD NOT FIND THEIR ANSWER

**StoryWorth's entire product is a living person's memories, collected weekly, over a year.** The obvious custody question is therefore: **what happens if the storyteller dies partway through?**

**What we could establish, via search index only.** StoryWorth publishes its subscription-end behavior: when a Basic or Color subscription ends, **the customer gets an email offering renewal or printing, and has a few months to edit stories without losing access to the account or the stories.** That is a clear, customer-friendly, published answer **to a different question.**

**What we could NOT find is any published answer to the death question**: who can access the account, who can complete or close the book, and what happens to the partial manuscript.

**What we did find is one customer's account of it happening.** A reviewer describes a grandfather receiving StoryWorth as a gift and dying a few months later, leaving **"a book with a few answers but mostly blank pages"**, and says plainly: **"There should be better options for when people pass away."** ([Keepsake Project review round-up](https://www.keepsakeproject.co/storyworth-reviews), via search index.)

**STATED PRECISELY, BECAUSE THE DIFFERENCE MATTERS AND THIS FILE HAS GOT IT WRONG BEFORE.** **We could not find a published policy. That is NOT the same as there being none.** **StoryWorth's own help center is unreachable from our container**, which is our network refusing the connection and is not evidence about the company. **This is logged as an open question, not as a failing.**

**So what for MySpawn: differentiation proof, and it is the cleanest one this file has produced.** Our positioning is custody transparency, and **the custody question for a memoir service is the one its customers are most likely to meet and least likely to have asked.** The useful move is **not** to point at StoryWorth. It is to make sure **our own answer to the identical question is published, specific and easy to find**, because we have spent this week writing about exactly this for other people's products.

**AND IT CUTS AT US DIRECTLY.** On 24 September this operation's own newsletter used the product as an **admission** that we had no published page answering the export question about ourselves. **This is the same shape: we are now three articles deep into telling readers to demand written answers about account inheritance, cancellation and sample destruction, and the obligation to publish ours is correspondingly larger.** **That is a to-do for the blog queue, not a competitive attack line.**
