## NEWLY DISCOVERED: Remento's actual price and product, and it lands on top of ours

Remento was noted in passing in dive #19 (Sept 11) only as "a StoryWorth competitor notable enough for a Wikipedia entry." That undersold it. The details matter:

- **Price: $99 per year.** Identical to ours. First year includes one hardcover book of up to 200 pages, unlimited collaborators, and a year of weekly prompts. Renews at **$99/year or $12/month**. Additional book copies **$69**; extending a book to 380 pages **+$30**; each additional storyteller **$99 one-time** including a year of access and a book credit. *(Pricing per Remento's own help-centre pricing guide and multiple 2026 review pages; company-published, verify before quoting in an ad.)*
- **The medium is video and voice, not writing.** Weekly prompts arrive by email or text, the storyteller answers by **recording voice or video with no app required**, and the recordings are transcribed and formatted into chapters automatically.
- **The bridge mechanic is the clever part.** The printed book carries **QR codes that link back to the original audio or video**, so a reader can scan and hear the story in the person's own voice.
- **Mainstream validation:** founder Charlie Greene appeared on ABC's **Shark Tank in March 2025** and took a **$300,000** deal with Mark Cuban. Founded 2020 in Los Angeles by Greene and Alex Massonneau.

**So what for MySpawn, honestly:**

1. **Price validation, first.** An independently built, televised, Cuban-backed consumer legacy product converged on **$99/year** without any reference to us. That is evidence our price architecture sits where this category's consumers actually transact, and it is worth telling the founder plainly.
2. **Framing collision, second, and this is the uncomfortable part.** Our binding rules retired letter-to-the-future framing in favour of **video messages**. Remento is already a video-first product at our price in an adjacent lane with national TV awareness. The phrase "record a video for your family, $99 a year" is now contested ground, not open ground.
3. **The differentiation still holds, and it is about time, not medium.** Remento's unlock is **immediate**: you record, and a book arrives this year. The Memory Vault's unlock is a **date you do not control once it is set**, tied to a milestone. And Remento stores stories; we store stories plus the biological record. The line to hold: *they turn your voice into a book you read now, we seal a message for a day you will not be there for, and we keep the sample.*
4. **Do not attack them.** Remento is a good product doing an honest thing, and mocking a Shark Tank consumer favourite would read badly. The correct posture is the StoryWorth posture from dive #6: a comparison article that tells the truth and lets the difference in mechanism do the work.

**Threat level: low-direct, moderate-positional.** No biological storage, no custody claim, no milestone lock. But they own more consumer attention at our exact price than we do, and their category vocabulary overlaps ours more than StoryWorth's did.

**Action queued (not taken):** add "Remento alternative" and "Remento vs Memory Vault" to the SEO comparison queue alongside the StoryWorth page already queued in dive #6. That is a founder call on whether we want to compete for that intent at all.

## DEEP DIVE #20: BENEFITS BROKERS (channel dive, second in the channel rotation)

The standing instruction from dive #14 was that channel dives replace company dives on quiet days, and that benefits brokers were next after estate attorneys. This is that dive. Two competitors already occupy this channel: **Legacy** (via Progyny, Carrot, Maven, logged dive #4) and **Empathy** (via Fortune 500 employers and life insurers, reaching a reported 40M+ people, logged dive #7). Both are free-to-the-employee and paid for by an employer or carrier.

**What the channel looks like in 2026**

- **Voluntary benefits are the growth category.** Employers want to widen choice without adding cost, so they add employee-paid options: legal services plans, identity-theft protection, supplemental life, accident and hospital indemnity cover. These are the products that look most like ours in shape: cheap, annual, peace-of-mind purchases with no claims complexity.
- **Family-building is expanding fast.** A reported **63% of employers** plan to increase family-building benefits, which already include fertility treatment discounts, fertility preservation, adoption assistance and surrogacy support. That is the door Legacy walked through.
- **Longevity and end-of-life planning have a demographic argument attached:** roughly **70% of Americans turning 65** will need some form of long-term care, which is the statistic brokers use to open legacy and long-term-care conversations.
- **Brokers are the gatekeepers,** not the employers. They design the package and run enrollment. Getting to an employee means convincing a broker first.
- **The rails are named and few.** Broker-friendly benefits administration platforms include **Employee Navigator, PlanSource and Selerix**. A voluntary benefit that is not on a platform effectively does not exist at enrollment time.

*(Figures above come from benefits-industry blogs and a broker survey summary rather than primary filings. Treat the 63% and 70% as directional; date and re-source before either appears in a pitch deck.)*

**Tactic worth stealing:** the **enrollment-window calendar**. Voluntary benefits sell in a narrow annual window, and the vendors that win are the ones whose materials are in the broker's hands months before open enrollment. That is a content and outreach rhythm we could copy even outside the channel: our own "seal it before the birthday" moments are calendar-driven in exactly the same way, and we currently market as though every day is equivalent.

**Honest assessment, because the point of a channel dive is to decide, not to admire:**

This channel is **not available to MySpawn in 2026**, and I would rather write that than list it as an opportunity for the fourth time. A broker putting a product in front of employees is underwriting a reputational risk, and the diligence questions are the ones we cannot yet answer well: how many members do you have, who holds the samples if you fail, what is the carrier relationship, and who administers it. With fewer than 1,000 founding members and a third-party storage dependency, we would not survive that conversation, and a failed broker pitch is remembered.

**What would make it available,** in order: (1) member count in the thousands; (2) a written, published continuity plan for what happens to samples if MySpawn ceases to operate, which is the single highest-value document we do not have and which doubles as consumer marketing; (3) a platform listing on one of the three named rails. Item (2) is worth doing regardless of the channel, and it is the recommendation from this dive.
