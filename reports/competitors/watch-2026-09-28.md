## FINDING 1: yesterday's "third pricing model" framing was too clean, and this is the correction

**Yesterday this file recorded that a cord-blood bank sells storage as fixed 30-year and lifetime prepaid plans, and called prepaid term "a third pricing model our file did not contain", set against annual and one-time-and-you-keep-it.**

**Today's dive shows the same company also charges annual storage fees, reported in the range of $175 to $250.** ([Americord pricing pages, via search index](https://www.americordblood.com/pricing).)

**So the taxonomy is not three clean models held by three kinds of company. One company offers both**, and the customer chooses which risk to carry. **That is a more accurate and less tidy picture, and the tidiness is what made yesterday's version wrong.**

**What survives the correction, and it is the part that mattered:** **a prepaid term still converts renewal risk into counterparty risk paid up front**, and the question of what happens in year thirty-one is still unanswered by most of the category. **The argument holds. The taxonomy does not.**

**SO WHAT FOR MYSPAWN:** if we ever build the comparison asset, **it compares the two ways to pay rather than three kinds of company.** Getting that wrong in public would be the same class of error as the currency mistake corrected on Saturday: a clean story imposed on messier facts.

**THE INTERESTING FACT IS NOT A PRICE. IT IS THE DEFINITION.** **Defining "lifetime" as 78 years is the answer to "what happens in year thirty-one", written down.** Most of this category uses "lifetime" as a mood. **Putting an integer on it is more honest than not**, and it deserves to be said plainly even though it belongs to a competitor.

**SO WHAT: differentiation proof, and a standard to hold ourselves to.** **Our own equivalent question is what happens if MySpawn ceases to operate, and our published answer is still PARTIAL** because the continuity document is drafted and unpublished. **A competitor has now put a number on its longest promise while ours is still a draft.**
