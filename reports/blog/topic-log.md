- 2026-09-22: **THE FIND THAT MADE THE ARTICLE WORTH WRITING: the archive is split across two legal regimes and the standard tool only reaches one of them.**
  **Tangible half:** most US states have adopted **Uniform Probate Code s. 2-513**, "separate writing identifying devise of certain types of tangible personal property" (verified in state code: **Hawaii 560:2-513, Massachusetts 190B 2-513, New Mexico 45-2-513, Idaho 15-2-513**). A will may point to a signed separate list; it may be written **before or after the will**, may be **altered afterwards**, must describe items and recipients **with reasonable certainty**, **cannot dispose of money**, and cannot cover anything the will already gives specifically. **No state count is claimed in the article, because no verified count was available.**
  **Digital half:** **RUFADAA**, adopted in nearly every state, governs **the data, not the device**. A phone is tangible property; the photographs on it and in its cloud account are digital assets under a separate regime, and **the default presumption is that a fiduciary does NOT have access unless the will or trust says so explicitly.**
  **So a personal property memorandum reading "my photographs to my daughter" reaches the shoebox and not the cloud account.** That is the article's spine and it is the kind of thing a reader can act on the same day.
