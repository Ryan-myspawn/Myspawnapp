# The 23andMe Settlement: What $150 Million Actually Paid Out

**Freshness check:** This angle has not been used in the last 7 days, and no article in the archive covers the settlements. `23andme-alternative` covers what to do next; `what-happens-dna-company-shuts-down` covers the custody lesson; `genetic-privacy-laws` mentions the settlement in one line. **None of them answers what anyone actually received.**
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55.
**Trend / hook used:** Both settlements reached their conclusions in July 2026, and the public numbers have not settled down since: the same event is being reported as $30 million, $46.75 million, $50 million and $150 million.

**Short answer: the consumer claim deadline was 17 February 2026 and it has passed. Individual payouts run from roughly $50 to $165, with up to $10,000 for documented extraordinary losses. The headline "$150 million" belongs to a separate settlement with 42 state attorneys general, and it is not $150 million of cash: it is $150 million in allowed claims against a bankruptcy estate, from which the states recover about $18 million. If you are seeing several different figures, they are mostly the same settlement described at different points in its life.**

## Why you are seeing three different numbers for one settlement

This is the most common confusion and it is worth clearing first, because the numbers are not contradicting each other.

The consumer class settlement is **$46.75 million** gross. **A US bankruptcy judge approved it on 7 July 2026.** Reporting that puts the figure nearer **$30 million** is describing what remains to be distributed, because a portion was already paid out earlier in the process. Our own records of this case put that earlier payment at about **$14.29 million**, leaving roughly **$32.5 million** to distribute. **Label that one honestly: it comes from our archive of this case rather than from a court document we have re-read today, and it should be checked against the settlement administrator before anyone relies on it.**

The **$150 million** is a different settlement entirely, and the next section explains why it is not what it sounds like.

**The general lesson is worth more than the arithmetic.** A large legal settlement is not one number. It is a gross figure, a net figure, an administration cost, a distribution schedule and a recovery rate, and **journalism usually reports whichever one was in the press release it received.** If you want to know what a settlement did, the question is never "how big was it" but **"how much reached the people it was for."**

## The two settlements are not the same thing

### The consumer class settlement

**Who is covered:** any US resident who was a 23andMe customer between **1 May 2023 and 2 October 2023**.

**What happened:** in October 2023, attackers obtained data linked to roughly **6.9 million** 23andMe customers, including ancestry information and relative networks.

**What the payouts look like:** most claims land between roughly **$50 and $165**. Customers who were notified that **health information** was affected are at the **$165** end. Documented extraordinary losses go up to **$10,000**, and "documented" is doing real work in that sentence.

**The deadline was 17 February 2026 and it has passed.** If you did not file by then, this settlement is closed to you.

### The 42-state settlement

On **14 July 2026**, a coalition of **42 state attorneys general**, led by Connecticut's William Tong, announced a settlement with the **bankruptcy trustee** for 23andMe over the same breach.

**The headline figure is $150 million in allowed claims.** The recovery is **about $18 million.**

That is not a discount anyone negotiated. It is what happens when the party paying is a bankruptcy estate: **the estate is finite, other creditors are in the queue, and an allowed claim is a place in that queue rather than a check.** The $18 million is reported as payable immediately out of available funds.

**So the ratio worth remembering is roughly twelve cents on the dollar.** Not because anyone behaved badly, but because that is the arithmetic of being owed money by a company that has already failed.

## What a settlement means when the payer is already bankrupt

This is the part that matters beyond this one company, and it is the reason we wrote this page rather than a news summary.

23andMe filed for Chapter 11 in **March 2025**. Its business and its customer data were sold through a court-approved process to the **TTAM Research Institute**, a non-profit formed by co-founder Anne Wojcicki, in a sale completed in **July 2025**.

By the time the breach claims were resolved in July 2026, the entity that caused the harm no longer existed in the form that caused it. **The claims were against an estate, not a business.**

Three consequences follow, and all three are general rather than specific to this case.

**First, the remedy arrives years later.** The breach was October 2023. The consumer deadline was February 2026. The settlements were approved in July 2026.

**Second, the remedy is proportional to what is left, not to what was lost.** A $150 million claim against a solvent company is a $150 million problem for that company. The same claim against an estate is a $18 million distribution.

**Third, and this is the one people find hardest: the money was never the thing you wanted.** Nobody who used a genetic testing service in 2023 was hoping to receive $165 in 2026. **The compensation is real and it is not the same as the data being un-leaked**, which is not a thing that can happen.

That is the actual custody lesson, and we have written about the mechanics of it in [what happens when a DNA company shuts down](https://myspawn.me/blog/what-happens-dna-company-shuts-down) and about what a sale does to your agreement in [data ownership after an acquisition](https://myspawn.me/blog/data-ownership-after-acquisition).

## Can you still claim anything?

**Not from the consumer class settlement.** The deadline of 17 February 2026 has passed and the settlement was approved on that basis.

**The state settlement is not something individuals claim.** It resolves the attorneys general's own claims against the estate. Funds recovered by a state are handled under that state's law and are not a per-resident payment you apply for.

**If you are a former customer and want to do something today, the useful actions are not financial.** Check whether your account still exists and what it still holds. Check whether you ever requested deletion and whether you received confirmation. And if you live somewhere with a genetic privacy statute, find out what it actually gives you: several US states have passed one in the last two years, and they differ substantially. We keep a plain-language summary in [genetic privacy laws in 2026](https://myspawn.me/blog/genetic-privacy-laws).

## FAQ

### How much is the 23andMe settlement payout per person?
Most claims land between roughly **$50 and $165**, with **$165** for customers notified that health information was affected, and up to **$10,000** for documented extraordinary losses. **The claim deadline of 17 February 2026 has passed.**

### Is the 23andMe settlement $30 million, $46.75 million or $150 million?
All three figures appear in reporting and they describe different things. **The consumer class settlement is $46.75 million gross**, with a smaller net figure remaining to distribute after an earlier payment. **The $150 million is a separate settlement with 42 state attorneys general, and it is $150 million in allowed claims against a bankruptcy estate, not cash.** The states' actual recovery is **about $18 million**.

### Why did the states only recover $18 million out of $150 million?
Because the payer is a bankruptcy estate. **An allowed claim is a position in a queue of creditors, not a check**, and the estate is finite. Roughly twelve cents on the dollar is what that queue produced here.

### Can I still file a claim?
**No.** The consumer deadline was 17 February 2026. The state settlement is not a per-person claim process; it resolves the attorneys general's claims and is handled under each state's own law.

### Who owns 23andMe's data now?
The business and customer data were sold through the bankruptcy to the **TTAM Research Institute**, a non-profit formed by co-founder Anne Wojcicki, in a sale completed in **July 2025**.

### Does a settlement mean my genetic data is safe now?
No, and the distinction is the point. **A settlement compensates for a breach; it does not reverse one.** Data that has been taken stays taken. The practical questions that remain are about custody going forward: who holds what, under which agreement, and what happens if that company stops operating.
