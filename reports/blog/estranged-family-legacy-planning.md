# Legacy Planning When You Are Estranged From Your Family

If you are not speaking to a child, a parent or a sibling, the most important thing to understand is this: **in most of the United States, saying nothing does not disinherit anyone.** Intestacy law does not ask whether you were close. An estranged child typically inherits exactly as a child you spoke to every week would. **Disinheriting someone requires explicit language in a valid will; silence achieves the opposite of what most people assume it achieves.** Everything else in this article follows from that one fact.

## The number everybody quotes is the wrong number

Estrangement is more common than the people living it tend to believe, and the statistic you have probably seen is being misused.

The first large-scale national survey of family estrangement in the United States was run by **Cornell sociologist Karl Pillemer** and published in his **2020 book *Fault Lines: Fractured Families and How to Mend Them*** *(book-length research reporting on an original national survey; we did not locate a newer large-scale US prevalence measure in this sweep, so treat the figures as the best available rather than as current-year data)*.

**Pillemer found that 27 percent of Americans aged 18 and older had cut off contact with a family member.** That is roughly 67 million people.

**The 27 percent is routinely misquoted as the share of adults estranged from a parent. It is not.** It covers **any** relative. **The parent-or-child figure is about 10 percent**, or roughly 25 million Americans.

We are flagging that because the inflated version gets used to sell things, and because the real number is quite large enough. **One adult in ten is estranged from a parent or a child.** That is not a fringe situation requiring a special kind of plan. It is a normal planning input that most planning content pretends does not exist.

## Silence is not a decision

Here is the mechanism that catches people.

**If you die without a will, your estate is distributed by your state's intestacy statute.** Those statutes allocate by relationship category. **They do not contain a clause about whether the relationship was good.** An estranged child in the children class inherits with the other children in that class.

**To exclude someone, your will generally has to say so, by name and explicitly.** Lawyers draft it that way for a reason: a court reading an ambiguous document will usually fall back toward the default, and the default includes the person you have not spoken to in nine years.

**This is state law and it varies, which is the one thing never to forget.** Louisiana is the clearest illustration, because it is the only US state with a civil-law system and it handles this differently from everywhere else: under Louisiana's **Civil Code article 1618**, a disinherison must be made in one of the forms prescribed for testaments, **and current Louisiana law provides no authority to disinherit a child without a will at all.**

**And Louisiana is actively revisiting it right now.** The Legislature has authorized and directed the **Louisiana State Law Institute** to study intestate succession and **mechanisms to prohibit an intestate successor from inheriting when estranged**, with **findings due no later than 4 February 2027** *(legislative instrument; a study directive, not a change in law. Nothing has changed yet and nobody should plan as though it has)*.

**The practical takeaway does not depend on how that study lands: a will that names people explicitly does the work. An absence of a will hands the decision to a statute written for the average family.**

## The three things that move regardless of the relationship

Estrangement changes who you want things to reach. It does not change the machinery, and the machinery has three layers that behave differently.

**Layer one: accounts, which follow their own rules.** Beneficiary designations on retirement accounts and life insurance typically override your will. **Platform legacy settings are separate again, and they are not consistent between companies**, which we covered in [what a legacy contact can and cannot do](/blog/platform-legacy-settings-explained) and [who inherits your DNA testing account](/blog/who-inherits-your-dna-testing-account). A legacy contact named in 2019 and forgotten is a live instruction.

**Layer two: documents, which fail at the custody step rather than the drafting step.** A will that nobody can reach is not operative. We went through where that actually breaks in [who keeps the family archive](/blog/who-keeps-the-family-archive).

## What a record can and cannot do, stated against our own interest

This is the part the category gets wrong, so we are going to be blunt about it.

**A preserved sample does not repair an estrangement. A recorded message is not a reconciliation. Neither is a letter that arrives after you are gone, and anyone selling you one as though it were is selling you something else.**

**What a record actually does is narrower and more durable: it removes a question from the future.** A grandchild you have never met, asking in 2070 what ran in the family, gets an answer that does not require anyone to have forgiven anyone. **A biological record is decision-neutral. ## A practical sequence

**One. Write or update the will, naming people explicitly.** Including, if that is your intention, the person you are excluding. Silence does not exclude.

**Two. Pull the beneficiary designations.** Retirement accounts, life insurance, transfer-on-death registrations. These usually beat the will and they are frequently a decade out of date.

**Three. Audit the platform defaults.** Named legacy contacts, recovery contacts, delegated access. Check [what a legacy contact can and cannot do](/blog/platform-legacy-settings-explained), because the answer differs by company and most people have never read the one they agreed to.

**Four. Name a custodian who is not the problem.** The most common failure in an estranged family is appointing an executor who is inside the dispute. A professional fiduciary costs money and ends arguments.

**Five. Deal with the biological record separately, because it is a separate layer.** Who may request it, under what proof, and who is told.

**Six. Write down where everything is, and tell one living person.** This is the step nobody does and the one that decides whether any of the first five work.

## FAQ

### Does an estranged child inherit if there is no will?
In most US states, yes. Intestacy statutes allocate by relationship category and do not assess the quality of the relationship. To exclude someone you generally need a valid will that says so explicitly. Succession is state law and varies, so confirm your own state.

### How common is family estrangement?
Cornell sociologist Karl Pillemer's national survey, published in 2020, found 27 percent of US adults had cut off contact with a relative, and about 10 percent were estranged from a parent or child specifically. The 27 percent figure is frequently misquoted as the parent-child number; it is not.

### Can I disinherit someone just by leaving them out of my will?
Often not safely. Many attorneys name the excluded person explicitly, because an ambiguous document invites a court to fall back toward the statutory default. In Louisiana, which is the only US civil-law state, disinherison has its own formal requirements under Civil Code article 1618.

### Is any state changing the rules for estranged heirs?
Louisiana has directed the Louisiana State Law Institute to study mechanisms to prohibit an estranged intestate successor from inheriting, with findings due no later than 4 February 2027. That is a study directive, not a change in law. Nothing has changed yet.

### Does preserving a DNA sample pass through my will?
Not usually. A preserved sample is a physical item held under a custody agreement with the facility, so access is governed by that agreement rather than by intestacy. That is why it needs its own instruction rather than a line in a will.

### Should I leave a message for someone I am estranged from?
That is not a question we are qualified to answer for you, and we are not going to pretend a product settles it. What we will say is practical: a recording is easier to make now than later, it can be withheld, and it does not obligate anybody to watch it.
