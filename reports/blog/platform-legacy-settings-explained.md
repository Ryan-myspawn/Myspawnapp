# What Happens to Your Accounts When You Die: The Four Settings, and What Each One Cannot Do

**The short answer: four major platforms now let you decide in advance what happens to your account. Three of them hand your chosen person custody of a profile and nothing inside it. Google is the exception and can actually deliver your data. All four are free, all four take minutes, and almost nobody sets them up.** Below is what each one does, precisely, and the specific limit on each.

## Apple Digital Legacy

**What it is.** You nominate a Legacy Contact who can request access to your Apple Account data after your death.

**How to set it.** On iPhone or iPad: Settings, tap your name, **Password & Security**, then **Legacy Contact**, then **Add Legacy Contact**. On Mac: Apple menu, System Settings, Apple ID, **Password & Security**, **Legacy Contact**. You need **iOS 15.2, iPadOS 15.2 or macOS Monterey 12.1 or later**, and **two-factor authentication must be on**.

**The part people miss.** Your Legacy Contact needs **two** things to get in: an **access key** and a **copy of your death certificate**. The access key is generated when you add them. It exists as a **QR code and a long alphanumeric string**.

If you notify them through iMessage and they accept, their device **stores a copy of the access key automatically**. If you do not, you need to get it to them another way: print it, send a PDF or screenshot, or **file it with your estate documents**, which is the option most estate lawyers would pick.

**The limit:** an access key with no death certificate does nothing, and a death certificate with no access key does nothing. **Set it and then make sure the key is somewhere findable.** A key that only exists on your own phone is a key nobody has.

## Google Inactive Account Manager

**This is the odd one out, and it is the most capable of the four.**

**What it is.** A timer. You choose how long Google should wait with no activity from you before it acts: options run from **3 months to 18 months**.

**What happens when it fires.** Google tries to reach you first, by **recovery email and a text to your recovery phone**, so a false alarm can be stopped before anything is shared. If you do not respond, it **emails up to ten trusted contacts** a message you wrote in advance.

**And here is the bit that makes it different from the other three.** Contacts you granted data access to receive **a download link for exactly the products you ticked** for them: Gmail, Drive, Photos, YouTube, or any subset. They get roughly **three months** to collect it. You can also have Gmail start sending an auto-reply you wrote, and you can have **the whole account deleted three months after it is declared inactive**.

**The limits, and there are three.** It fires on **inactivity, not on death**, so a long hospital stay or a deliberate break can trigger it. The download window **expires**. And the deletion option is exactly as final as it sounds.

**It is also the only one of the four that will actually hand somebody your files.** That is worth knowing before you assume the others do the same. They do not.

## Meta Legacy Contact

**What it is.** Facebook has had this since **February 2015**, the oldest of the four. You name a Legacy Contact, and once the account is memorialized they get a specific, short list of powers.

**What they can do:** write a **pinned post** at the top of the memorialized profile, for example to announce a service; **respond to new friend requests**; **view posts even ones you had set to Only Me**; decide who can see and post tributes; and delete tribute posts. If you separately grant it, they can **download an archive** of the photos, posts and profile information you shared.

**What they cannot do:** they **cannot log in as you**, and they **cannot see your private messages**. Ever, with any paperwork.

**The limit:** the archive permission is a separate toggle. If you do not grant it, your Legacy Contact gets a profile to tidy and nothing to keep.

## TikTok Legacy Account

**The newest.** TikTok added a Legacy Account setting in **late August 2026**, first reported on **24 August** from screenshots of the setting inside account settings, then covered by trade press including MediaPost on 26 August. *(Multi-outlet trade reporting of a shipped in-app setting, not a TikTok press release. No country-by-country rollout list has been published, so availability varies by region and app version.)*

**What it offers:** nominate another user to look after the account, or choose **"Delete after death."** Naming somebody is **optional**.

**What the person you name can do:** very little, and the restriction list is unusually blunt. They **cannot log in. They cannot remove or edit your posts. They cannot read your messages, or your comments on other people's posts. They cannot remove your friends.**

**The limit is the entire feature.** This is custody of a profile with no access to anything inside it.

## The correction we owed you

We have spent the past week telling people that **not one of these settings lets your person open anything.** That is true of Apple in practice, true of Meta unless you tick the archive box, and true of TikTok absolutely.

**It is wrong about Google.** Inactive Account Manager can send a named contact a working download link for your Gmail, Drive, Photos and YouTube data. We got that wrong in our own material and we are fixing it here first.

**The accurate version: three of the four are about custody of an account. One of them, Google's, is a genuine data handover, and it is also the only one that triggers on inactivity rather than death and the only one that can delete the account for you.**

## What none of them do

Even Google's, which is the most generous, is **an export**. A folder of everything, delivered to somebody at an unpredictable moment, with a clock on it.

That is not the same as a message. Nobody's platform setting will hand your eighteen-year-old a video you recorded for their eighteenth birthday, on their eighteenth birthday. The tools are built to resolve an account, which is an administrative problem. Anything you actually want received has to be recorded on purpose, addressed to a named person, and given a date.

## FAQ

**What happens to my Facebook account when I die?**
If somebody reports the death, it is memorialized. Your Legacy Contact can pin a post, respond to friend requests, view posts including ones set to Only Me, and manage tributes. They cannot log in as you and cannot read your private messages. They can download an archive only if you granted that permission separately.

**Can my family get into my Apple account after I die?**
Only if you named a Legacy Contact in advance. That person needs both the access key generated at setup and a copy of your death certificate. Without the key, the death certificate is not enough.

**Does Google delete your account when you die?**
Not for dying, no. Inactive Account Manager acts on **inactivity**, between 3 and 18 months depending on your setting, and it warns you by recovery email and text first. You can optionally have the account deleted three months after it is declared inactive.

**Does TikTok have a legacy setting?**
Yes, added in late August 2026. You can nominate another user or select "Delete after death." Availability varies by region and app version.

**Which platform actually gives my family my data?**
**Google.** It is the only one of the four that can send a named contact a download link for the specific products you chose. The others give custody of a profile rather than its contents.
