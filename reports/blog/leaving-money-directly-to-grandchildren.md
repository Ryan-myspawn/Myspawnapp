**Primary audience:** Men 25-55.
**Trend-hook used:** no 7-to-14-day news hook is claimed. The 2026 figures below are current because the One Big Beautiful Bill Act changed them this year, and that is the honest reason this is worth writing now rather than a trend.

**Short answer: you can leave money or property straight to a grandchild, and US tax law has a dedicated tax built to catch you doing it.** It is the generation-skipping transfer tax, a flat 40 percent, and it bites only above a lifetime exemption that rose to about $15 million per person in 2026. Below that, a bequest to a grandchild is ordinary estate planning. The harder problem is not the tax: a grandchild who inherits at 19 usually gets money with no context attached.

## Nearly half of them did not inherit from a parent

Most inheritance advice assumes wealth moves one step, parent to child. A good deal of it moves two.

In a survey of 600 high-net-worth individuals across Asia-Pacific, Europe and the United States, conducted by Financial Times Longitude in February and March and reported in June 2025, **roughly 47 percent of respondents had inherited directly from their grandparents**, and a majority of those received between $1 million and $25 million ([Funds Society](https://www.fundssociety.com/en/news/markets/high-net-worth-families-around-the-world-are-accelerating-the-transfer-of-wealth-to-their-heirs/), [WealthBriefing](https://www.wealthbriefing.com/html/article.php/hnw-families-accelerate-wealth-transfer%2C-highlight-need-for-guidance--study)).

**Two labels belong on that figure and neither is optional.** The respondents were high-net-worth, so this is not a statement about ordinary families. And the sample spans three continents, so it is not a United States statistic. Treat it as evidence that skip-generation transfer is a common shape among wealthy families, not as a national percentage.

It is still the right reason to read on. A transfer that moves two steps is a different planning object from one that moves one, with a different tax, a different instrument, and a much longer gap between the decision and the money.

## The tax that exists specifically to stop you skipping

Congress built the estate tax on an assumption: wealth gets taxed once per generation. Leaving assets straight to a grandchild skips a turn, so the generation-skipping transfer tax exists to collect what the skipped generation would have paid. Two numbers do most of the work.

- **The rate is a flat 40 percent** on transfers to a skip person, defined generally as someone two or more generations below you ([Q3 Advisors](https://q3adv.com/generation-skipping-transfer-tax/)).
- **The lifetime GST exemption is about $15 million per person for 2026**, roughly $30 million for a married couple, up from $13,990,000 in 2025. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed 4 July 2025, amended the basic exclusion amount in the tax code and the GST exemption tracks it, with inflation adjustments and no scheduled reversion ([Miller Canfield](https://www.millercanfield.com/resources-One-Big-Beautiful-Bill-Estate-Gift-Tax-Exclusion-Generation-Skipping-Transfer-Exemption-Increases-to-15-Million.html), [Thrivent](https://www.thrivent.com/insights/taxes/what-is-the-generation-skipping-tax-exemption)).
- Separately, the **annual gift tax exclusion is $19,000 per recipient for 2026**, unchanged from 2025, per Revenue Procedure 2025-32 issued in October 2025 ([Rehberg Law](https://www.rehbergLaw.com/post/irs-releases-2026-exemption-and-exclusion-amounts)).

At a $15 million exemption, **the GST tax is irrelevant to the overwhelming majority of American families.** If you are leaving a grandchild $20,000 and a box of photographs, nothing in this section applies to you.

## Three ways a transfer gets caught

The GST tax recognises three taxable events, and knowing which one you are triggering is the whole of the planning.

1. **Direct skip.** You give or bequeath something straight to a skip person. A cheque to a grandchild while their parent is alive is the clean example.
2. **Taxable termination.** An interest in a trust ends in a way that leaves only skip persons as beneficiaries. The classic case is a trust paying income to your child for life, with the remainder to your grandchildren: the tax event arrives when your child dies, possibly decades after you set it up.
3. **Taxable distribution.** A trust makes a distribution to a skip person that is not itself a direct skip or a taxable termination.

The second and third kinds arrive **after you are no longer available to be asked anything.** A direct skip is a decision you make and see. A taxable termination is one a trustee administers against facts you never knew.

## The exception that promotes a grandchild

Under the predeceased parent rule, **if the grandchild's parent, your child, is already dead at the relevant time, the grandchild moves up a generation** and the transfer is no longer a skip. Section 2651(e) of the tax code replaced a narrower earlier provision under the 1997 Act and broadened the rule to reach taxable terminations and taxable distributions as well as direct skips, and to cover collateral heirs such as grandnieces and grandnephews.

**The timing is strict and it is where people get hurt.** The test is whether the parent is dead when the transfer is first subject to estate or gift tax, and if there is more than one such time, the earliest. A practitioner summary puts the trap plainly: fund a trust for your child with a remainder to your grandchild, and if your child dies *after* the funding, the exception does not retroactively apply ([Greenleaf Trust](https://greenleaftrust.com/missives/generation-skipping-transfers-avoiding-a-few-traps-along-the-way/)).

Treasury's final regulations in 2005 also closed a side door: **a person who disclaims an inheritance is not treated as a predeceased parent** merely because state law pretends a disclaimant died first ([TD 9214, irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-regs/td_9214.pdf); 2004 proposed regulations, [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-regs/14598803.pdf); practitioner comment, [Tax Notes](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/other-documents/public-comments-on-regulations/trust-and-estate-counsel-comment-on-predeceased-parent-rule/yrlv)).

## The routes that are not a trust

If you are below the exemption, which almost everyone is, skipping a generation is mostly an administrative choice rather than a tax structure.

- **A custodial account** under your state's Uniform Transfers to Minors Act holds assets until the state's age of majority, when the grandchild gets full control, whatever their plans are that year.
- **A 529 education account** is narrower in use and more forgiving in timing, and you can own it while the grandchild is beneficiary.
- **A named bequest in your will** is the simplest route and the slowest, because it waits for probate.
- **A trust** is the only one that lets you attach conditions and timing, which is the only reason to pay for one.

**The honest comparison: three of those four hand over a lump sum on a birthday.** That is the part of skip-generation planning that no exemption amount addresses. ## The gap nobody's tax planning closes

Here is the finding this article started from, turned around. In that same Capital Group survey, **65 percent of the Gen X and millennial inheritors said they had regrets about how they used their inheritance**, with nearly two in five wishing they had invested more of it ([WealthBriefing](https://www.wealthbriefing.com/html/article.php/hnw-families-accelerate-wealth-transfer%2C-highlight-need-for-guidance--study)). Same sample, same two labels: high-net-worth, three continents, not a United States figure.

The money arrived and most of them wished they had done something else with it. **No exemption amount, no trust provision and no custodial account fixes that, because the problem is not the structure of the transfer. It is that the person who decided was two generations away and unavailable for questions.**

That gap is where recorded material does work a trust cannot. A video message released on a specific date, the reason a decision was made, a family medical history written down while the person who lived it is still here: none of it is taxed, and all of it travels two generations as easily as one. ## FAQ

**Can I leave money directly to my grandchildren instead of my children?**
Yes. There is no rule requiring wealth to pass one generation at a time. What exists is the generation-skipping transfer tax, a flat 40 percent on transfers to someone two or more generations below you, which applies only above a lifetime exemption of roughly $15 million per person in 2026.

**How much can I give a grandchild in 2026 without tax consequences?**
The annual gift tax exclusion is $19,000 per recipient for 2026, unchanged from 2025. Gifts at or below that amount do not use any of your lifetime exemption. Confirm the current figure against Revenue Procedure 2025-32 before relying on it.

**What is a direct skip?**
A transfer made straight to a skip person, usually a grandchild, while the generation in between is still alive. It is one of three GST taxable events, alongside a taxable termination and a taxable distribution.

**Does the generation-skipping tax apply if my child has died?**
Usually not. Under the predeceased parent rule in section 2651(e), a grandchild whose parent is already dead at the relevant time moves up a generation and is no longer a skip person. The timing is strict: the parent must be dead when the transfer first becomes subject to estate or gift tax.

**Do I need a generation-skipping trust?**
Only if you need conditions or timing, or you are near the exemption. Below it, a custodial account, a 529 account or a named bequest in a will all move assets to a grandchild more cheaply. A trust is what you pay for when a lump sum on a birthday is the thing you are trying to avoid.

**What should I leave a grandchild besides money?**
The things that answer questions the money raises: a recorded video message, the reasoning behind a decision, a written family medical history, a preserved biological record. None of them are taxed and none of them need a trustee.
