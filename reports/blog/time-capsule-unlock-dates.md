# Choosing Unlock Dates: Why 18 and 21 Are Usually the Wrong Answer

**Freshness check:** This angle has not been used in the last 7 days, and no article in the archive covers unlock-date selection. `video-message-18th-birthday` covers what to record and how; `digital-time-capsule` covers format and storage; neither asks which date, or what happens when the date is wrong.
**Primary audience:** Men 25-55.
**Trend / hook used:** Editorial pick from the standing queue, lane (c). Built on 2026-current US demographic data rather than on a trend, because the premise is empirical: the milestones people pick for unlock dates are less reliable than they were when those milestones became conventional.

**Short answer: pick ages, not events, and pick more than one. Event-based unlocks fail in three specific ways: the event may never happen, it may happen more than once, or it may happen in the worst week of someone's life. Age-based unlocks fail in only one way, which is that the age may be a bad fit, and that is fixable by spreading three or four dates across two decades instead of betting everything on one. Also worth knowing before you choose: "18" is not a single legal date in the United States.**

## "18" is not one date

The most common unlock date people choose is the eighteenth birthday, on the reasonable assumption that this is when a child becomes an adult everywhere. It is not.

**The age of majority is 18 in most US states, 19 in Alabama and Nebraska, and 21 in Mississippi** ([Policygenius state summary](https://www.policygenius.com/estate-planning/age-of-majority-by-state/)). If your unlock is tied to a legal status rather than to a birthday, the date moves depending on where the recipient lives when it arrives, which is not something you can know in advance.

This matters less for a recorded video than for money, but it matters. It is also a useful early signal about the whole exercise: **the milestones that feel universal are usually conventions, and conventions drift.**

## Event-based unlocks fail three ways

A lot of capsule products and a lot of well-meaning plans use events: on her wedding day, when he graduates, when you have your first child. Each of those has a specific failure mode, and none of them is unlikely.

### The event may not arrive

This is the one people underestimate, and it is the best documented.

**A Pew Research Center analysis found that 25 percent of 40-year-olds in the United States had never been married** ([Pew Research Center, published June 2023, using 2021 data](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/06/28/a-record-high-share-of-40-year-olds-in-the-us-have-never-been-married/)). **The figure was 28 percent for men and 22 percent for women, and in 1980 it was 6 percent overall.** Label that honestly: it is a research-center analysis of census data, **it is a 2021 data year published in 2023**, and it is not a forecast.

Graduation is not a safe bet either. **The six-year bachelor's completion rate at four-year institutions sits somewhere around 60 to 64 percent** depending on which cohort is counted ([NCES Fast Facts](https://nces.ed.gov/fastfacts/display.asp?id=40)). The spread between those two numbers is not a contradiction; it reflects different cohorts, full-time versus all students. **But it means a graduation-triggered message has a meaningful chance of never triggering.**

A message that waits for a wedding that does not happen does not fail loudly. **It simply never plays**, and nobody finds out.

### The event may arrive more than once

Second marriages exist. Second degrees exist. A capsule set to open "on your wedding day" has an ambiguity built into it that no software resolves gracefully and that a family may find genuinely painful to adjudicate. **Whoever administers it then has to decide which wedding you meant, and they will be guessing.**

### The event may arrive at the worst possible time

Events cluster with other events. A wedding week is not a week with attention to spare. A graduation is often a move, a job hunt and a financial cliff at once. **A recorded message from someone who has died lands hardest when the recipient has room for it**, and event dates are chosen precisely because they are the days when there is no room.

This is the same structural problem the archive keeps running into from other directions: [who ends up with the family archive](https://myspawn.me/blog/who-keeps-the-family-archive) is usually decided in the worst week of somebody's life, for the same reason.

## What the numbers actually say, with their dates attached

It would be easy to read the marriage figures as "nobody gets married any more" and build a gloomy article on it. That is not what the data shows and it is worth correcting.

**There were 2,082,354 marriages registered in the US in 2024, a rate of 6.3 per 1,000 people, and the highest count since 2019** (CDC/NCHS vital statistics). **Marriage is not disappearing. It is moving later and becoming less universal**, which are different things and only the second one affects your unlock date.

One caution, recorded because it is exactly the kind of number that gets quoted badly: **sources reporting the 2024 median age at first marriage do not agree with each other.** One set of figures gives roughly 30.2 for men and 28.6 for women; another gives 30.8 and 28.8. **Both are described as record highs from Census/ACS data.** We are not picking one. The useful fact is the direction, which is later, and the useful habit is asking which year a number was measured before repeating it.

## The rule that works: a ladder, not a date

The practical answer is unglamorous.

**Pick three or four ages, spread across two decades, and accept that some will land better than others.** A workable default ladder looks like this:

- **One in the late teens**, around the legal-adulthood mark for wherever they actually live. This one is usually about permission and context.
- **One in the mid-twenties.** Far enough out that they are a different person, close enough that you can still picture them.
- **One around thirty.** This is the one most people wish they had, and almost nobody sets.
- **One with no date at all**, released whenever they ask for it.

Then add **two exceptions that are not ages**:

1. **On confirmation of your death**, whenever that is. This is the only event trigger that is guaranteed to fire exactly once.
2. **On request, at any time.** A recipient who wants to hear from you should not have to wait for a birthday.

That last one is worth dwelling on, because it inverts the usual design. **A capsule that can only be opened on a schedule is a capsule that cannot answer a question.** MySpawn's Memory Vault handles milestone unlocks as dates you set rather than events you describe, for the reasons above; the point stands whichever tool you use.

## The part nobody plans for: you will not be there to explain it

Every unlock date carries an implicit message that the recording itself does not contain: **why this date.**

If you chose 25 because that is when you had your first real job, the recipient does not know that unless you say so in the recording. If you chose 30 because your own father died at 32, they definitely do not know that, and it may be the most important thing in the file.

**Say the reason out loud, in the recording, in the first fifteen seconds.** It costs nothing and it is the single most common omission in messages people record for the future. A video that opens with "I picked this birthday because" is doing work that no scheduling system can do for you.

And if you are recording for someone who may not have children, or may not marry, this is the moment to say that too. [Legacy planning without children](https://myspawn.me/blog/childfree-legacy) is not a smaller version of legacy planning; it is a different set of questions, and a capsule that assumes a family tree will read as a reproach to someone who does not have one.

## FAQ

### What is the best age to open a time capsule?
There is no single best age, which is why a ladder beats a date. **Three or four unlocks spread from the late teens to around thirty gives you several chances to land well instead of one.** If you must pick one, the mid-twenties is a better bet than 18: the recipient is settled enough to have room for it and young enough that it still changes something.

### Should I use an event like a wedding instead of an age?
Generally no. **Twenty-five percent of American 40-year-olds have never married** (Pew, 2021 data, published 2023), second weddings create ambiguity about which one you meant, and event weeks are the weeks with the least attention available. **The one event trigger worth using is confirmation of your death**, because it fires exactly once.

### Is 18 the age of majority everywhere in the US?
No. **It is 19 in Alabama and Nebraska and 21 in Mississippi**, and 18 in the rest. If your unlock is tied to legal adulthood rather than to a birthday, the date depends on where the recipient lives at the time.

### What happens to a scheduled video message if the company holding it shuts down?
That is a custody question and it is the right one to ask. Ask any provider what happens to stored material if they cease to operate, who holds it, and whether the answer is published. We have written about [what happens when a DNA company shuts down](https://myspawn.me/blog/what-happens-dna-company-shuts-down) and about [what can actually be exported from an account](https://myspawn.me/blog/what-can-leave-your-account); the same two questions apply to a video file.

### Can I change an unlock date after I set it?
With most tools, yes, while you are alive. **The thing you usually cannot change afterwards is the recording itself**, so if the date moves a long way, it is worth re-recording rather than letting a message intended for an eighteen-year-old arrive to a thirty-year-old.

### How many messages is too many?
Fewer than people expect. **Three or four recordings that each say one thing beat a dozen that circle the same ground.** The constraint is not storage; it is that the recipient has to watch them, and a long queue is a chore rather than a gift.
