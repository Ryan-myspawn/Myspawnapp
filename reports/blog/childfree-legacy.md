# Childfree Legacy: What to Leave Behind When You're Not Leaving Children

*Published: August 22, 2026 · Lane (e) audience angles · myspawn.me/blog*

Roughly one in five American adults will never have children, and most legacy advice simply pretends they don't exist. Here is the direct answer: a childfree legacy is built from four assets you already control: impact (where your money and work land), memory (the record of who you were), your estate's legal machinery (which has no defaults when there are no kids), and, increasingly, your biological record itself. None of these requires descendants. All of them require decisions, and the data says most childfree adults haven't made them yet: only about 20 percent have a will.

## The numbers: childfree is mainstream, and the industry hasn't noticed

The population: about **21 to 23 percent of US adults** are childfree or permanently childless, per nationally representative data confirmed in a 2025 *Journal of Marriage and Family* study ([The Global Statistics summary](https://www.theglobalstatistics.com/childfree-by-choice-statistics-in-us/)). That is not a niche. That is roughly 50 million adults.

A survey released July 28, 2026 puts hard numbers on how differently this group thinks about legacy ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/28/3334329/0/en/New-Survey-Finds-Nearly-6-in-10-Childfree-Adults-Say-Financial-Advice-Focused-on-Building-Future-Wealth-Not-Enjoying-Life-Today.html)):

- **84 percent** would rather spend most of their money during their lifetime than preserve it for heirs.
- Asked where they plan to leave money, **charitable organizations (61 percent)** nearly tied **family members (64 percent)**, upending the core assumption of inheritance planning.
- **31 percent** have received financial advice that assumed they had, or would someday have, children.
- Only **19.9 percent** have a will, and the top reason given was having no obvious person to name as executor.

Read those together and the picture is clear: a fifth of the country is improvising its legacy without a playbook, because the playbook was written for parents.

## First, kill the bad definition

The lazy definition says legacy equals descendants. By that definition, most of history's most remembered people failed at it. Teachers, builders, aunts and uncles, founders, donors, mentors: the ledger of what a person leaves has never been limited to who inherits their chromosomes at dinner.

A better definition, and the one this guide uses: **your legacy is everything that still points back at you after you're gone.** Money, work, institutions, recorded memory, and record of self all qualify. Children are one channel. They were never the only one.

## The four-asset childfree legacy stack

### 1. Impact: direct your money on purpose, not by default

Without a will, state intestacy law decides everything, and it only knows how to find blood relatives. Estate planners describe childfree planning as planning "when there are no defaults" ([Bragg Financial](https://braggfinancial.com/when-there-are-no-defaults-estate-planning-for-childfree-adults/), [Kitces](https://www.kitces.com/blog/client-childfree-trust-planning-lifetime-care-defense-estate-ltc-insurance/)). The upside of no defaults is total freedom: the 61 percent planning charitable gifts can name a scholarship, a research fund, a library, a niece's education. The mechanics are ordinary: a will or living trust, named beneficiaries on every account (these override wills), and if charity is the goal, even a simple beneficiary designation on a retirement account works.

The executor problem, the number-one blocker in the survey, has professional answers: banks, trust companies, and licensed professional fiduciaries take the role for a fee. Not having a nephew you trust is a solvable problem, not a reason to run intestate.

### 2. Memory: the record of who you were

Here is the uncomfortable asymmetry: parents get remembered by default (imperfectly, but somebody's fridge has their photo). A childfree person's memory relies entirely on what gets deliberately recorded. That makes the memory layer more important, not less.

The modern version is not a dusty scrapbook. It is short videos: you explaining a decision you're proud of, telling the story only you know, showing the workshop or the kitchen or the spreadsheet where you were most yourself. Recorded now, sealed to arrive later: a goddaughter's 21st, a nephew's wedding, the anniversary of something that mattered. ### 3. Machinery: the boring documents, done

The checklist is short and the completion rate (20 percent) is embarrassing for a group this financially independent:
- Will or living trust, with a named (possibly professional) executor
- Beneficiary designations reviewed on every account and policy
- Durable financial power of attorney and healthcare directive (childfree adults need these MORE, because no adult child will materialize to handle a crisis; advisors call this the care-planning gap ([Bernstein](https://www.bernstein.com/our-insights/insights/2025/articles/beyond-bloodlines-estate-planning-when-youre-childfree.html)))
- A digital-legacy pass: platform legacy contacts set, passwords escrowed, and local copies of anything that lives only in a cloud account (inactive accounts get deleted; dead people are very inactive)

## FAQ

**What happens to my estate if I die childfree without a will?**
State intestacy law distributes it to your nearest blood relatives in a fixed order (spouse, parents, siblings, then more distant kin). If none are found, the estate escheats to the state. Charities, friends, and partners you weren't married to receive nothing without a will.

**Who can I name as executor if I have no children?**
A sibling, a trusted friend, or a professional: banks, trust companies, and licensed professional fiduciaries serve as executors for a fee. Lacking an obvious person is the most common blocker, and the professional route solves it.

**Is leaving money to charity instead of family normal?**
Increasingly, yes. In a July 2026 survey of childfree adults, 61 percent planned gifts to charitable organizations, statistically neck and neck with the 64 percent naming family members.

**Why would someone without children preserve their DNA?**
As the reference copy of their branch of the family: relatives and their descendants share your lineage, and a preserved sample keeps the family's biological record complete for genealogy, family health history, and questions nobody has thought to ask yet. It is storage of a record, not a path to descendants.

**What legacy documents matter most for childfree adults?**
A will or trust with a named executor, beneficiary designations, durable power of attorney, and a healthcare directive. The last two matter most while you're alive, because no adult child exists to step in during a crisis.
